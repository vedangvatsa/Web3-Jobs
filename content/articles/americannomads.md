For United States citizens and permanent residents (green card holders), location independence comes with a unique legal reality: citizenship-based taxation. 

Unlike citizens of nearly every other nation on earth—who can sever domestic tax obligations simply by moving abroad and spending more than 183 days outside their home country—US citizens remain subject to US federal income tax on their worldwide income no matter where they live, work, or sleep.

However, the US tax code also contains powerful statutory provisions designed to prevent double taxation for expatriates and overseas remote workers. 

By properly utilizing the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE), the Foreign Housing Exclusion, and the Foreign Tax Credit (FTC), American digital nomads earning up to roughly $126,500 (adjusted annually for inflation) can legally reduce their US federal income tax liability to zero dollars.

In addition, severing state-level tax domicile from high-tax states like California or New York can save an additional 8 to 13 percent in state income taxes every single year.

This guide provides a comprehensive tax blueprint for American digital nomads, explaining how the FEIE works, how to satisfy the 330-day Physical Presence Test, how to break state tax residency, and how to maintain total IRS compliance.

## 1. The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE / Form 2555)

The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (IRC Section 911) is the foundational tax tool for American digital nomads. It allows qualifying US citizens working abroad to exclude a substantial amount of foreign earned income from federal income taxation.

### Key FEIE Thresholds and Parameters:
- **Exclusion Limit:** Roughly $126,500 per individual (adjusted annually for inflation). A married couple where both spouses work remotely abroad can exclude over $250,000 in combined salary.
- **Earned Income vs. Passive Income:** The FEIE applies strictly to "earned income" (salaries, wages, professional service fees, independent contractor earnings). It does NOT apply to passive income (capital gains from crypto or stocks, rental property income, dividends, interest, or pension distributions).

## 2. Qualifying for the FEIE: The Physical Presence Test

To claim the FEIE, you must pass one of two statutory IRS tests: the Bona Fide Residence Test or the Physical Presence Test. For digital nomads who move between international destinations, the Physical Presence Test (PPT) is the standard path.

### The Physical Presence Test Rules:
1. **The 330-Day Rule:** You must be physically present in a foreign country or countries for at least 330 full days during any rolling 365-day period (12 consecutive months).
2. **What Counts as a Full Day:** A "full day" is a continuous 24-hour period starting at midnight and ending at the following midnight.
3. **International Transit Does Not Count:** Days spent in international waters or airspace traveling between countries do not count toward your 330 full days. If your flight departs London at 10:00 PM and arrives in Bangkok at 3:00 PM the following afternoon, neither day counts as a full day in a foreign country.
4. **The 35-Day US Visit Allowance:** In any 365-day qualification period, you can spend a maximum of 35 days (roughly 5 weeks) inside the United States for vacations, family holidays, or company meetings. Overstaying this 35-day limit by even one day completely invalidates your FEIE eligibility for that period.

## 3. The Self-Employment Tax Trap for Contractors

Many American freelance software engineers and agency founders celebrate excluding their income under the FEIE, only to be shocked by an IRS Self-Employment Tax bill.

### How Self-Employment Tax Operates:
- The FEIE excludes **federal income tax**, but it does NOT exclude **Self-Employment Tax (SECA)**, which funds Social Security and Medicare at a combined rate of 15.3 percent on net freelance earnings.
- **W-2 Remote Employees:** If you work as a W-2 employee for a US company, your employer withholds Social Security and Medicare taxes automatically. The FEIE eliminates your federal income tax withholding via Form W-4.
- **1099 Contractors and Solo LLCs:** If you operate as a sole proprietorship or single-member LLC, you must pay the 15.3 percent self-employment tax on your net profits up to the annual Social Security wage base, even if your federal income tax is zero.
- **The Foreign Corporation Solution:** Experienced founders often establish an offshore operating entity (such as in Dubai or the British Virgin Islands) that employs them, eliminating US self-employment tax liabilities, though this requires careful compliance with Controlled Foreign Corporation (CFC / Subpart F) and GILTI tax rules.

## 4. Severing State Tax Domicile (The "Sticky State" Problem)

Federal taxes are only half the battle. If your last legal residence was in a "sticky state" like California, New York, Virginia, or South Carolina, state revenue agencies can claim you remain a state tax resident even while you live in Thailand or Spain.

California's Franchise Tax Board (FTB), for example, enforces a strict domicile standard: unless you establish a permanent legal domicile elsewhere, California considers your absence temporary and taxes your worldwide income.

### How to Establish a Tax-Free State Domicile:
Before leaving the US, relocate your legal domicile to a state with zero personal income tax:
1. **The Premier Nomad Domicile States:** **Florida**, **Texas**, **Nevada**, **Wyoming**, or **South Dakota**. South Dakota is particularly popular because it allows travelers to establish legal state residency and obtain a driver's license with just one night spent in a local hotel or RV park.
2. **Steps to Establish Domicile:**
   - Register with an approved legal mail forwarding service (such as in Sioux Falls, SD or Jacksonville, FL).
   - Transfer your driver's license and vehicle registration to your new state.
   - Register to vote in your new state.
   - Update your bank accounts, credit cards, and brokerages to reflect your new address.
   - Close all physical ties (terminate leases, sell local property) in your old high-tax state.

## 5. Foreign Bank Account Reporting (FBAR & FATCA)

Living overseas often requires opening foreign bank accounts for local rent and daily expenses. Under US federal law, foreign financial accounts trigger mandatory disclosure requirements:

### FBAR (FinCEN Form 114)
- **The Rule:** If the aggregate maximum balance of all your foreign financial accounts (bank accounts, brokerage accounts, prepaid cards) exceeds **$10,000** at any point during the calendar year, you must electronically file an FBAR with the US Treasury.
- **Penalties:** Non-willful failure to file an FBAR carries civil penalties exceeding 10,000 dollars per violation, while willful failure can result in penalties of 100,000 dollars or 50 percent of the account balance.

### Form 8938 (FATCA)
- Filed alongside your annual Form 1040 tax return if your foreign financial assets exceed specific thresholds (typically $200,000 for single expats living abroad at year-end, or $400,000 for married couples).
