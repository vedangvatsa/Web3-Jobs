The Aztec Foundation released Aztec Tierra, an upgrade designated Alpha V6, on Tuesday. The [announcement](https://aztec.network/blog/introducing-aztec-tierra) said the upgrade delivers stronger security, formal verification of the proving system and faster deposits from Ethereum, alongside a new set of private applications and a refreshed website.

The security work centers on a flaw in the previous version. [Altcoin Buzz reported](https://www.altcoinbuzz.io/aztec-tierra-upgrade-patches-critical-v5-flaw-and-doubles-proof-speed) that core contributors found the problem on July 27 through internal AI-assisted auditing and that Aztec disclosed it on Aug. 7. The finding said an attacker may be able to construct a proof that passes verification for a transaction the network should reject. Aztec's disclosure stated that the issue "places V5 funds, applications, and contract state at risk" and that contributors "cannot determine whether anyone exploited the flaw before this finding." No losses have been confirmed, and none have been ruled out.

Tierra "resolves identified vulnerabilities from previous versions, including the critical issue discovered in V5," the foundation said, and layers on further hardening. That includes new AI-powered security testing tooling and formal verification of core cryptographic components, which [Decrypt described](https://decrypt.co/382387/ethereum-privacy-network-aztec-security-speed-upgrade) as a mathematical process for proving code behaves as intended rather than relying only on tests and audits. Altcoin Buzz pointed out that Tierra is still an Alpha release, a stage Aztec itself describes as existing to surface findings of the kind that turned up in V5.

The upgrade also speeds things up. Private transactions now generate client-side proofs in about one second on a native device, which Aztec calls a twofold increase over the previous network. A new architecture cuts Ethereum-to-Aztec message delivery from an average of two minutes to as low as 12 seconds, speeding up deposits and cross-chain activity. Altcoin Buzz noted that the 12-second figure is a best case rather than an average.

"Unstoppable apps need unstoppable infrastructure," said Zac Williamson, co-founder and chief executive of the Aztec Foundation. "Privacy shouldn't be something you have to request or trust someone else to protect. It should be built into the network."

The launch arrives with applications covering payments, trading, AI and social. Leading the list is zk.money, a private payments and savings app built by Aztec Labs that the team revived this year, according to Decrypt. With Tierra's faster messaging, users can deposit directly from any Ethereum wallet into the app without complex bridging. Others include Galactica for private AI inference, Nemi for private perpetuals trading, Nulo as a private browser wallet, Xochi for private intents and trading, Nyx for private payments to any Ethereum wallet, and Olla, which offers liquid staking for the AZTEC token.

"Users can deposit directly from any Ethereum wallet and make lightning-fast private transactions without complicated bridging workflows," said Joe Andrews, co-founder and chief executive of Aztec Labs.

The network itself runs with more than 3,000 validators, and Aztec says it has no privileged viewing keys and no centralized sequencer, the operator that orders transactions on many layer-2 networks. Decrypt reported that Aztec raised $17 million in an earlier funding round to build out the network.

The launch list also includes Rolling Chat, a private message board based on Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin's own Aztec experiments, and the foundation tied the release to a wider push for privacy on Ethereum. "The future of private applications won't be a collection of privacy solutions launched and controlled by centralized networks," said David Steinrueck, vice president of marketing at the Aztec Foundation. "It will be built on composable, programmable smart contracts on a permissionless, open network."

One open question remains for anyone still on the old version. Altcoin Buzz reported that whether existing V5 contracts must be recompiled and redeployed at new addresses, and how long V5 will keep receiving support, has not been confirmed in Aztec's announcement.
