The Commodity Futures Trading Commission approved a designated contract market and a derivatives clearing organization built on blockchain infrastructure on Nov. 16, and crypto exchange Backpack said the same day that the approvals cover its US derivatives exchange and clearing operations. Chairman Michael Selig said the decisions show that [a regulated pathway exists for a new generation of market infrastructure built using blockchain technology](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/SpeechesTestimony/seligstatement111626).

In his statement, Selig wrote that the Commodity Exchange Act sets core principles for exchanges and clearinghouses without prescribing any particular technology. Firms that run on blockchain, he said, must satisfy the same standards as traditional market infrastructure, including anti-money laundering programs, customer protection and fund segregation, market surveillance, financial resources, risk management policies and system safeguards. The chairman commended the staff review that preceded the approvals.

The clearing half of the package landed first. The commission registered TLD Clearing, Inc., doing business as Backpack Clearing, on Nov. 13, [the order of registration shows](https://www.cftc.gov/media/14926/TLD%20Clearing%2C%20Inc.%20-%20DCO%20Registration%20Order%20%2811-13-2026%29/download). The order authorizes the clearinghouse to clear two categories of business: futures on a margined basis, and fully collateralized futures, options on futures and swaps. It also requires the firm to treat funds in clearing member accounts as member property under the US Bankruptcy Code, held separate from the company's own money, and to police its own admission and eligibility rules for clearing members.

Backpack plans to offer perpetual futures trading around the clock to US customers, accept both fiat and crypto collateral, allow cross margining across positions, and give traders access to global liquidity through a unified order book, [Crypto Briefing reported](https://cryptobriefing.com/cftc-approves-blockchain-derivatives-exchange-clearinghouse/) on Nov. 16. The company has not announced a launch date for those services.

Mark Wetjen, president of Backpack US, said the approvals show blockchain infrastructure can operate under federal oversight while moving derivatives price discovery from offshore venues to the United States. "This is an achievement that demonstrates that the highest standards of regulatory compliance and blockchain innovation can go hand in hand," Wetjen said.

Chief executive Armani Ferrante said Backpack has run the regulatory approval playbook before entering markets across Asia, Europe, the UAE and now the US. "The opportunity in the U.S. extends far beyond crypto traders," Ferrante said, adding that the company aims to bring financial products together on one platform while keeping liquidity available to sophisticated market participants.

The exchange has already shipped regulated perpetual futures in Europe and built real-time proof of reserves on its blockchain infrastructure, [the report noted](https://cryptobriefing.com/cftc-approves-blockchain-derivatives-exchange-clearinghouse/). The commission's registration order covers the clearing entity only, while the designation approved on Nov. 16 covers the trading venue.

In clearing, Backpack's entity told the commission it will monitor and enforce compliance with its own admission and continuing eligibility standards for clearing members, and enforce the rest of its rules against them, the order states. Commission staff reviewed the application, all of its amendments and the firm's representations, then ran a technical evaluation of the proposed operations before concluding that the registration requirements under Section 5b of the Commodity Exchange Act were met.

Selig said in his statement that he has pushed since taking the chairmanship for clear rules of the road so American innovators can build and invest on US soil. He added that today's approvals demonstrate the agency's principles-based framework can handle emerging technologies. The commission staff spent time learning about blockchain technology during the application review, Selig said, and he thanked them for that work.

The chairman placed the decision in the market's history, writing that US derivatives markets have evolved alongside new technology from open outcry trading to electronic markets, and that blockchain presents another opening to modernize financial markets. He said the approvals show the framework remains equipped for that work. The statement did not set a deadline for any follow-up steps, and no launch date has been announced for Backpack's US services.

The TLD Clearing order was signed in Washington on Nov. 13, 2026, three days before the chairman's statement on the paired exchange approval. Selig said in that statement that firms employing novel technologies must clear the same bar as those on traditional rails, including financial resources and risk management requirements.
