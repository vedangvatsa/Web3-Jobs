Backpack said Wednesday that the National Futures Association approved it to operate as a futures commission merchant, the third US regulatory approval the crypto exchange has collected in a week and the last one it says it needs before offering leveraged perpetual futures to American customers.

The company [announced](https://learn.backpack.exchange/blog/backpack-secures-futures-commission-merchant-license-and-nfa-membership-completing-us-cftc-regulatory-framework) Nov 18 that the NFA cleared it to act as a futures commission merchant, the intermediary that onboards customers and runs compliance checks on them. The NFA licenses and supervises such firms under authority delegated by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, Unchained [reported](https://unchainedcrypto.com/crypto-exchange-backpack-says-nfa-approval-completes-stack-to-bring-leveraged-perps-to-u-s/).

The approval arrived two days after Backpack announced its designated contract market and derivatives clearing organization licenses. Bitcoin.com News [wrote](https://news.bitcoin.com/regulation-and-legal/backpack-securies-final-us-regulatory-approval-leveraged-crypto-futures/) Tuesday that the clearinghouse arm, Backpack Clearing, received its registration Nov 13 and that the exchange arm, TLD Markets operating as Backpack Exchange, was designated Nov 16.

"The FCM license completes the picture," Mark Wetjen, president of Backpack US and a former acting CFTC chairman, said in the announcement. "With the exchange, the clearinghouse, and now the intermediary all operating under CFTC oversight, we can offer U.S. customers a single, fully regulated path to trade on the most advanced market infrastructure available." He added in the release that "customer protection, transparency, and compliance are built in at every layer."

The three licenses together establish an integrated structure covering trade execution, clearing and customer services, according to Bitcoin.com News. Backpack described the FCM as critical infrastructure for the customer-facing side of the business. The FCM is the piece that onboards end customers and screens them, including anti-money laundering checks and checks against sanctions administered by the Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control, the company said.

Under those approvals, US customers would be able to take leveraged positions around the clock in crypto perpetual futures and other perpetual contracts, plus event contracts that pay out on real-world outcomes, Backpack said. Perpetual futures are futures contracts with no expiry date. Customers could post fiat or crypto as collateral, cross-margin positions against one another and reach the exchange's global liquidity through a single order book, according to the announcement. The company called the plans the first US entry by a crypto-native exchange to offer leveraged perps with globally pooled liquidity.

CEO Armani Ferrante said in the same release that Backpack has "taken the front door approach in every market we've entered." He said it was critical for the company "to set the standard in the U.S. for what a fully regulated, fully compliant, crypto-native exchange looks like," and that investors "should not have to turn to offshore exchanges to access these investments."

Backpack says it serves users in more than 150 countries and regions and has handled more than $450 billion in trading volume. Its group also includes a self-custodial wallet and Backpack Securities, a brokerage arm that tokenizes assets. The company framed the approvals as moving price discovery from offshore venues onto US regulated infrastructure; the release does not name customers, venues or volumes that would shift.

No launch date is set. The company has not announced when American derivatives trading will go live, [the report](https://news.bitcoin.com/regulation-and-legal/backpack-securies-final-us-regulatory-approval-leveraged-crypto-futures/) says.

The approval lands in a crowded push to move perpetual futures onto regulated US venues. Backpack's earlier licenses arrived alongside those of tokenization company Tzero, which received CFTC authorization for its own derivatives exchange and clearinghouse, Bitcoin.com News reported Tuesday. The same report noted that Kraken parent Payward is pursuing regulated perpetual futures for eligible US customers through the Hyperliquid protocol, using Bitnomial's exchange infrastructure and NinjaTrader's brokerage operations, subject to regulatory approval. It also reported that the CFTC has recently opened the door to perpetual futures tied to individual US stocks, extending the products beyond crypto.

Industry figures are treating the shift as a long time coming. [Unchained quoted](https://unchainedcrypto.com/crypto-exchange-backpack-says-nfa-approval-completes-stack-to-bring-leveraged-perps-to-u-s/) Franklin Crypto head Chris Perkins saying earlier this month on its Bits + Bips podcast that "we're finally seeing innovation come on shore for the first time in decades, frankly," a comment the story tied to Intercontinental Exchange's planned US market for perpetual futures tied to Hyperliquid.
