BIT Asset Management's Bitcoin Fund ETF, the first Swiss exchange-traded fund to hold bitcoin directly, began trading on SIX Swiss Exchange on Nov. 26 under the ticker BBIT. The fund launched with 350 bitcoin in assets, the company said in [its announcement](https://www.bit.com/pressroom/six-listing), and SIX welcomed the listing in a [media release](https://www.six-group.com/en/newsroom/media-releases/2026/20261126-bit-etf-issuer.html) the same day.

The debut was quiet. [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/bit-debuts-switzerlands-first-spot-bitcoin-etf) reported that 1,601 shares had changed hands by the first hour of trading, with the fund opening at 8 Swiss francs (about $9.6) per share and rising 0.6% to 8.05 francs by 9 a.m. UTC. BBIT trades in both Swiss francs and US dollars.

## Structure and custody

BBIT is structured as a Swiss investment fund under the Collective Investment Schemes Act, not as the debt-style exchange-traded products that have carried bitcoin exposure in Europe until now. The exchange said the fund tracks the SIX Reference Rate Bitcoin Index with a passive strategy and holds its coins offline at a Swiss bank, segregated from other assets.

BIT named AMINA Bank, a FINMA-regulated Swiss bank, as the custodian of the fund's bitcoin, and said the assets are legally segregated under Swiss law. Pernet von Ballmoos (PvB) is the issuer, with BIT Asset Management acting as sponsor, asset manager and distributor. The public offering is limited to Switzerland, and the fund can be bought through banks, brokers and investment platforms connected to SIX, without a wallet or exchange account.

"We're comfortable launching BBIT now," Cynthia Wu, co-founder and chief commercial officer of BIT, [said in the release](https://www.bit.com/pressroom/six-listing), adding that the product gives investors outside the United States a regulated way to hold bitcoin through the bank or broker they already use.

## A fund, not a note

BIT framed the launch around that structural difference. In its release the company said that until now, investors outside the United States who wanted regulated bitcoin exposure had limited options, because US spot bitcoin ETFs are not directly accessible to many non-US investors and most European bitcoin products are exchange-traded products, which are debt instruments carrying the credit risk of their issuer.

Wu said in the release that investors who want a US product have plenty of choice, but that anyone wanting a non-US product in a neutral jurisdiction "really didn't" until now. BIT said the fund's assets are legally segregated under Swiss law and its bitcoin is held in segregated cold storage at AMINA Bank, while investors hold fund units with rights under Swiss collective investment scheme law rather than an issuer debt claim.

FINMA approved the launch of the bitcoin ETF in October, Cointelegraph noted, and the fund remains subject to the Collective Investment Schemes Act, which governs Swiss investment funds and provides investor protection measures.

## Approval and scale

FINMA approved the fund on Oct. 20, 2026, after an 18-month review that included an exemption from Swiss fund diversification rules, allowing the fund to hold bitcoin alone. BIT said BBIT follows a physically backed strategy without derivatives or securities lending, and called it Europe's only spot bitcoin ETF. The claim has a qualifier: The Jacobi fund listed on Euronext Amsterdam in August 2023 as Europe's first spot bitcoin ETF, and that listing has been inactive since February, according to Cointelegraph.

The listing lifts the number of ETF issuers at SIX to 41, the exchange said, and adds to a market of more than 8,200 tradable ETFs across its listed market and RFQ services. SIX said it has added 325 new ETF listings so far this year.

SIX has been listing crypto-linked products for a decade. It said bitcoin-linked structured products traded on its venue as early as 2016, it became the first regulated stock exchange worldwide to list a physically backed crypto-basket ETP in November 2018, and a physically backed single-asset bitcoin ETP followed in 2019. More than 70 approved crypto assets are eligible as underlyings to ETPs and structured products at the exchange. BBIT enters that lineup as an investment fund rather than a note.

BIT Asset Management, a FINMA-supervised manager headquartered in Zurich, received its license in 2018 and launched Switzerland's first FINMA-approved crypto fund in December 2021.
