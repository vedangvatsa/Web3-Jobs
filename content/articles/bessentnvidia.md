Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent gave Nvidia's board 10 days on Sunday to explain potential export-control failures, putting the chipmaker's handling of alleged diversions of advanced AI chips to China under direct federal scrutiny. The demand, made on Nov. 15, centers on allegations that Nvidia chips reached China through Megaspeed, a company based in Singapore, and the board's response is due within 10 days of that date.

Bessent sought immediate clarification from Nvidia's directors about the company's compliance with US export controls, and he said potential breaches could undermine national security, [TokenPost reported](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/regulation/57898). The concern is diversion through an intermediary in a third country rather than a direct Nvidia sale to China.

Under current US policy Nvidia cannot sell chips above the H200 and H100 tier to China, [Crypto Briefing reported](https://cryptobriefing.com/bessent-nvidia-board-export-controls-deadline/). Those chips train large AI models, which makes their destination strategically significant. The alleged problem is not a direct sale but the possibility that controlled hardware reached China anyway through a Singapore-based route.

Bessent's remarks were aimed at more than Nvidia. He also expressed frustration with the Commerce Department, led by Secretary Howard Lutnick, over earlier recommendations to add Megaspeed to the US Entity List. Multiple government agencies had reportedly supported that move, but Commerce did not act on it, [the report said](https://cryptobriefing.com/bessent-nvidia-board-export-controls-deadline/).

Washington cites the risk of technology leakage as its reason for the China limits, and most consumer hardware does not carry the same restrictions as the AI chips at issue. On the Commerce dispute, the earlier entity-list recommendations came from multiple government agencies and were not followed, which is the gap the Treasury secretary raised alongside his questions for the board.

The two accounts were published within minutes of each other on Nov. 15. Crypto Briefing posted its report at 3:07 p.m. EST, and TokenPost followed at 3:12 p.m. EST. Both describe the same 10-day deadline, the same Megaspeed allegations and the same complaint about Commerce.

Rather than a direct Nvidia sale to China, the complaint is that controlled hardware moved through a Singapore-based intermediary, which puts the question for the board on where its products ended up after sale and what the company can verify about that path. The response is due within 10 days of the Nov. 15 demand, which puts the deadline on about Nov. 25.

Congress has taken notice of the same dispute. Senate Banking Committee Chair Tim Scott has reportedly summoned Lutnick and Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang to testify about export-control enforcement, and the Treasury secretary's comments have fueled bipartisan concern on Capitol Hill, [the report said](https://cryptobriefing.com/bessent-nvidia-board-export-controls-deadline/). Nvidia may face a broader investigation into its sales practices and compliance culture.

The scrutiny lands as Nvidia prepares to report fiscal third-quarter earnings. Export restrictions have shaped the company's business for years. The Megaspeed allegations raise a different question from what Nvidia is allowed to sell: whether the company can verify where its products end up after purchase.

The 10-day window gives Congress and investors a concrete date to watch. A detailed, credible answer from the board could ease pressure, while a vague one could invite the deeper investigation lawmakers are already signaling, [according to the report](https://cryptobriefing.com/bessent-nvidia-board-export-controls-deadline/). Compliance failures, if confirmed, could bring restrictions on Nvidia's product sales and hit revenue. Whether Commerce moves to add Megaspeed to the entity list may serve as an early test of how aggressively the administration enforces chip export rules.

Bessent has effectively put Commerce on notice alongside Nvidia, [the report noted](https://cryptobriefing.com/bessent-nvidia-board-export-controls-deadline/). TokenPost's account of the demand matches on the deadline, the national security framing and the criticism of Lutnick's department.

The two outlets agree on the core sequence: Bessent's Sunday demand, the 10-day clock, the Megaspeed allegations and the Commerce complaint. Neither has reported a response from Nvidia's board, and the company has not published an answer to the Treasury secretary's request.
