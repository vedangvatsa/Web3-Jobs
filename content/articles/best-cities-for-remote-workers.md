This guide helps remote workers choose a base to live and work from in 2026. It covers five cities where you can find reliable internet, coworking options, and a clear legal path to stay. Each city includes real budget ranges, visa requirements tied to 2026 official thresholds, and honest trade-offs so you can match your income, time zone, and lifestyle to the right place.

For context on remote work in Web3, see our guides to [remote work](/remote-work-in-web3) and the [crypto guide for digital nomads](/crypto-guide-for-digital-nomads).

## Who This Guide Is For

* Remote employees who must join calls in US or EU time zones and need stable fiber or broadband.
* Freelancers and founders who bill clients outside the country they live in and need a legal stay beyond 90 days.
* People comparing costs before signing a lease. You will find monthly rent and total budget ranges, not vague "affordable" labels.
* Web3 professionals who want access to community without paying San Francisco or London prices.

If you need visa-free travel for a few weeks only, a tourist permit may be enough. If you plan to stay months and sign a lease, use the visa routes below.

## How We Chose These Cities

We evaluated each city on seven factors you can verify:

* **Cost of living:** rent for a one-bedroom, plus total monthly spend for a single person. Sources: Numbeo, Bleap Finance, Wise, and local 2026 expat cost guides.
* **Internet reliability:** availability of fiber in central neighborhoods and typical coworking speeds.
* **Safety and daily life:** neighborhood-level safety, healthcare access, air quality, and transport.
* **Community:** number of coworking spaces, meetups, and an existing remote worker base.
* **Visa and legal stay:** is there a dedicated remote work permit or a practical long-stay alternative, and what does it require in 2026.
* **Time zone:** overlap with US/EU clients.
* **Lifestyle fit:** climate, walkability, and how easy it is to live without a car.

Thresholds change every year. Verify the current income figure with the consulate where you will apply before you gather bank statements.

## 1. Lisbon, Portugal

Lisbon draws remote workers for Atlantic light, mild winters, safety, and Schengen travel, not for low prices. The "cheap Lisbon" story from 2018 no longer matches 2026 rents.

**What it costs in 2026:**
* One-bedroom: 1,000 to 1,700 EUR per month. Average for a central T1 is about 1,250 EUR. Furnished flats on expat platforms cost 15 to 25 percent more than unfurnished local leases on Idealista. Source: Bleap Finance July 2026, Numbeo August 2026.
* Single-person total: 2,000 to 2,500 EUR per month with rent, groceries (about 300 EUR), transport pass (about 40 EUR), and broadband (about 38 EUR). A family of four averages 3,206 EUR without rent, or about 4,355 EUR with rent. Rent growth slowed to about 2 percent year over year in 2026, but supply near Metro stations remains tight.
* Time zone: Western European Time (UTC+0, UTC+1 in summer). Good overlap with the UK and EU, morning overlap with US East Coast.

**Internet and work base:**
Fiber is widely available in central districts. Coworking is dense in Santos, Marvila, and Parque das Nacoes, with hot desks from about 150 to 250 EUR per month. Most cafes have decent wifi, but expect noise and limited outlets during peak hours.

**Visa to stay long term:**
Portugal has no golden visa for remote work. The path is the D8 Remote Work Visa, run by AIMA (Agency for Integration, Migration and Asylum). You apply at a Portuguese consulate or VFS center in your country of residence.
* Income: 4 times the Portuguese minimum wage. In 2026 the minimum wage is 920 EUR, so the threshold is 3,680 EUR per month for a single applicant, plus 50 percent for a spouse and 30 percent per child. You must show bank statements and contracts that prove consistent remote income from outside Portugal.
* Savings: at least 11,040 EUR.
* Two routes: temporary stay (up to 1 year, renewable in practice varies by consulate) or residence route (initial 4-month visa that you convert in Portugal to a 2-year residence permit, renewable for 3 years). Both require private health insurance, a clean criminal record, and proof of accommodation.
* Tax: the old NHR regime ended in 2024. The replacement IFICI offers a flat 20 percent on qualifying Portuguese-source income for specific high-value roles, but most generic remote work does not qualify. Plan tax residency separately. Source: AIMA, Global Citizen Solutions and Lexidy 2026 guides.

**Pros:**
* EU base with Schengen travel, strong tech hiring, and English widely spoken in work settings.
* Safe, walkable, with good public transport and public healthcare access once resident.
* Year-round outdoor life and easy flights to the rest of Europe.

**Cons:**
* Rents are high for local wages. Expect bidding and fast turnover for flats under 1,000 EUR near a Metro.
* Summer crowds in central neighborhoods. Winter is mild but can feel damp.
* Bureaucracy at AIMA can be slow. Book appointments early and keep every document apostilled and translated.

**Best for:** EU-focused remote workers who want Schengen mobility, reliable infrastructure, and are comfortable paying mid-priced Western European rent to get it.

## 2. Chiang Mai, Thailand

Chiang Mai has hosted remote workers for more than a decade. It is quiet, compact, and inexpensive, with a mature nomad support system in Nimmanhaemin and the Old City.

**What it costs in 2026:**
* Single-person total: 1,200 to 1,800 USD for a comfortable setup. Budget nomads can manage 800 to 1,000 USD by choosing local food and a basic studio. A modern one-bedroom with pool is 250 to 450 EUR per month. Sources: DMCQuote 2026, HelloThailand, Where in the World is Nina July 2026 update.
* Coworking: 80 to 200 USD per month. Street food 0.80 to 2 USD, mid-range dinner 4 to 8 USD per person.
* Time zone: Indochina Time (UTC+7). Good for APAC and EU overlap, late evenings for US calls.

**Internet and work base:**
Fiber is common in central condos and cafes. Nimman has dozens of coworking spaces where you can rotate daily. International airport has limited direct long-haul flights compared to Bangkok.

**Visa to stay long term:**
Thailand launched the Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) in June 2024. It is a 5-year multiple-entry visa that allows up to 180 days per entry, with one 180-day extension inside Thailand to reach 360 days before you must exit and re-enter.
* Income: about 16,000 USD per year from remote work, proven by bank statements, contracts, or tax returns. Many agents advise showing 2,000 to 3,000 EUR per month to provide a buffer.
* Cost: 10,000 THB, about 280 USD.
* Rules: apply outside Thailand, work only for non-Thai companies or clients, family can be included. Stay 180 days or more in a calendar year and you may become Thai tax resident, so plan stays carefully.

**Pros:**
* Low daily costs, excellent food, mountain and temple surroundings, and a large, welcoming nomad community.
* Easy to find short-term housing and coworking without a long lease.

**Cons:**
* Burning season from February to April brings poor air quality. Check IQAir before booking that period.
* Healthcare for complex cases often means a trip to Bangkok.
* Thai language helps outside central areas, and long-term cultural isolation is possible if you do not engage locally.

**Best for:** budget-focused workers who want community, warm weather from November to February, and the lowest monthly burn of the cities on this list.

## 3. Medellin, Colombia

Medellin offers spring-like weather year round in the Aburra Valley, a working metro and tram system, and costs well below US or Western European cities. It has moved from a short-stay stop to a longer base for many remote workers.

**What it costs in 2026:**
* Single-person total: 1,200 to 1,800 USD comfortable, up to 3,000 USD for a high-end setup with modern apartment, gym, and coworking. Source: The Rio Times June 2026 expat guide, Wise 2026.
* One-bedroom: El Poblado or Laureles 500 to 1,200 USD, Envigado or Sabaneta 300 to 700 USD. Groceries about 200 USD per month per person, basic utilities about 95 USD plus internet about 28 USD, metro pass about 67 USD.
* Time zone: Colombia Time (UTC-5). Same as US Eastern Standard Time, which makes US client work simple.

**Internet and work base:**
Wired internet in El Poblado and Laureles is solid for video calls. Coworking is plentiful and often includes community events. Altitude is about 1,500 meters, so give yourself a few days to adjust.

**Visa to stay long term:**
Colombia issues the Visa V - Nomadas Digitales under Resolution 5477 of 2022, Article 46. Official information is published by the Cancilleria (Ministry of Foreign Affairs).
* Income: 3 times the Colombian monthly minimum wage (SMMLV). In 2026 that is about 4,680,000 COP per month, roughly 1,200 to 1,400 USD depending on the exchange rate on the day the file is reviewed. Each month must clear the threshold, not just the average. Show bank statements for the last 3 months with matching foreign-source deposits.
* Validity: up to 2 years. It is a Visitor category visa, so time on it does not count toward permanent residency or citizenship. It is multiple entry.
* Limits: you may not work for Colombian companies or take Colombian-source income. Health insurance that covers repatriation for the full visa period is required. Criminal background check, apostilled and translated to Spanish, is often requested. Processing is typically 2 to 6 weeks, decision window up to 30 days, government fees about 220 to 320 USD total.
* Tax: 183 days of presence in a calendar year can trigger Colombian tax residency on worldwide income. The visa's practical annual presence cap is often described as 180 days. Confirm the current interpretation with the Cancilleria or a Colombian immigration attorney before you plan a full year.

**Pros:**
* Excellent climate, low to mid costs, and a growing startup and tech community with frequent meetups.
* Efficient metro and affordable ride apps. Excellent coffee and food scene.

**Cons:**
* Safety varies by neighborhood. El Poblado, Laureles, and Envigado are calm for daily life. Phone snatching near nightlife in El Poblado is the most common issue. Nationally Colombia holds a US Level 3 "Reconsider Travel" advisory reissued March 31 2026, while the UK FCDO does not flag Medellin specifically. Practice no dar papaya, avoid flashing valuables and use ride apps at night.
* Spanish helps a lot for leases, healthcare, and daily admin. English is less common than in Lisbon or Budapest.
* Currency swings can move your income below the peso threshold between application and review. Apply with a clear buffer above the minimum.

**Best for:** remote workers who want US time zone alignment, spring weather, and a lower monthly spend than Mexico City or Lisbon.

## 4. Budapest, Hungary

Budapest pairs Central European architecture and thermal baths with prices below Vienna or Prague. It is one of the most affordable EU capitals in 2026.

**What it costs in 2026:**
* Single-person total: 1,000 to 1,700 EUR per month. Budget living can be 640 to 1,100 EUR outside the center or in university cities like Debrecen and Szeged. Source: Megaport January 2026, ExpatLife.ai.
* One-bedroom centre: 670 to 900 EUR. Smaller towns offer 500 to 700 EUR.
* Time zone: Central European Time (UTC+1, UTC+2 in summer). Full overlap with EU clients, early morning for US East Coast.
* Internet: fiber 100 to 500 Mbps in city areas, 60+ coworking spaces in Budapest, average coworking hot desk about 122 USD per month.

**Visa to stay long term:**
Hungary's White Card is the official residence permit for remote work. Official source: National Directorate-General for Aliens Policing (OIF).
* Who qualifies: non-EU/EEA/Swiss nationals who work remotely for a company outside Hungary or own a profitable company outside Hungary, using advanced digital technology. You may not work for a Hungarian employer or own a Hungarian company share while on this card.
* Income: at least 3,000 EUR net per month for the 6 months before entry, proven by bank statements for 6 months and an employer certificate or tax authority proof. You must maintain this level while in Hungary. A 10,000 EUR savings buffer is commonly advised.
* Validity: 1 year, extendable once for 1 more year. Maximum 2 years total. Extension can only be filed in Hungary via EnterHungary, at least 30 days before expiry and only if you spent at least 90 days in Hungary in the last 180 days. Processing is about 21 to 30 days.
* No family reunification on the White Card. Each partner must qualify separately. Time on the White Card does not count toward a national residence card or permanent residency.

**Pros:**
* Lower rent than most EU capitals, strong cafe culture, and central location for train travel to Vienna, Krakow, or Bucharest. Flat 15 percent personal income tax is often cited, but check if it applies to your foreign-source structure.
* Reliable internet and a compact, transit-friendly city.

**Cons:**
* Winter is grey and cold. Hungarian is difficult and English is limited outside business and central districts.
* Politics and press freedom concerns affect some residents' long-term plans.
* The 2-year cap means you need a different permit if you want to settle longer.

**Best for:** solo remote workers who want an EU base with lower costs than Lisbon and are comfortable with a 1 to 2 year horizon, not permanent settlement.

## 5. Mexico City, Mexico

Mexico City is a large, high-energy capital with top museums, diverse neighborhoods like Roma, Condesa, Juarez, and Polanco, and more Spanish immersion than coastal tourist towns. It is far cheaper than New York or San Francisco but no longer the ultra-cheap city older blogs describe.

**What it costs in 2026:**
* Single-person total: 1,600 to 2,400 USD comfortable, 2,500 to 3,200 USD upscale with central apartment and frequent dining out. Source: Midlife Nomads March 2026, NoodlePants, StayWorkCDMX.
* One-bedroom: furnished via Airbnb or short-stay platforms 800 to 1,200 USD in Roma/Condesa. Local unfurnished leases in pesos are lower but require a longer commitment, guarantor, and Spanish paperwork. Always compare listings in MXN.
* Coworking: 120 to 250 USD per month for a hot desk. Home fiber from providers like Telmex, Izzi, and Totalplay runs 20 to 35 USD per month if not included in rent. Cafes charge 2.50 to 4.50 USD for coffee and are laptop friendly in off-peak hours.
* Time zone: Central Time (UTC-6). Strong overlap with US and Canada.
* Altitude: 2,240 meters. Plan for a short adjustment period.

**Internet and work base:**
Fiber covers most of Roma, Condesa, Polanco, and Juarez. Central coworking is dense, and many apartments include internet that handles video calls. Outages are rare in central areas but possible in older buildings.

**Visa to stay long term:**
Mexico does not have a dedicated digital nomad visa. Remote workers use the Temporary Resident Visa (Residente Temporal) via economic solvency. Official guidelines use UMA (Unidad de Medida y Actualizacion). For 2026 the UMA is 117.31 MXN per day.
* 2026 typical thresholds (verify with the consulate where you apply, as exact peso to dollar conversion varies): about 4,400 USD monthly net income sustained every month for the last 6 months (some consulates ask 12 months), or about 74,000 to 74,700 USD in savings maintained every month for the last 12 months without dropping below the minimum. Add about 1,434 USD per month or equivalent savings per dependent (220 x UMA). Amounts are quoted in Mexican pesos as 680 x UMA for temporary residency income and 11,460 x UMA for savings. Sources: Mexperience August 2026, Ministry of Interior and National Immigration Institute guidelines updated July 2025.
* Validity: 1 year initially, renewable for 1, 2, or 3 more years, up to 4 years total, then you may apply for permanent residency. Apply outside Mexico at a consulate, then complete the canje at an INM office within 30 days of entry. Government fees for year one are about 350 to 450 USD.
* Work: the card via economic solvency does not authorize work for a Mexican employer. Remote work for non-Mexican clients is the standard use, but if you want to earn Mexican-source income you need separate work authorization (about 4,341 MXN in 2026).
* Tax: 183 days of presence in a calendar year can trigger Mexican tax residency. The US taxes citizens on worldwide income regardless of location, so US citizens file in both countries and use treaties or the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion where it applies. Get cross-border advice.

**Pros:**
* Huge cultural offer, strong food scene, excellent private healthcare and low out-of-pocket costs, and a large, active nomad and startup community.
* Good flight connections across the Americas and to Europe.

**Cons:**
* Air quality is moderate to poor in dry and cool months. Check annual AQI before signing a long lease.
* Safety varies by block. Research neighborhoods and use ride apps at night.
* Traffic is heavy and furnished rents in Roma/Condesa carry an expat markup.

**Best for:** people who want big-city amenities, US time zone overlap, and Spanish immersion, and who can meet the income or savings test for temporary residency.

## Emerging Options Worth Watching

* **Bali, Indonesia (Canggu, Ubud):** low daily costs and beach or rice-field living with strong coworking density. Indonesia's remote work options changed in recent years and now include a Second Home Visa and other longer-stay routes with higher financial tests than the old tourist stays. Visa paperwork and local sponsor rules shift often, so confirm current costs and income tests on an official immigration site before budgeting.
* **Oaxaca and Merida, Mexico:** slower pace than Mexico City, stronger local culture, and lower rents. Merida has Caribbean access and a smaller but growing remote community. Same Temporary Resident rules as Mexico City apply.
* **Sofia, Bulgaria and Bucharest, Romania:** affordable EU cities with growing tech activity. Bulgaria and Romania are now full Schengen members since 2025, which helps travel planning. Each has its own long-stay routes, typically freelancer or business-based, rather than a single low-threshold nomad visa.

## How to Pick Your Base and Get Started

1. **Match time zone to your work.** EU overlap: Lisbon or Budapest. US overlap: Medellin or Mexico City. APAC/EU split: Chiang Mai.
2. **Run a real budget.** Add rent + coworking + health insurance + visa fees + flights. Add 15 percent for furnished markup if you book on English-language platforms.
3. **Test before you commit.** Book one month in an apart-hotel or coliving space. Test your building's internet at different times of day and visit coworking spaces before signing a 12-month lease.
4. **Prepare visa paperwork early.** Get bank statements for 6 to 12 months, an employer letter that states remote work and salary in consistent currency, apostilles and certified translations, and travel health insurance that meets the consulate's minimum coverage. For Mexico and Portugal, start 3 to 4 months before your target move date.
5. **Plan health cover and tax.** Check if your policy covers the full visa period and repatriation. Mark the 183-day line for tax residency in Colombia and Mexico, and 180 days for Thailand. Talk to an accountant who knows your citizenship and your host country.

## FAQ

**Which city is cheapest overall?**
Chiang Mai. A single person lives comfortably on 1,200 to 1,800 USD per month, with central one-bedrooms from 250 to 450 EUR. Medellin is next at 1,200 to 1,800 USD, then Budapest at 1,000 to 1,700 EUR, Mexico City at 1,600 to 2,400 USD, and Lisbon at 2,000 to 2,500 EUR. Prices assume central housing plus coworking. Move outside the center or share a flat to cut rent by 20 to 40 percent.

**Which visa has the lowest income bar?**
Colombia at 3 times the monthly minimum wage, about 1,200 to 1,400 USD in 2026, for up to 2 years. Thailand's DTV asks about 16,000 USD per year. Hungary and Portugal both ask about 3,000 to 3,680 EUR per month and require 6 months of consistent deposits. Mexico's Temporary Resident asks about 4,400 USD per month or about 74,000 USD in savings, but it is renewable up to 4 years and leads to permanent residency.

**Can I bring a partner or family?**
Hungary's White Card does not allow family reunification. Each person qualifies alone. Portugal's D8, Colombia's Nomadas Digitales, Thailand's DTV, and Mexico's Temporary Resident all allow partners or children with extra income or savings per dependent. For Mexico, add about 1,434 USD per dependent in 2026.

**Where is internet most reliable for video calls?**
Lisbon, Budapest, and central Mexico City have the most consistent fiber in apartments and coworking spaces. Chiang Mai and Medellin are solid in central neighborhoods and in dedicated coworking spaces, but test your specific building before committing to daily calls.

**Do I need to speak the local language?**
You can manage in Lisbon with English in work and central hospitality. In Budapest, Medellin, Mexico City, and Chiang Mai, basic Portuguese, Hungarian, Spanish, or Thai helps a lot for leases, utilities, and healthcare. In Mexico and Colombia, INM and Migracion offices operate almost entirely in Spanish.

**Is it safe to live in Medellin and Mexico City?**
Both are livable when you choose neighborhoods carefully. In Medellin, El Poblado, Laureles, and Envigado are calm for daily life, with phone snatching near nightlife as the main risk. In Mexico City, Roma, Condesa, Juarez, and Polanco are common bases, with air quality and traffic as the bigger daily issues than violent crime. Follow local advice, avoid showing valuables on the street, and use ride apps after dark.
