Binance Chief Financial Officer Kaiser Ng is stepping down from the cryptocurrency exchange after steering the company's financial operations through significant regulatory restructuring, with senior finance executive Justin Yek appointed as his successor, according to corporate announcements reported by [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/binances-finance-chief-kaiser-ng-to-leave-company-report-says).

The executive departure comes during an intensely turbulent period for the world's largest digital asset exchange. On Oct. 29, Binance suffered a catastrophic security exploit that resulted in the theft of approximately $6.3 billion in virtual assets across multiple blockchains, an incident federal law enforcement agencies have attributed to North Korea's state-sponsored TraderTraitor cyber syndicate.

Binance leadership emphasized that Ng's departure was not prompted by the recent breach. According to statements shared by exchange executives, the leadership handover had been in development for approximately six months as part of a planned transition toward corporate institutionalization under Chief Executive Officer Richard Teng.

Ng served as finance chief during a critical chapter in Binance's corporate history. He managed the exchange's treasury and balance sheet through the aftermath of its comprehensive $4.3 billion settlement with the U.S. Department of Justice, the Treasury Department, and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, detailed in [DOJ plea agreements](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/binance-and-ceo-plead-guilty-federal-charges-4b-resolution) in late 2023. That settlement required the installation of independent corporate monitors and extensive overhauls of internal anti-money laundering and sanctions compliance frameworks.

His successor, Justin Yek, brings extensive experience from both traditional investment banking and digital asset corporate finance. Prior to being named CFO, Yek held senior roles overseeing corporate development, capital allocation, and strategic financial planning at Binance. In his new role, Yek assumes responsibility for corporate treasury management, financial reporting, and the deployment of the exchange's Secure Asset Fund for Users (SAFU) insurance reserves. Binance has maintained that SAFU holds roughly $1 billion in diversified liquid assets, though tracking firms noted that portions of reserve wallets were touched during the October security incident.

Under DOJ and Treasury settlement agreements, Binance remains subject to an independent compliance monitorship led by Forensic Risk Alliance. The monitor's team conducts ongoing on-site reviews of transaction monitoring systems, executive communications, and financial reconciliation practices. Maintaining transparent financial reporting under this level of external oversight represents one of the most demanding responsibilities for the incoming finance chief, who must regularly certify accounting accuracy to international regulatory authorities.

The leadership change arrives as Binance faces mounting operational and regulatory challenges in key jurisdictions. In Europe, the exchange has been notifying customers in several European Union member states, including France, that accounts must be closed by Dec. 31 to comply with localized licensing requirements and prepare for the European Union's Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulatory rollout. Beginning Nov. 29, the exchange will begin applying maintenance fees to unwithdrawn balances in those regions, prompting users to transfer assets to domestic providers. In the Asia-Pacific region, the exchange has also been re-evaluating affiliate exchange structures, including South Korea's GOPAX, to navigate regional capital restrictions and domestic banking rules.

In the United States, congressional scrutiny continues to intensify. U.S. Senator Richard Blumenthal, chairman of the Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, recently established a Jan. 4 deadline for Binance, stablecoin issuer Tether, and associated crypto enterprises to provide detailed corporate accounting and transaction records regarding illicit financing risks and overseas asset movements, as [Unchained reported](https://unchainedcrypto.com/with-probe-gavel-in-reach-blumenthal-warns-binance-and-tether-of-alternative-measures/).

Despite the recent exploit, Binance has continued to maintain regular trading operations and liquid order books, stating that its corporate reserves and operational cash flows remain sufficient to absorb financial losses without impacting customer account withdrawals. The exchange has also coordinated with blockchain analytics firms, including Elliptic and TRM Labs, to track stolen funds moving through decentralized exchanges, liquidity pools, and cross-chain bridges.

For Yek, the primary mandate will involve restoring institutional confidence and guiding the firm through mandatory compliance monitoring reviews. As traditional institutional liquidity providers demand rigorous corporate governance and transparent audited statements, Binance's leadership transition signals an ongoing effort to align its corporate structure with standard multinational financial corporate practices.
