Binance said on Thursday it has completed coverage of the $6.34 billion hacked on October 29, replacing the affected customer tokens with company funds two weeks after the largest crypto theft on record. In its announcement, the exchange wrote that it "has completed coverage of assets impacted in the security incident" and published a [new proof-of-reserves snapshot](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/binance-covers-6-34-billion-hack), repeating the message on X.

Coverage here means the exchange rebuilt customer balances from its own books. It does not mean the stolen coins were recovered. Binance said it committed more than $6.34 billion in company funds and bought the affected tokens through market and over-the-counter channels, drawing on a partial deployment of its Secure Asset Fund for Users, a slice of its futures insurance fund held above the level required by its risk framework, and its corporate treasury. The exchange also committed to replenishing the user-protection fund to about $1 billion after that partial draw. A small number of tokens Binance no longer lists were hit as well; the exchange said those holdings will move through its delisting process and affected users will be covered.

The new reserve snapshot was taken at 00:00 UTC on November 12, or 7 p.m. Eastern on Wednesday, and covers 198 tokens, up from the 10 tokens in the October 31 snapshot taken days after the breach. The figures are posted on the exchange's proof-of-reserves website. Customers cannot check the 1:1 backing claim yet. Binance said the full zero-knowledge proof behind the snapshot will take about 40 to 50 hours to generate because of the computational workload, after which holders of the affected tokens "will be able to independently verify that their assets are backed at a minimum of 1:1." Until that proof lands, the backing figure rests on the exchange's own statement. Binance said the snapshot "provides an updated view of assets that are fully accounted for," and it added that security remains a priority and that work on its infrastructure is still underway. For customers, the two remaining checks are whether the zero-knowledge proof matches the snapshot and how quickly the rest of the withdrawal queues clear.

Withdrawals are still returning in phases. Binance first reopened them on November 1, starting with bitcoin, and has expanded to other networks on a rolling basis. The exchange said it continues to reopen withdrawals in phases and will share further details once they are confirmed, and the Thursday update did not reopen the platform all at once.

The legal side moved the same day. The [US Department of Justice said](https://cointelegraph.com/news/doj-12b-north-korean-crypto-seizure-400m-facestealer-scheme) it obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the $6.3 billion taken, part of more than $2.7 billion in stolen assets that US authorities and private-sector partners have frozen since the hack. Investigators are still working to recover additional funds, the department said. The FBI has attributed the breach to North Korea's TraderTraitor group. Kim Yo Jong, sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, denied her country's involvement, the report said. Cointelegraph also noted Binance's statement the same day that the affected assets were covered and that the new snapshot showed at least 1:1 backing.

Binance first detailed the intrusion in a November 3 incident report. Attackers used a fake job interview and a poisoned developer tool to reach staff, then drained hot wallets on October 29. The exchange had pledged more than $6.34 billion to cover customer losses within hours of the breach, later reporting coverage of 90% and then 95% as withdrawals restarted, followed by 98% of affected tokens replenished, with the remainder in less liquid assets that took longer to buy. Thursday's statement is the first time the exchange says that process is finished.

The announcement gives no total for how much of the stolen crypto has been recovered and does not name an attacker, and the zero-knowledge proof behind the new snapshot was still being generated.
