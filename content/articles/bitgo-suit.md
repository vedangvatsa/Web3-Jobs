Two companies tied to market maker DWF Labs sued crypto custodian BitGo for $141 million in London's High Court, alleging BitGo sold tokens it had agreed to keep locked. The claimants are DWF Maas, based in the British Virgin Islands, and Panama-based Falcon Digital, [Unchained reported](https://unchainedcrypto.com/market-maker-dwf-labs-affiliates-sue-bitgo-for-141-million-over-alleged-early-token-sales/).

The tokens at issue are Falcon Finance (FF) and ESPORTS, the gaming token linked to Yooldo. BitGo received them at a discount in exchange for a commitment not to sell until lock-up and vesting periods expired, then moved them to exchanges roughly two months before the first scheduled release, [as reported Oct. 9](https://decrypt.co/380593/dwf-bitgo-141-million-lawsuit-early-token-sales). The structure called for an initial three-month lock-up with further vesting restrictions after that.

"The discount BitGo received was conditional on the tokens remaining locked," a DWF spokesperson said, describing the early move to exchanges, in remarks [CoinDesk carried](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/10/09/dwf-labs-subsidiaries-sue-bitgo-for-usd141-million-over-alleged-token-lock-up-breach) from the Financial Times, which first reported the suit on Oct. 9. "We raised this with BitGo in April and May, and with no undertaking forthcoming, court action became necessary."

DWF argues the early selling did the damage. Selling into a thin market created heavy downward pressure that eroded the value of the tokens it still held, [per the Oct. 9 account](https://decrypt.co/380593/dwf-bitgo-141-million-lawsuit-early-token-sales). The numbers cited show FF sliding from $0.08 at the start of the lock-up in early March to about $0.07 by late April, while ESPORTS fell from about $0.28 in mid-March to $0.07 by early June, when DWF alleges BitGo sold, [per the same-day account](https://unchainedcrypto.com/market-maker-dwf-labs-affiliates-sue-bitgo-for-141-million-over-alleged-early-token-sales/).

## Positions on each side

DWF says the holding periods were meant to let it build products that would make the tokens easier to trade. Its filing language states there is no contractual or other legal excuse for BitGo transferring or selling them, [one Oct. 9 version notes](https://unchainedcrypto.com/market-maker-dwf-labs-affiliates-sue-bitgo-for-141-million-over-alleged-early-token-sales/). "We remain hopeful of, and open to, resolution," the spokesperson added.

BitGo has not answered the substance in public. The company declined to comment for the Financial Times report, and the allegations have not been tested in court, [as covered Oct. 9](https://decrypt.co/380593/dwf-bitgo-141-million-lawsuit-early-token-sales). Neither company responded to direct follow-up requests on Oct. 9. A fourth account, CoinMarketCap Academy, matched the core facts on Oct. 10 and added the same status line, that BitGo declined to comment and the allegations have not been proven in court, in its [write-up](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/dwf-labs-affiliates-sue-bitgo-141m-token-lock-up).
