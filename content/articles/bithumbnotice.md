Bithumb published an updated notice on Nov. 25 saying an October hack of its internal groupware system exposed more people and far more personal data than it first disclosed, including passport numbers, bank account numbers and crypto wallet addresses. The South Korean exchange also said the intruder had access for about 15 days before being shut out, not the roughly one hour it reported last month.

The revised timeline runs from 1:49 p.m. on Oct. 12 to 3:22 p.m. on Oct. 27, Korea time, during which an unauthorized outside party accessed the Daou Office mobile groupware system and took personal information and files stored there, according to [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/bithumb-hack-exposed-passports-wallet-addresses). Bithumb had previously said the hack lasted about an hour, from 11:28 a.m. to 12:27 p.m. on Oct. 27, and that it detected the intrusion at 12:14 p.m. that day. The new account means the attacker went unnoticed for more than two weeks.

Bithumb first reported the incident on Oct. 29, saying the data of 6,444 customers and 715 employees and partner staff, or 7,159 people in total, had been exposed. It did not give an updated count in the Nov. 25 notice. [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/bithumb-october-breach-additional-data-exposed/) reported that the victim count has since grown past the original 7,159, without a precise figure, and that within the original customer group 844 people had personal data exposed in readable form while another 5,600 customers were affected by leaks of encrypted data.

The list of exposed data now runs to 19 items. It includes member numbers, names, mobile numbers, email addresses, dates of birth, resident registration numbers, passport numbers and driver's license numbers, Bitcoin Magazine reported. It also covers bank account numbers, home addresses, gender, nationality, copies of ID documents, occupations, employer names and work contact details, as well as transaction histories and wallet addresses. Bithumb said not every item applies to every person, and that affected users will be told individually by email and text message what was taken.

Bithumb maintains the core of its business was not touched. The exchange said Daou Office is an internal business system separate from customer exchange accounts and asset data, that it found no sign its exchange service systems or customer database were directly breached, and that digital assets remained safe. It said it has stepped up real-time monitoring of unusual trades and withdrawals. The report described the groupware as the company's internal messaging and collaboration hub, where staff share files, schedules and work communications.

The company has notified affected individuals and pledged to compensate them for any confirmed losses tied to the breach. CEO Lee Jae-won issued an apology to those affected and emphasized that the company would support users against potential secondary harms such as phishing attempts, Crypto Briefing reported. Bithumb set up a dedicated support desk covering financial and legal counseling as well as compensation reviews.

"We will take responsibility for all harm confirmed to have resulted from this personal information breach and provide all necessary support and compensation to affected customers," [Bithumb said in the notice](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/bithumb-hack-exposed-passports-wallet-addresses). The exchange said it blocked the attacker's IP addresses, fixed the vulnerability behind the incident and is running a security review of all of its externally exposed services. It also warned users that it never asks for passwords, verification codes or one-time passcodes by phone, text or messenger, and urged anyone who gets such a request to stop responding, confirm through its official app or customer center and report financial losses to police.

The disclosure adds to a difficult year for the exchange. Bithumb mistakenly credited users with billions of dollars in bitcoin in February, and regulators fined it $24 million and imposed a six-month partial suspension in March over anti-money laundering violations, Bitcoin Magazine reported. Crypto Briefing placed the incident in a broader wave of cyber incidents that hit Korean financial firms during late October 2026.
