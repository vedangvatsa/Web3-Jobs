Staff at the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission issued a formal advisory permitting registered derivatives clearing organizations (DCOs) and futures commission merchants (FCMs) to count qualifying crypto assets, including Bitcoin and Ether, toward their financial resource and operating expense requirements.

The guidance clarifies how regulated derivatives intermediaries can hold and value digital assets on their balance sheets when demonstrating compliance with federal liquidity and capital-adequacy rules. Historically, derivatives clearinghouses and brokerages faced friction when holding digital assets directly, often relying exclusively on cash or U.S. Treasury securities to satisfy regulatory capital buffers.

Under the new advisory, firms must apply conservative valuation haircuts to account for intraday price volatility, maintain segregated custody controls with qualified custodians, and meet daily mark-to-market reporting standards. Assets must also demonstrate sufficient spot-market liquidity and transparent pricing benchmarks before they can be included in financial resource calculations.

The move builds on the CFTC's broader effort to integrate blockchain-based collateral and settlement infrastructure into traditional derivatives markets. Industry participants noted that allowing BTC and ETH to count toward operating and financial resource buffers reduces capital friction for regulated exchanges and clearinghouses operating 24/7 digital asset markets.

For institutional market makers and crypto-native clearing firms, the change means existing digital asset reserves can be utilized more efficiently without forcing unnecessary fiat conversions. At the same time, regulators emphasized that the advisory applies strictly to firm-level financial resource requirements and does not alter existing customer-fund protection rules or segregation mandates.

Market observers expect the guidance to encourage more traditional clearinghouses and futures brokers to expand their digital asset product suites, particularly as institutional demand for regulated crypto derivatives continues to grow.
