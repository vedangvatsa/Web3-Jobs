Staff at the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission issued a formal advisory permitting registered derivatives clearing organizations and futures commission merchants to count qualifying crypto assets, including Bitcoin and Ether, toward their financial resource and operating expense requirements.

The guidance clarifies how regulated derivatives intermediaries can hold and value digital assets on their balance sheets when demonstrating compliance with federal liquidity and capital-adequacy rules. Under the advisory, firms must apply conservative valuation haircuts, maintain segregated custody controls, and meet daily mark-to-market reporting standards.

The move builds on the CFTC's broader push to integrate blockchain-based collateral and settlement infrastructure into traditional derivatives markets. Industry participants noted that allowing BTC and ETH to count toward operating and financial resource buffers reduces capital friction for regulated exchanges and clearinghouses operating 24/7 digital asset markets.

Regulators emphasized that the advisory applies only to assets meeting strict liquidity and custody benchmarks and does not alter existing customer-fund protection rules.
