Circle's Arc blockchain is scheduled to open its public mainnet on Sept. 16, moving the network out of a private mainnet phase that included more than 100 ecosystem and institutional builders. The stablecoin-native Layer 1 uses USDC as its gas token and is designed for payments, foreign exchange, capital markets, tokenized assets, and connected financial applications, according to a [Bitget Wallet guide published Sept. 14](https://web3.bitget.com/en/academy/what-is-arc-blockchain-mainnet). A Sept. 15 report [carried by Gate](https://www.gate.com/news/detail/pools-partners-with-arc-as-first-launchpad-arc-mainnet-launches-september-24279535) confirms the Sept. 16 schedule and names BlackRock and DTCC among the validators.

The founding validator cohort gives the launch its institutional character. Circle's [Aug. 5 announcement](https://www.circle.com/pressroom/circle-announces-founding-validator-cohort-and-major-integrations-for-arc-ahead-of-september-16-mainnet-launch) names BlackRock, DTCC, Galaxy, Global Payments, ICE, Mastercard, MoneyGram, SBI Group, Standard Chartered, Sumitomo Corporation, and Visa alongside Circle itself. Circle describes the arrangement as a network secured by the institutions building on it, intended to meet the trust, security, operational, and compliance standards of critical financial infrastructure. The network's own disclaimers state that Arc runs on a permissioned validator set, even as Circle presents the chain as open for developers to build on.

Executives at several validators tied their participation to payments and settlement work. "Our participation as a founding validator on Arc reflects that commitment, supporting trusted, interoperable infrastructure that can help connect emerging blockchain networks with the broader financial systems businesses rely on every day," said Jorn Lambert, chief product officer at Mastercard. "Arc reflects where the industry is heading: trusted, compliant, and unified infrastructure that makes stablecoins practical for real-world payments," said Anthony Soohoo, chairman and CEO of MoneyGram. Rubail Birwadker, Visa's global head of growth product and partnerships, called Arc compliant, high-trust network infrastructure for onchain payments. Ole Matthiessen of Standard Chartered said the bank sees the launch as progress toward trusted and compliant onchain financial applications for institutions.

Two integrations point to how Arc connects with existing markets. BlackRock is expected to deploy BUIDL, the BlackRock USD Institutional Digital Liquidity Fund, on Arc using its native USDC integration, so institutional investors can subscribe, redeem, and deploy fund assets inside one onchain environment. "Stablecoins and tokenized assets are inextricably linked within the future of financial market infrastructure," said Robert Mitchnick, global head of digital assets at BlackRock. "Purpose-built rails like Arc can support faster settlement, improved collateral mobility, and broader institutional adoption of digital assets." Circle is also working with DTCC to enable tokenization of assets held at The Depository Trust Company on Arc starting in the second half of 2027, which would allow stablecoin-native settlement outside DTC against DTC-tokenized assets while preserving the protections of traditionally held assets. BlackRock, BNY, DTCC, and Standard Chartered are each exploring further integrations spanning tokenized settlement, custody, stablecoin access, and foreign exchange and repo infrastructure.

Day-one activity spans trading, payments, and custody. Circle lists DeFi protocols and capital allocators including Aave, Aerodrome, FalconX, Galaxy, GSR, Keyrock, Morpho, Nonco, Uniswap, and XFX; payments providers Rain, Thunes, and Wirex for card settlement, regulated consumer platforms, and cross-border flows; and exchanges and wallet providers including Binance Wallet, Chainlink, Fireblocks, Kraken, Ledger, MetaMask, Uniswap Labs, and Upbit. Bitget Wallet said it will support Arc from the first day of mainnet with self-custodial asset management, cross-network transfers, trading, and dApp connections. On Sept. 15, meme-coin launchpad Pools announced a partnership making it Arc's first launchpad, an announcement Arc's product lead confirmed by retweeting it, according to PANews reporting carried by Gate.

The network design centers on dollar-denominated fees. Because USDC is the gas token, transaction fees are paid in USDC and users need no separate network token, with costs stated in a stablecoin rather than a volatile asset. Circle's disclaimers add the other side of that design: the ability to transact depends on obtaining and using USDC for gas. The chain targets deterministic finality in under a second, which suits applications that need fast settlement and clear confirmation, and it is EVM-compatible so developers can use familiar Ethereum tooling. At launch, Circle plans a product suite with a composable application framework for common onchain workflows, AI-assisted tools for building applications and smart contracts, capabilities for issuers to deploy and manage tokenized real-world assets, and interfaces for developers, users, and agents.

Circle's own documents set limits on what the launch promises. Arc has not been reviewed or approved by the New York State Department of Financial Services or any other regulator, and Arc Network Services provides software services only, not regulated financial or advisory services. Features may be modified, delayed, or cancelled without notice. The disclaimers cite smart contract vulnerabilities, network disruptions, and the absence of recourse for transaction errors or losses as inherent blockchain risks. "Arc is built on a simple premise: that the global financial system deserves a blockchain network it can trust," said Jeremy Allaire, co-founder, CEO, and chairman of Circle. "With some of the world's most respected financial institutions expected to be live securing the network as validators, more than 100 ecosystem and enterprise builders from the world's largest banks, asset managers, and DeFi protocols already building on Arc private mainnet, Arc is ready for a September 16 launch."

The public mainnet opening on Sept. 16 starts the test of that positioning. Developers and institutions get general access to build payments, trading, DeFi, tokenized-asset, and foreign-exchange applications on the network for the first time. The next dated commitment after launch is the DTCC tokenization integration, targeted for the second half of 2027.
