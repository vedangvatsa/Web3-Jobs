The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency has granted final approval for Coinbase to operate Coinbase National Trust Company under a federal banking charter, according to regulatory filings reported by [Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/read/news/coinbase-secures-final-occ-approval-for-its-national-trust-bank). The decision concludes an application process that began more than a year ago and establishes Coinbase alongside Circle and Ripple among crypto-native firms operating under direct federal banking oversight.

Under the national trust charter, the new entity operates as a limited-purpose trust institution rather than a commercial bank. It does not accept FDIC-insured customer deposits, make loans, or pay interest on cash balances. Instead, the entity will custody institutional digital assets, manage reserve backing for regulated stablecoins, and execute institutional asset settlement within the federal prudential framework established by the OCC, [Bitcoin Magazine reported](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/coinbase-full-occ-national-trust-charter).

The approval allows Coinbase to replace a fragmented patchwork of state money-transmitter licenses with a single federal regulatory framework. For years, digital asset custodians in the United States have operated primarily through state-chartered trusts, such as the New York Department of Financial Services limited-purpose trust framework, or registered as money service businesses under FinCEN. A national trust bank charter establishes federal preemption over state-by-state licensing rules, simplifying multi-state institutional offerings.

Federal oversight brings substantial compliance obligations. Coinbase National Trust Company must maintain separate capital reserves, satisfy federal liquidity guidelines, undergo periodic examination by OCC bank examiners, and submit to regular audits of risk management and internal controls, according to [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/coinbase-occ-approval-national-trust-bank-charter). The trust company must also comply with federal Bank Secrecy Act and anti-money-laundering mandates directly administered by banking regulators rather than only through state agencies.

The OCC's action reflects a broader trend of federal regulators establishing formal oversight for digital asset market infrastructure. Circle received OCC approval for its national trust bank charter earlier in the year, followed by Ripple's approval for custody of its RLUSD reserves. As banking reports highlighted, the approvals signal that the OCC is willing to charter institutions focused purely on asset administration, settlement, and stablecoin reserve management without traditional fractional-reserve lending.

The timing comes amid intense scrutiny of cryptocurrency custody following the record $6.3 billion security breach suffered by Binance on Oct. 29. Traditional financial institutions and institutional allocators have increasingly demanded that custodial counterparties hold federal regulatory credentials with explicit fiduciary standards. By securing an OCC charter, Coinbase can offer corporate treasuries and regulated asset managers custody accounts that meet strict statutory definitions of qualified custodians under federal securities rules.

Coinbase has previously moved to distance its custodial infrastructure from international exchange vulnerabilities, disclosing that more than 99% of its client assets remain in cold storage. Operating through a nationally chartered trust bank ensures customer assets remain legally segregated in bankruptcy-remote custodial accounts under federal banking law, rather than general creditor pools.

The charter also strengthens Coinbase's institutional stablecoin business. Through its partnership with Circle on the USDC ecosystem, Coinbase handles substantial reserve processing and corporate onboarding. As traditional corporate treasuries explore stablecoin settlements for supply-chain payments, federal trust status provides counterparties with standard regulatory disclosures comparable to conventional custody banks. Major commercial banks, including PNC Bank, have recently begun piloting corporate treasury settlement programs using USDC, highlighting the growing intersection between commercial banking and regulated digital asset tokens.

State banking associations and community banking trade groups have previously voiced opposition to OCC trust charters for digital asset firms, arguing in court filings that non-depository charters allow technology companies to bypass commercial banking capital requirements. However, federal courts have affirmed the OCC's statutory authority under the National Bank Act to grant limited-purpose trust charters to fiduciary organizations that administer assets rather than taking demand deposits.

The OCC charter does not expand retail trading or alter Coinbase Inc.'s consumer exchange operations, which continue to operate under existing commercial licenses. Instead, Coinbase National Trust Company serves as an institutional infrastructure anchor, enabling institutional trading desks, pension funds, and asset management platforms to settle digital asset transactions within an established federal banking architecture. The trust company will be governed by a separate board of directors with designated compliance committees directly responsible to OCC examiners in Washington.
