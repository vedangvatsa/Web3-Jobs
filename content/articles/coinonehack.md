South Korean crypto exchange Coinone told customers on Sunday that an attacker reached the servers of MetaM, the contractor that runs its customer support, and viewed or took records of conversations in which people had shared sensitive details. The exchange warned of phishing and identity theft risk and said the potentially exposed data includes passwords, bank account numbers, national identification numbers and crypto wallet addresses, [according to Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/coinone-data-breach-phishing-identity-theft-customer-information).

The intrusion happened at about 4 a.m. local time on Thursday, Nov. 12, and Coinone learned of it around 9:20 a.m. that morning after MetaM reported the problem, the exchange said in its [Sunday notice](https://coinone.co.kr/info/notice/5950), [reported by crypto.news](https://crypto.news/coinone-data-breach-customer-support-hack/). Coinone apologized to customers and said it feels responsibility as the party that handed the support work to the contractor. MetaM detected suspicious activity following a customer-service outage at 6:03 a.m. on Nov. 12 and later found evidence that telephone numbers and consultation conversations had been accessed without authorization or removed from its systems, the contractor said in a Nov. 14 disclosure [cited from Yonhap](https://crypto.news/coinone-data-breach-customer-support-hack/). MetaM reported the incident to the Korea Internet & Security Agency on Nov. 13, isolated affected servers, blocked suspicious IP addresses and brought in cybersecurity specialists.

The full list of data that may have been taken covers information customers supplied during chat and phone support: names, phone numbers, email addresses, dates of birth, bank account numbers, passwords, South Korean resident registration numbers, ID document issue dates, home addresses, wallet addresses and transaction IDs. Coinone said the actual exposure varies by customer because it depends on what each person shared, and that people who never contacted the support center are unaffected. It has not said how many customers are affected.

Coinone said its member accounts, trading platform and crypto wallet systems run separately from MetaM's servers, and that it found no evidence of a direct intrusion into them as of the Nov. 15 disclosure. Investigators are still working out the full extent of the intrusion and what was taken from MetaM's systems. Account access requires a second factor through a registered one-time password or KakaoTalk verification on the customer's own phone, so the leaked details alone would not let an attacker log in or withdraw funds, the exchange explained. The exchange blocked the attacker's IP address and shifted outsourced support to email consultations after learning of the breach, [the exchange said](https://crypto.news/coinone-data-breach-customer-support-hack/).

The exchange warned that criminals could use the support records to impersonate Coinone staff in fraudulent calls, phishing emails and spam messages, and advised anyone who shared a password during a support interaction to change it immediately, along with any other site reusing that password. Coinone said its employees will never ask for passwords, one-time passwords or verification codes by phone, email or text, and told customers not to respond to requests to move crypto or change registered withdrawal addresses. The notice also outlines compensation procedures under South Korean law, including statutory claims of up to 3 million won when actual losses are hard to prove, [the notice said](https://crypto.news/coinone-data-breach-customer-support-hack/).

Coinone reported the incident to the Korea Internet & Security Agency, the Personal Information Protection Commission and the Financial Supervisory Service and is reviewing MetaM's security arrangements with the contractor and outside specialists. It did not respond to a [request for additional comment](https://crypto.news/coinone-data-breach-customer-support-hack/).

The disclosure lands as South Korea tightens rules for financial-sector cybersecurity. The public notice came three days after the intrusion. In October, the Financial Services Commission identified security deficiencies at 130 of 555 financial firms it reviewed, a figure [noted](https://cointelegraph.com/news/coinone-data-breach-phishing-identity-theft-customer-information) in both reports. On Nov. 12, the regulator said crypto exchanges would be allowed to suspend withdrawals when a hack is suspected and called for round-the-clock transaction monitoring, [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/coinone-data-breach-phishing-identity-theft-customer-information). Bithumb has separately reported an employee data breach while investigating whether customer information was accessed, [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/coinone-data-breach-customer-support-hack/) said.
