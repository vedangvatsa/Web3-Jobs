The U.S. Supreme Court declined on Monday to hear Custodia Bank's appeal over its rejected application for a Federal Reserve master account, ending the Wyoming bank's years-long court fight for direct access to the central bank's payment rails. The docket for No. 26-62, Custodia Bank, Inc. v. Federal Reserve Board of Governors, records "Petition DENIED" on Nov. 16, 2026 after the case was distributed for the court's Nov. 13 conference.

The denial leaves in place a ruling from the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit that regional Federal Reserve Banks have discretion to turn away even legally eligible institutions. A master account is a bank's account at a regional Federal Reserve Bank and the entry point to Fedwire and other payment services, so without one a state-chartered bank has to route money through a correspondent institution, according to [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/supreme-court-ends-custodia-fed-master).

Custodia is a Wyoming special purpose depository institution that cannot make loans and must hold dollar deposits fully reserved in liquid assets. It applied for an account at the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City in October 2020 and was told in January 2021 that it was legally eligible, with no showstoppers, and it also sought Federal Reserve membership in August 2021, Bitcoin Magazine reported. The Board of Governors published master-account guidelines in August 2022 that placed firms without federal insurance or federal supervision into the strictest review tier, and Custodia fell into that group. On Jan. 27, 2023, the Kansas City Fed denied the account, citing a business model narrowly focused on crypto-asset activities and concluding that the risks were highly likely to be inconsistent with safe and sound banking practices. The Board rejected Custodia's membership application the same day.

Custodia had already sued the Fed and the Kansas City Fed in June 2022 over the delay in deciding its application. U.S. District Judge Scott Skavdahl ruled in March 2024 that the Federal Reserve Act gives Reserve Banks discretion to grant or deny accounts, and the Tenth Circuit affirmed that decision 2-1 on Oct. 31, 2025. The full Tenth Circuit denied rehearing on March 13, 2026, with Judge Timothy Tymkovich writing in dissent that the statutory language "shall means shall" and that denial of a master account is akin to a death sentence for a bank, as [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/supreme-court-ends-custodia-fed-master) reported.

The petition filed in July was argued by Kannon Shanmugam of Davis Polk & Wardwell and asked whether the Monetary Control Act of 1980 gives presidents of regional Federal Reserve Banks discretionary authority to deny accounts to eligible nonmember banks. The Board and the Kansas City Fed filed opposition briefs on Oct. 13, according to [the Supreme Court docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-62.html). The docket also lists amicus briefs from the Blockchain Association, The Digital Chamber and a group that includes Senator Cynthia Lummis and former Senator Patrick Toomey.

The decision means a state charter, even one that makes a bank legally eligible, does not guarantee a door into the Fed's payment system, and crypto-focused banks now have little judicial recourse in the Tenth Circuit if a Reserve Bank turns them away. Critics of that pattern of exclusion, and of banks cutting off crypto firms, have long labeled it Operation Choke Point 2.0. Neither Custodia nor CEO Caitlin Long had commented on the denial as of Monday morning, [the report](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/supreme-court-ends-custodia-fed-master) said.

The Blockchain Association argued in its August amicus brief that the Fed's power to reject applications could weaken the authority of state banking regulators, according to Cointelegraph. In his March dissent, Tymkovich wrote that without a master account a bank cannot operate in the modern banking system. Custodia's petition itself was distributed to the justices on Oct. 28 for the conference held Nov. 13, three days before the court listed the case as denied.

Custodia's case ran alongside other crypto firms' efforts to reach the Fed's payment system. [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/custodia-bank-loses-supreme-court-bid-for-direct-fed-payment-access) reported that Kraken Financial received approval for a restricted account from the Kansas City Fed in March, allowing it to send and receive payments through Fedwire for an initial one-year term, though without the full services of a master account. The same report said Custodia could still apply for another master account or seek limited access to the payment system under a separate proposal.
