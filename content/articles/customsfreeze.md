Germany's lower house of parliament has passed a customs reform bill that would let authorities freeze cryptocurrency and other high-value assets suspected of having unlawful origins before any criminal investigation is opened. The Bundestag approved the Customs Financial Justice Act on Nov. 12, according to the German government, as [Decrypt reported](https://decrypt.co/383297/german-lawmakers-back-customs-powers-to-seize-crypto-of-unclear-origin). [Cointelegraph's Nov. 16 account](https://cointelegraph.com/news/german-lawmakers-back-crypto-freezes-outside-criminal-cases) of the same vote said the bill explicitly mentions cryptocurrencies and forms part of a wider push against money laundering and organized crime.

The bill targets assets worth more than 100,000 euros, roughly $111,000, where authorities have factual indications that the holdings came from unlawful sources. Under the text lawmakers approved, customs could hold such assets for up to 30 days. Filing an application for confiscation can suspend that deadline while a court decides, and in contested cases a court would have to be satisfied that the assets were of unlawful origin before ordering confiscation, which puts the burden of explaining the money's history on the holder, Cointelegraph reported from a translated version of the bill.

Social Democratic Party lawmaker Ingo Vogel said the powers let authorities pursue suspicious assets even when there are not yet grounds to open a criminal case, according to the Bundestag's summary of the debate. Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil framed the change as a signal to offenders, saying, "With this, we are sending a clear signal to criminals: we are getting tougher," as quoted in the government's own statement. Decrypt said the government describes the instrument as a "suspicious wealth order."

Klingbeil has also said customs could hold assets for up to 180 days without a conviction while owners demonstrate how they acquired them, with permanent confiscation still requiring a court ruling. An amendment from the governing coalition assigns that decision to a panel of finance court judges rather than a single judge. "The Porsche and the Rolex are then gone for now," he said, according to the Berliner Zeitung.

The government expects the law to raise about 308 million euros in additional revenue, a figure opposition lawmakers used against it. The Greens pointed to official estimates that Germany's money laundering problem runs to 100 billion euros and argued the projected intake shows the gap between the law's ambitions and its yield, while the Left Party said the confiscation process is "built for the cash courier, not the banker," Decrypt reported. The Bundestag's finance committee backed the bill with votes from the governing CDU/CSU and SPD as well as the far-right AfD; the Left voted against and the Greens abstained.

Professional groups raised objections before the vote. The Federal Bar Association warned in an Oct. 28 statement that the meaning of unlawful origin is too vague and that missing records risk being treated as evidence of illicit wealth, giving the example of assets inherited across generations. The German blockchain association Bundesblock told lawmakers in a March 31 submission on an earlier draft that blockchain analysis should not automatically count as proof of ownership or control, and that incorrect wallet attribution could infringe fundamental rights.

Beyond the confiscation powers, the bill reorganizes Germany's customs administration, strengthens the agency's Financial Intelligence Unit and gives customs a legal basis to deploy technologies such as artificial intelligence. The measure forms part of an action plan against organized crime that the government adopted on Feb. 25, 2026, Decrypt reported.

Germany has already sold large crypto seizures under its existing rules. Authorities in Saxony sold 49,858 Bitcoin linked to the Movie2k piracy investigation during 2024, raising about 2.6 billion euros, or $2.8 billion, according to Cointelegraph. Decrypt added that analytics firm Arkham later said authorities failed to seize a further 45,000 Bitcoin tied to the same site.

Two steps remain before the customs powers take effect. The bill needs consent from the Bundesrat, the upper house representing Germany's states, and is intended to enter into force on Jan. 1, 2027. Local broadcaster MDR reported on Friday that a Leipzig judge proposed ending proceedings against the main Movie2k defendant in exchange for 40,000 Bitcoin and a payment of 38 million euros for victims, a proposal that remains under consideration.
