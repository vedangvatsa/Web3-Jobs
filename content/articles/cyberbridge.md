South Korean exchanges Upbit and Bithumb suspended deposits and withdrawals of the CYBER token on Nov. 22, hours after the Cyber project said it was investigating unexpected token minting on Solana and a transfer of tokens out of its bridge's Ethereum lockbox. Upbit posted its notice at 2:08 a.m. Korea Standard Time and Bithumb followed at 2:18 a.m., both citing circumstances around a suspected security incident, according to [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/upbit-bithumb-halt-cyber-transfers-bridge).

The two notices invoked Article 17, item 2(b), of the Enforcement Decree of South Korea's Virtual Asset User Protection Act, the legal basis that lets exchanges restrict transfers during a suspected incident, [the report said](https://crypto.news/upbit-bithumb-halt-cyber-transfers-bridge). Upbit's restriction covers CYBER on the Ethereum network; Bithumb named the token without specifying a network. Bithumb kept CYBER trading available during the freeze and said transfers would resume once network stability was secured and a new announcement was published. It did not give a reopening date. Upbit told customers that deposit returns would pause during the interruption and that registering new withdrawal addresses might be unavailable.

Cyber [said on Nov. 21](https://cointelegraph.com/news/upbit-bithumb-suspend-cyber-transfers-bridge-exploit) that it detected unexpected minting of CYBER on Solana, followed by a move of tokens out of the bridge's Ethereum lockbox. Its statement opened: "On November 21, we noticed unexpected minting of CYBER on Solana, followed by a transfer of CYBER out of the bridge's Ethereum lockbox." "Our team is looking into what happened and checking which assets and bridge routes are affected," the project [added in the same statement](https://crypto.news/upbit-bithumb-halt-cyber-transfers-bridge). It asked users to stop using the bridge and to avoid depositing CYBER to exchanges while the review continues. "There's no need to migrate your tokens, connect your wallet, or sign anything," the team told users, [the report said](https://crypto.news/upbit-bithumb-halt-cyber-transfers-bridge).

An incident advisory from 0xCERT, citing on-chain reporting, described a mint of roughly 5.21 million CYBER on Solana and a similar-sized movement from the Ethereum lockbox about two minutes later. Most of the released tokens were reportedly sold for roughly 575 ETH, the advisory said. The advisory and Cyber's own notice do not say how the mint occurred, whether the two movements resulted from a compromised component, or how much value was ultimately lost. Cyber has not established the underlying fault or confirmed a final financial loss.

Cointelegraph [reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/upbit-bithumb-suspend-cyber-transfers-bridge-exploit) that the suspensions came days after South Korea's Financial Services Commission announced plans to let exchanges halt deposits and withdrawals when a hack is suspected, without waiting for a confirmed breach. The commission [described the change on Nov. 12](https://crypto.news/upbit-bithumb-halt-cyber-transfers-bridge) as a no-action letter that would apply only where preventive suspensions met specified conditions, not as unrestricted authority to freeze customer funds.

Cyber, formerly known as CyberConnect, operates an Ethereum layer-2 network for social applications, and its CYBER token moves between supported networks through a bridge that holds tokens on one chain when corresponding assets are issued on another. Bithumb told customers that withdrawal requests submitted before the cutoff would be processed in sequence after system checks. Bithumb also warned customers of possible price fluctuations during the suspension, and deposits sent before the cutoff may take longer to appear if network confirmations are delayed, [the notices show](https://crypto.news/upbit-bithumb-halt-cyber-transfers-bridge). Upbit's notice identified the Ethereum network specifically, and the exchange later published a separate CYBER trading warning. Neither exchange gave a fixed reopening date.

Exchanges in the country have restricted tokens after bridge incidents before. [Upbit and Bithumb suspended SAND transfers on Aug. 22](https://crypto.news/upbit-bithumb-halt-cyber-transfers-bridge) and issued investment-caution notices two days later while reviewing unresolved security concerns, and the Sandbox later said the problem was contained. On Nov. 6, GOPAX suspended trading and transfers after receiving a hacking threat and said it had not detected an actual breach at the time, moving assets toward offline storage while it reviewed the threat.

Cyber's review of the incident remains open. The project has not published a cause, a loss total, or a timetable for restoring bridge routes.
