DBS and JPMorgan launched a service on Nov. 16 that lets institutional clients move tokenized US dollar deposits between Singapore and the United States at any hour, the banks said. The connection links DBS Token Services with JPMorgan's Kinexys platform, and Ant International became the first client to use it for cross-market cash management, according to [Cointelegraph's report](https://cointelegraph.com/news/dbs-jpmorgans-kinexys-launch-tokenized-transfers) on the announcement.

Tokenized deposits are ordinary bank deposits recorded on a blockchain rather than a separate stablecoin issued by a third party. [CryptoSlate described](https://cryptoslate.com/jpmorgan-and-dbs-offer-24-7-dollar-transfers-without-stablecoins/) the service as a way for corporate treasurers to move bank money outside traditional banking hours, without relying on stablecoins.

The two networks are stitched together with Partior, which coordinates settlement between the banks' permissioned systems, CryptoSlate reported. The link puts into production the interoperability framework the banks first announced in November 2025. It also supports the Monetary Authority of Singapore's Project BLOOM, a central bank program launched in October 2025 to expand the use of tokenized deposits and regulated stablecoins for payments at home and across borders. DBS and JPMorgan co-lead one of the project's working groups on transfers between blockchain platforms, Cointelegraph reported.

Hours of operation differ depending on which side of the link a transfer starts on. Transfers inside Kinexys's Blockchain Deposit Account network run 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, CryptoSlate reported, but moving money between those accounts and traditional demand-deposit accounts pauses for maintenance every Saturday from 3 p.m. to 6 p.m. Eastern Time. The banks did not disclose fees, required prefunded balances or transaction amounts in the launch, so customers still lack a like-for-like cost comparison against stablecoin transfers, according to CryptoSlate.

Demand data suggests the banks are not alone in the shift. A DBS survey of 422 business leaders across 11 markets, published Oct. 28, found that more than half already use tokenized deposits or plan to, Cointelegraph reported, citing the bank's report.

The launch also arrives as regulators and banks study how deposit products compete with stablecoins. A Bank for International Settlements working paper published Oct. 13, by researchers Rashad Ahmed and Inaki Aldasoro, used weekly US data from January 2019 through June 2025 and found that higher deposit rates were followed by slower stablecoin growth, with the relationship stronger for USDC than USDT, CryptoSlate reported. A one-standard-deviation increase in deposit repricing tracked with about 2.4 percentage points slower stablecoin growth over ten weeks, the paper said.

CryptoSlate drew the contrast between the two forms of money directly. A tokenized deposit keeps the customer's relationship with its bank, while USDC is issued by Circle affiliates and backed by cash and cash-equivalent reserves, so moving either one on a blockchain does not make their counterparty or redemption arrangements identical. Circle says standard banking-rail USDC redemptions can be free and typically settle in up to two business days, while qualifying Circle Platform and Circle Payments Network customers can obtain free near-real-time redemption, terms that depend on cash pre-positioned at bank partners.

JPMorgan's broader Kinexys platform has handled more than $4 trillion in cumulative transactions and $7 billion in average daily volume, figures the bank reported using its internal data and that CryptoSlate cited. The company also moved its JPM Coin deposit token to Ethereum and Base in October for institutional clients, a public-chain product separate from the permissioned account network DBS now touches.

In September, DBS and Citi's New York office completed a dollar payment over SWIFT Digital Ledger in minutes during a weekend, CryptoSlate reported, one of several projects pushing bank money onto shared ledgers. The Clearing House has also picked Quant to support a tokenized-deposit network connected to RTP and CHIPS, with participating institutions expected to gain access in the first half of 2027.
