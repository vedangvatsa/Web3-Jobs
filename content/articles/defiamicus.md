Eighteen crypto groups asked an Illinois court on Nov. 11 for permission to file a brief arguing that the state's Digital Asset Tax Act is unlawful and cannot be enforced as written. The DeFi Education Fund led the request, joined by 17 other organizations and companies that include venture firm Andreessen Horowitz and Uniswap Labs, which contributes to the development of the Uniswap Protocol, according to [Unchained's Nov. 12 report](https://unchainedcrypto.com/18-crypto-firms-seek-to-join-court-fight-over-illinois-0-2-crypto-tax/). The plaintiffs in the consolidated cases do not oppose the request, and the DeFi Education Fund announced it the following day, but Unchained found no court order granting leave as of its report.

The filing lands in a week of allied submissions against the levy. Over the previous week the US Department of Justice filed a statement of interest while the US Chamber of Commerce and the Securities Industry and Financial Markets Association submitted an amicus filing, [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/us-justice-department-business-groups-defi-illinois-crypto-tax) on Nov. 12. The Justice Department argued on Nov. 6 that the state tax undermines federal efforts to promote cryptocurrency innovation and US leadership in the sector, citing the creation of a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve and the GENIUS Act, which set federal rules for stablecoins.

The Chamber and SIFMA warned in their own Nov. 6 filing that the tax would reach banks, broker-dealers, custodians and asset managers nationwide and could expose them to significant compliance costs. The filing drew a sharp contrast between gains and losses: "This means that a customer who loses money owes the same tax as a customer who earns a profit," it said, and a customer who moves the same asset between two accounts faces the tax on both ends without a sale, a gain or a change in beneficial ownership, [as quoted by Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/us-justice-department-business-groups-defi-illinois-crypto-tax). The groups also said firms would have to build systems to identify Illinois customers, value digital assets and decide which transactions are taxable while facing potential criminal penalties for violations.

The statute itself is Public Act 104-468, which requires brokers that exchange, transfer or hold a digital asset for an Illinois customer to collect 0.2% of the asset's value on each covered transaction, [Unchained explained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/18-crypto-firms-seek-to-join-court-fight-over-illinois-0-2-crypto-tax/). The start date is set in statute for Jan. 1, 2027. Illinois officials and The Digital Chamber jointly asked the court in October to push that date to July 1, 2027, and Cointelegraph's account says the tax was delayed until July 1, 2027, while Unchained said it could not confirm the court had approved the delay. The state's budget office projected the measure would generate $60 million in fiscal 2027 revenue, Cointelegraph reported.

The proposed DeFi brief centers on who would owe the duty. Its authors argue the Act's definitions are vague and overbroad for decentralized finance applications run by code rather than by a company holding customer money, writing that software developers who help people transact for themselves "have no customers to collect from, no assets to remit from, and no way to know where a transaction occurred," [the report said](https://unchainedcrypto.com/18-crypto-firms-seek-to-join-court-fight-over-illinois-0-2-crypto-tax/). The groups point to draft rules the Revenue Department circulated on Sept. 28, which they say stretch "broker" to certain decentralized exchanges, contrary to the Act and incorporated federal regulations that exclude anyone solely validating transactions or providing non-custodial wallets. Reissuing the rules would not fix the problem, the brief adds, because the vagueness is written into the statute itself.

A second line of attack is discrimination. The groups argue Illinois does not tax the same activity when it involves stocks, bonds or precious metals, which they say breaks the Illinois Constitution's Uniformity Clause and the federal Internet Tax Freedom Act, and they ask the court to find the Act "unlawful and unenforceable." Two industry challenges are consolidated in Sangamon County circuit court, one led by The Digital Chamber and one from the Blockchain Association and the Crypto Council for Innovation, with Revenue Director David Harris, Attorney General Kwame Raoul and Sangamon County State's Attorney John Milhiser as defendants. Commodity Futures Trading Commission Chairman Michael Selig called the law effectively a "sin tax" on blockchain technology in a July 1 op-ed, which Unchained cited.

The DeFi coalition's Nov. 11 filing separately warned that Illinois' proposed rules could impose collection duties on software developers and other participants who lack customer information, control over user funds or any way to remit the tax. As of Unchained's Nov. 12 report, the court had not ruled on the request to file the brief.
