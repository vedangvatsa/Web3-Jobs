The Hong Kong Monetary Authority said on Nov. 16, 2026, that it had deepened its collaboration with Swift to explore cross-border payments that use tokenized bank deposits. The work will examine links between Swift's blockchain-based ledger and the settlement layer of Project Ensemble, the HKMA's tokenization initiative for wholesale payments.

In the [press release](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/news-and-media/press-releases/2026/11/20261116-4/), the authority said the effort includes Bank of China (Hong Kong), Standard Chartered Bank (Hong Kong) and HSBC in Hong Kong. The group will develop cross-border payment mechanisms that use tokenized deposits in multiple currencies. Tokenized deposits are bank balances recorded on a blockchain; they remain claims on the issuing commercial bank, unlike central bank digital currency, which is a claim on a central bank, Cointelegraph [explained](https://cointelegraph.com/news/hkma-swift-tokenized-deposits-cross-border-payments).

The HKMA has worked with Swift on central bank digital currency interoperability through the Project Ensemble Sandbox since 2023, the release said. The latest phase extends that work to the wider banking community and to connectivity between the Swift ledger and Ensemble's settlement infrastructure. EnsembleTX, the pilot stage of the project, runs the settlement side of the experiment.

"In Hong Kong, EnsembleTX has entered a new chapter to advance the tokenisation ecosystem," said Howard Lee, deputy chief executive of the HKMA. "On the international front, our collaborative relationship with Swift further extends Project Ensemble's engagement with international financial institutions. This collaboration represents another significant step in exploring how tokenised deposits and interoperable infrastructure can support real-time, always-on and programmable cross-border transactions."

Thierry Chilosi, Swift's chief business officer, said in the same release that Swift already connects market infrastructures that move and settle transactions in 150 currencies. "As domestic systems evolve to support new forms of digital value, we are exploring how the Swift ledger can connect these emerging platforms across borders - helping financial institutions settle transactions more efficiently and bring the benefits of digital value to clients globally," Chilosi said.

The collaboration follows a Nov. 2 upgrade to EnsembleTX, which let banks settle tokenized deposit transactions around the clock using tokenized central bank money, according to [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/hkma-swift-tokenized-deposits-cross-border-payments). Ensemble's pilot launched in November 2025 and initially relied on Hong Kong's dollar-denominated real-time gross settlement system before the around-the-clock capability arrived.

Swift said in July that its blockchain-based ledger was ready for initial use and that it was preparing to pilot tokenized cross-border payments with 17 major banks, including Citi, DBS and HSBC, Cointelegraph reported. Those banks already run domestic or cross-border experiments; the Nov. 16 announcement ties Hong Kong's Ensemble work to that Swift ledger effort rather than launching a live payments corridor.

The HKMA said the international collaboration could support Hong Kong's competitive position in tokenization and that it looks forward to working with Swift and the wider community to advance the city's tokenization ecosystem. The release does not set a start date for production payments, does not disclose which currencies will be used first, and does not publish commercial terms for the banks taking part.

Swift's July update already framed the ledger as a connector for domestic systems that move to new forms of digital value. Pairing that ledger with Ensemble's settlement layer would let a payment instruction leave one jurisdiction as a tokenized deposit claim and settle against tokenized central bank money in Hong Kong, which is the arrangement EnsembleTX tested after its Nov. 2 upgrade, according to Cointelegraph's account of the program. The announcement itself describes exploration and interoperability work rather than a live corridor with completed customer transfers.

Cross-border payments using tokenized deposits still depend on how the two ledgers handle finality, currency conversion and compliance checks between jurisdictions. The Nov. 16 statement names the banks, the infrastructure links and the multi-currency goal; it does not report transfers already settled between the participating institutions, and the pilot remains the venue for those technical questions.
