BlackRock's ether staking fund has recorded 20 consecutive trading days of net inflows totaling $307.72 million, running from July 28 to Sept. 11 without a single day of net redemptions, according to SoSoValue data [reported on Sept. 14 by news.bitcoin.com](https://news.bitcoin.com/finance/blackrock-ether-staking-etf-draws-308m-over-20-days-as-assets-top-1b/). The iShares Staked Ethereum Trust ETF, which trades on Nasdaq under the ticker ETHB, held about $1.05 billion in net assets roughly six months after launch. BlackRock's own [fund page](https://www.blackrock.com/us/individual/products/348532/ishares-staked-ethereum-trust-etf) listed net assets of $1,069,723,957 as of Sept. 14.

Demand accelerated into late August and early September. The fund attracted $42.64 million on Aug. 28 and another $52.91 million on Sept. 2, the report said. Since trading began on Nasdaq on March 12, ETHB has accumulated $830.67 million in cumulative net inflows. On Sept. 9 alone, ETHB drew $22.94 million, the largest single-day inflow of any ether ETF that day, which brought its cumulative total to $798 million at that point, according to ChainCatcher reporting on SoSoValue data [carried by KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/ethereum-spot-etfs-see-34-75m-net-inflow-on-sept-9-blackrock-s-ethb-leads). Total net inflows into spot ether ETFs that day were $34.75 million.

ETHB differs from a standard spot ether fund because it stakes a large share of its holdings. The trust stakes between 70 percent and 95 percent of its ether, the report said. As of Sept. 11, the fund held about 313,789 staked ether worth $802.9 million, or 74.55 percent of assets, with another 107,128 ether worth $274.1 million left unstaked. BlackRock's fund page put the 30-day staking rewards rate at 1.53 percent as of Sept. 14. The trust distributes staking income monthly. Its latest payout covered a Sept. 9 record date at $0.036487 per share, payable Sept. 10, following $0.032499 in August, $0.032059 in July, and $0.015237 in June.

The product terms show how the staking exposure is packaged for brokerage accounts. The trust seeks to reflect the performance of the price of ether plus rewards from staking a portion of its holdings, and it offers that exposure without direct custody of crypto, according to BlackRock. The benchmark is the CME CF Ether-Dollar Reference Rate New York Variant, and the custodian is Coinbase Custody Trust Company. The headline sponsor fee is 0.25 percent, with a waiver to 0.12 percent on the first $2.5 billion of assets for 12 months starting March 12, 2026. BlackRock lists Feb. 18, 2026 as the fund launch date. As of Sept. 14, the trust had 32.72 million shares outstanding, a net asset value of $32.69, and a 30-day average trading volume of 566,753 shares.

Trading in the trust stays close to its asset value. BlackRock's page showed a premium or discount of minus 0.08 percent on Sept. 11 with a 30-day median bid-ask spread of 0.06 percent, alongside a closing price of $32.68 and daily volume of 1,891,356 shares that day. The trust had 32.72 million shares outstanding as of Sept. 14, and each creation basket represented 514.60 ether, worth about $1,307,731. The benchmark level for ether stood at $2,541.26 on Sept. 14. The fee waiver applies only to the first $2.5 billion of assets during the 12 months from March 12, 2026; assets above that line pay the full 0.25 percent, and every investor pays the same weighted-average rate. The trust is not registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940 and is not a commodity pool under the Commodity Exchange Act, BlackRock states.

The staking fund remains far smaller than BlackRock's flagship spot ether product. ETHA, the iShares Ethereum Trust, has accumulated roughly $13 billion in cumulative net inflows and holds about $9.11 billion in net assets, the Sept. 14 report said. On Sept. 14, spot ether ETFs drew $121 million in total net inflows, with ETHA contributing $80.50 million to reach $13.09 billion in cumulative inflows, while Grayscale's Ethereum Mini Trust added $16.23 million to reach $1.93 billion, according to ChainCatcher reporting on SoSoValue data [carried by Gate](https://www.gate.com/news/detail/ethereum-spot-etfs-record-121m-net-inflows-on-september-14-blackrock-etha-24277613). Five days earlier, on Sept. 9, ETHA had added $9.71 million for a cumulative $12.883 billion. Across all spot ether ETFs, total net assets stood at $15.688 billion on Sept. 9, equal to 5.2 percent of ether's market capitalization, with cumulative inflows of $13.203 billion.

The gap between the two BlackRock funds suggests staking exposure is adding to ether demand rather than replacing the plain spot product. The Sept. 14 report noted that investors entering ETHB have so far behaved like long-term allocators rather than short-term traders, pointing to the absence of outflow days across the 20-day run. A spot fund gives holders exposure to the price of ether. A staking fund adds a return stream tied to the network itself, which can appeal to institutions that compare crypto against income-producing assets.

That structure carries constraints that do not apply to unstaked holdings. BlackRock's disclosures state that staked ether moves through activation, exit, and withdrawal queues during which it cannot be sold or transferred, and that the Ethereum protocol limits how much staked ether can enter or leave in each period, so queues can extend for days, weeks, or months when validator demand is high. Ether waiting in the activation queue earns no rewards, and neither does ether in the withdrawal period. Staked ether can also face security breaches, smart contract failures, or validator and custodian failures that could destroy part or all of the staked balance and its rewards, and BlackRock states there is no guarantee the trust receives any staking rewards at all.

The inflow streak does not by itself determine where ether trades next. ETHB is still roughly one-tenth the size of ETHA by assets, and the Sept. 14 report cautioned that macroeconomic conditions, risk appetite, and broader ETF flows will continue to dominate short-term price moves. What the figures do establish is a second channel of institutional demand: alongside the $13 billion that has entered ETHA, more than $830 million has now entered a BlackRock vehicle that locks most of its ether into staking. BlackRock's fund page lists the Sept. 9 distribution of $0.036487 per share, payable Sept. 10, as the trust's latest monthly staking payout.
