The Ethereum Foundation has doubled the size of its internal security team as part of a multi-year effort to prepare the Ethereum protocol for post-quantum cryptography.

The expanded team will focus on researching, prototyping, and auditing quantum-resistant signature schemes, hash-based commitments, and account-abstraction migration paths. While large-scale fault-tolerant quantum computers capable of breaking elliptic-curve cryptography remain years away, core researchers view early protocol readiness as essential given the time required to coordinate network-wide upgrades.

Key workstreams include evaluating lattice-based and hash-based signature alternatives, minimizing calldata and verification overhead for post-quantum proofs, and ensuring backward compatibility for existing wallets and smart contracts.

The hiring expansion reflects a broader industry shift toward long-term cryptographic resilience across Layer 1 networks.
