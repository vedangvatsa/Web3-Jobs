Coinbase is aiming to start offering its own prediction market contracts to U.S. customers in the first half of 2027, Bloomberg reported on Nov. 20, citing a person familiar with the plans. [Decrypt reported](https://decrypt.co/383682/coinbase-targets-in-house-prediction-markets-by-first-half-of-2027-report) that the contracts would be offered to both retail and institutional customers, and [Unchained described](https://unchainedcrypto.com/coinbase-sets-mid-2027-goal-to-write-its-own-event-contracts-bloomberg-reports/) the same target date, also citing Bloomberg.

The plan has not been confirmed by the company. A Coinbase spokesperson told Bloomberg the exchange had "nothing new to announce today" and pointed to October plans to expand prediction markets and improve their economics by building its own clearing and other infrastructure. The spokesperson did not address the timing of an in-house launch. Coinbase has not confirmed the reported timeline, and both accounts of the plan rest on the Bloomberg sourcing rather than a company announcement.

Today the contracts Coinbase users trade come from Kalshi, its partner in rolling out prediction markets across the U.S. Running the contracts in-house would let Coinbase choose which markets it lists and keep more of the revenue from trades.

Coinbase has been laying the groundwork. On Dec. 22, 2025 it agreed to buy prediction markets startup The Clearing Company, and on Sept. 28, 2026 it received approval from the Commodity Futures Trading Commission for its own clearinghouse, Coinbase Clearing LLC. The exchange already runs a regulated derivatives exchange. On the company's third-quarter earnings call on Oct. 29, CEO Brian Armstrong told investors that "we got CFTC approval to list our own prediction market contracts now, which will improve our economics," Unchained reported. He did not give a date. The reported timeline remains unconfirmed by Coinbase.

The business itself has grown fast. Coinbase rolled out prediction markets across the U.S. through a [partnership with Kalshi](https://decrypt.co/383682/coinbase-targets-in-house-prediction-markets-by-first-half-of-2027-report) after unveiling the product alongside stock trading last year. CFO Alesia Haas said on the same day's investor Q&A that prediction markets passed $200 million in annualized run-rate revenue during the third quarter, calling the segment one of the fastest-growing new revenue products. Daily prediction traders rose more than 60% in the period, with revenue growing 82% a quarter on average since launch, the company reported in October. Haas said on that call that "the growth in prediction markets offset the decline in overall spot trading, and we delivered flat revenue quarter over quarter," [Unchained reported](https://unchainedcrypto.com/coinbase-sets-mid-2027-goal-to-write-its-own-event-contracts-bloomberg-reports/). Decrypt quoted her from the same day saying prediction markets are "adding another revenue stream and are less correlated to crypto volatility." On an analyst call, Haas said the new clearinghouse means Coinbase "can launch our own contracts, and we can also then earn better economics" while continuing to offer contracts through partners such as Kalshi. Asked about timing then, she said: "We have not shared a timeline."

The [same report](https://unchainedcrypto.com/coinbase-sets-mid-2027-goal-to-write-its-own-event-contracts-bloomberg-reports/) calculated that $200 million in annualized run-rate revenue works out to about $50 million a quarter, roughly 8% of the $590 million in transaction revenue Coinbase reported for the third quarter, with predictions and derivatives together making up 25% of transaction revenue.

The products have also drawn state legal challenges. Coinbase sued three states over their attempts to regulate prediction markets, and New York sued Coinbase and Gemini over their offerings in April. Earlier in November, a federal appeals court declined to pause state enforcement against Kalshi in New York and Connecticut and against Coinbase in Connecticut while the companies appeal, [according to that report](https://unchainedcrypto.com/coinbase-sets-mid-2027-goal-to-write-its-own-event-contracts-bloomberg-reports/). A Louisiana judge temporarily barred more than 30 prediction market providers, including Coinbase's derivatives arm, from offering event contracts in the state after Attorney General Liz Murrill sued them on Nov. 12, Decrypt reported, citing Forbes; the CFTC has sued Louisiana in federal court to block the order, with a hearing on a longer injunction set for Nov. 30.
