Evernorth Holdings signed a Common Stock Purchase Agreement with CF Principal Investments LLC, an affiliate of Cantor Fitzgerald, on Oct. 23. The deal gives the Nasdaq-listed XRP treasury company the right to sell up to 5,218,935 newly issued Class A shares to Cantor over about 14 months, the company said in an [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2092592/000162828026067061/evernorth-elocx8xk.htm) filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

The [preliminary prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2092592/000162828026067081/xrpn-20261023.htm) filed the same afternoon puts numbers on the arrangement. Selling all 5,218,935 shares at the Oct. 22 closing price of $15.46 would bring in up to about $80.7 million in gross proceeds, and up to $78.3 million after estimated fees and expenses over roughly 14 months. Actual receipts will depend on the share price on each sale day and on how many shares Evernorth chooses to issue. CryptoSlate [described](https://cryptoslate.com/evernorth-takes-a-page-from-michael-saylors-bitcoin-playbook-with-new-80-million-xrp-funding-plan/) the structure as a version of the equity-funded buying program that Strategy built around Bitcoin.

Cantor pays 97 percent of the volume-weighted average price for each block of shares, the 8-K states. Evernorth sends purchase notices between 6 a.m. and 9 a.m. New York time on trading days and decides for itself when to issue shares. Each purchase is capped at 20 percent of the shares traded during that purchase period, and Cantor and its affiliates cannot end up owning more than 4.99 percent of Evernorth's stock.

The facility also carries costs that apply whether or not Evernorth sells anything. The company agreed to pay Cantor an upfront commitment fee of $521,893.50, equal to 1.0 percent of the total commitment multiplied by $10.00 per share, within five business days after the SEC declares the resale registration effective. The fee is fully earned and nonrefundable. The filing allows additional fees tied to the price of the first million shares sold, with the combined total capped at $1 million.

On the use of the money, the prospectus quotes Evernorth as intending to spend proceeds "for general corporate purposes, including the acquisition of XRP and other activities within the XRP ecosystem, as well as working capital." The document adds that the company expects "a substantial portion of any such proceeds will be used to acquire additional XRP" and that it will keep broad discretion over the allocation. In its risk factors, Evernorth [wrote](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2092592/000162828026067081/xrpn-20261023.htm) that it intends to purchase additional XRP and increase its overall holdings.

Evernorth already holds a large XRP position. The prospectus reports that the company held approximately 473 million XRP as of Oct. 9, 2026, and names XRP per share as its primary key performance indicator. The same document lists XRP's price swings among its leading risks, stating that the business is expected to be heavily dependent on the price of XRP. The company completed its stock market listing this month, and Bitcoin.com [noted](https://news.bitcoin.com/featured/evernorth-seeks-to-register-5-2m-shares-as-it-plans-more-xrp-purchases/) that issuing stock to buy more tokens only raises that measure when the shares sell above the value of the XRP behind them, while any issuance dilutes existing holders unless they buy more stock.

CryptoSlate [reported](https://cryptoslate.com/evernorth-takes-a-page-from-michael-saylors-bitcoin-playbook-with-new-80-million-xrp-funding-plan/) after reviewing the filings that Evernorth generally intends to raise additional capital when its stock trades at a premium to the net asset value of its XRP holdings, which lets it acquire more tokens while adding to the asset value behind each share.

There is a second funding route besides the Cantor facility. Bitcoin.com [reported](https://news.bitcoin.com/featured/evernorth-seeks-to-register-5-2m-shares-as-it-plans-more-xrp-purchases/) that up to 9,283,847 shares were issuable through Evernorth's outstanding warrants as of Oct. 22, exercisable at $11.50 each, so a full cash exercise would put about $106.8 million into the company. Warrant holders decide whether to act, so none of that money is committed.

The 8-K lets Evernorth walk away from the facility after it starts, with three trading days' notice and no penalty once the commitment fees are paid. It also lets the company end the agreement and switch to a traditional at-the-market arrangement with Cantor Fitzgerald & Co. as sales agent if it becomes eligible to register shares on Form S-3. Cantor's obligation to buy anything begins only after the SEC declares the resale registration statement effective and a final prospectus is filed.
