Franklin Crypto Trust filed a prospectus supplement with the SEC on Nov. 25 saying its Franklin Crypto Index ETF will buy Hyperliquid's HYPE token on or about Dec. 1, 2026, when CF Benchmarks adds HYPE to the index the fund tracks. The [424B3 supplement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2033807/000207184426001935/draftezpz424b3nov2026.htm) states that HYPE becomes an index constituent effective Dec. 1, joining bitcoin, ether, XRP, Solana, Dogecoin, Cardano and Stellar Lumens.

The fund trades on Cboe under the ticker EZPZ. It tracks the CF Institutional Digital Asset Index, US settlement price, and the filing says the fund will invest in all eight tokens "in approximately the same weights as they represent in the Underlying Index" once the change takes effect. The document is Supplement No. 1, dated Nov. 25, 2026, to a prospectus dated Dec. 1, 2025, filed under registration number 333-281615. The supplement does not state what share of the fund HYPE will represent, and it does not change the fund's fees.

HYPE's entry is a mechanical result of the index rules rather than a separate Franklin decision to back the token. CF Benchmarks [announced on Nov. 16](https://unchainedcrypto.com/index-reshuffle-puts-hyperliquids-hype-in-franklins-ezpz-crypto-etf-on-dec-1/) that HYPE would join the benchmark in a reconstitution effective Dec. 1, and the trust then filed the supplement under the fund's existing listing authority. [Unchained reported](https://unchainedcrypto.com/index-reshuffle-puts-hyperliquids-hype-in-franklins-ezpz-crypto-etf-on-dec-1/) that the fund can hold tokens beyond bitcoin and ether because Cboe BZX Exchange adopted generic listing standards that the SEC approved under file number SR-CboeBZX-2025-104, allowing the fund to hold any token that belongs to its underlying index. The supplement says the index is rebalanced quarterly and other constituents "may be added or removed from time to time" under the index provider's rules.

The filing says the trust's sponsor will keep monitoring the index composition and adjust the fund's holdings to replicate it, subject to those listing standards and the sponsor's declaration of trust. The supplement amends a prospectus dated Dec. 1, 2025.

The timing tracks the benchmark's calendar. The index follows a quarterly rebalancing schedule, so the December change is one of four scheduled windows a year in which constituents can enter or leave. [Cointelegraph noted](https://cointelegraph.com/news/franklin-templeton-hype-crypto-index-etf) that EZPZ currently holds bitcoin, ether, XRP, Solana, Dogecoin, Cardano and Stellar Lumens, the seven tokens that make up the index before the December reconstitution. The supplement's list of eight tokens for Dec. 1 matches that sequence with HYPE appended.

EZPZ held only bitcoin and ether until December 2025, [according to Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/index-reshuffle-puts-hyperliquids-hype-in-franklins-ezpz-crypto-etf-on-dec-1/). A November 2025 filing set out the move into a broader basket on or about Dec. 1, 2025 and added in-kind creations and redemptions alongside the existing cash process, letting large brokers swap tokens directly for fund shares.

The addition lands in a growing field of U.S. Hyperliquid products. [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/franklin-templeton-hype-crypto-index-etf) that three U.S. spot exchange-traded products tracking HYPE have launched since May, from 21Shares, Bitwise and Grayscale, and that they held roughly $266.5 million in assets under management as of Nov. 27, citing Coinperps data. EZPZ reaches the same token through a basket rather than a single-asset structure, holding its seven current constituents until the December reconstitution takes effect.

HYPE is the native token of Hyperliquid, a layer-1 blockchain built around a decentralized exchange for perpetual futures. Cointelegraph put the protocol's total value locked at $7.3 billion, up from $4.13 billion in early January, and cited DefiLlama in reporting $80.3 million of revenue over the previous 30 days. The report noted that the protocol uses part of its trading fee revenue to buy back HYPE on the open market, and that a portion of fees is paid in the token. The supplement describes a fund that holds the index constituents in their benchmark weights, with the sponsor adjusting holdings to track the index at each rebalance.

The EZPZ supplement does not create a standalone HYPE fund. HYPE has had a U.S. fund since May, when 21Shares launched THYP on Nasdaq as a spot Hyperliquid ETF, [Unchained reported](https://unchainedcrypto.com/index-reshuffle-puts-hyperliquids-hype-in-franklins-ezpz-crypto-etf-on-dec-1/).
