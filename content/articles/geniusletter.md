Six senators asked Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on November 17 to extend safe harbor protection under section 3 of the GENIUS Act to stablecoin issuers licensed by states, arguing the department's proposed rule shields some federal applicants but leaves state issuers exposed. The letter was [released by Senator Cynthia Lummis's office on November 18](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/press-releases/lummis-gillibrand-colleagues-urge-treasury-to-give-state-stablecoin-issuers-equal-footing-with-federal-issuers/).

Lummis (R-WY), who chairs the Senate Banking Committee's Digital Assets Subcommittee, and Senator Kirsten Gillibrand (D-NY) led the group. Senators Angela Alsobrooks (D-MD), Kevin Cramer (R-ND), Pete Ricketts (R-NE) and Mark Warner (D-VA) also signed, according to the [press release](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/press-releases/lummis-gillibrand-colleagues-urge-treasury-to-give-state-stablecoin-issuers-equal-footing-with-federal-issuers/).

The letter targets Treasury's proposed rule implementing section 3 of the GENIUS Act, the federal stablecoin law. As drafted, the senators wrote, the rule gives a safe harbor to issuers whose applications are pending with federal regulators as long as the regulator grants a waiver. State issuers get no equivalent protection. [Unchained reported](https://unchainedcrypto.com/senators-say-treasurys-genius-act-rule-leaves-state-stablecoin-issuers-without-cover/) that the senators want coverage for issuers operating in states that will have a qualifying framework in place before January 18, 2028.

Lummis's release said states that have certified their regimes as "substantially similar" to federal law should have a safe harbor from section 3 compliance while the Stablecoin Certification Review Committee, the Treasury-chaired panel, works through those certifications in 2027. Issuers supervised by the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency received a similar safe harbor in the proposed rulemaking. The senators argued that parity with the OCC matters because Congress wrote the law to preserve a choice between state and federal charters.

The senators warned that uncertainty over when state regimes will be certified could push issuers toward a federal license, including one from the OCC, which they called "the opposite of our intent in drafting this legislation." Treasury "must allow issuers to choose from state or federal charters, as contemplated by the GENIUS Act, without the risk of business disruption or uncertainty," they wrote, according to [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/us-senators-genius-act-rules-favor-federal-stablecoin-issuers).

Congress expected Wyoming, New York and Nebraska to count as substantially similar from the start, which is why it gave those states a faster certification route under section 4(c)(7) of the law, the Lummis release [said](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/press-releases/lummis-gillibrand-colleagues-urge-treasury-to-give-state-stablecoin-issuers-equal-footing-with-federal-issuers/). The GENIUS Act generally lets issuers with $10 billion or less in outstanding stablecoins operate under a qualifying state regime, with exemptions available above that threshold. The release also noted that issuers in some states, Wyoming among them, are not required to move to federal supervision when they cross that mark.

Wyoming's role in the letter is not incidental. Lummis's office traced the state's digital asset framework to a Special Purpose Depository charter created in 2019, years before the GENIUS Act existed, and the release said Congress relied on that history when it designed the expedited certification path. "For Treasury to finalize this proposal in a way that reflects Congressional intent, it must allow issuers to choose from state or federal charters, as contemplated by the GENIUS Act, without the risk of business disruption or uncertainty," the senators wrote in the letter itself.

Treasury published the proposal in the Federal Register on August 18, and the comment period closed October 19. The department expects the GENIUS Act to take effect on January 18, 2027. "Parity with the OCC is essential to preserve the dual banking system," Lummis's release said, referring to the arrangement that lets banks choose between a state and a national charter. The request follows a June appeal in which the same group urged Treasury to preserve state participation and clarify the certification process, [Cointelegraph noted](https://cointelegraph.com/news/us-senators-genius-act-rules-favor-federal-stablecoin-issuers).

Not everyone thinks a broader safe harbor changes the calculus. Kevin Wysocki, head of policy at Anchorage Digital, told [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/us-senators-genius-act-rules-favor-federal-stablecoin-issuers) that issuers are choosing federal oversight because they risk having to switch regulators if their stablecoins exceed $10 billion in circulation, though exemptions are available. Treasury has not yet indicated whether it will amend the proposed rule in response to the letter.
