OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Wallet on Friday, a payment feature that lets its AI agents buy products inside ChatGPT using American Express, Mastercard and Visa cards, [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/openai-ai-payments-chatgpt-wallet-visa-mastercard-amex) on Nov. 13. The launch targets ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the United States with eligible U.S.-issued cards, and every purchase currently needs the user's approval.

The flow starts when a customer saves an eligible card, reviews an order and approves the payment with a fingerprint or Face ID check. The card network then creates a payment token containing substitute card details, which the agent submits through the merchant's existing checkout form, [Decrypt reported](https://decrypt.co/383278/openai-chatgpt-wallet-ai-agents-shop-without-crypto) on Nov. 14. The customer's actual card number never reaches the merchant or the agent, OpenAI said, and the buyer keeps the card rewards and dispute protections that apply to the underlying card.

Wallet works through dots, OpenAI's always-on AI assistants that can browse websites and carry out tasks, rather than in ordinary ChatGPT chats, according to Decrypt. The rollout is limited to Pro subscribers aged 18 or older in the United States. OpenAI said it plans to eventually let users authorize purchases within preset spending limits and other restrictions without approving each transaction, though Decrypt reported the company has not announced when that feature will arrive.

Sherri Haymond, Mastercard's global head of digital commercialization, framed the design around the existing trust in card payments. "An agent making a purchase should strengthen, not bypass, the trust built into payments," she said in OpenAI's announcement, as quoted by Decrypt. Payment networks generate a fresh token for each approved purchase, Cointelegraph noted, so agents handle substitute credentials rather than stored card data.

The product differs from OpenAI's earlier checkout work. OpenAI introduced Instant Checkout with Stripe in September 2025, which let people buy from participating merchants inside ChatGPT; Wallet instead lets agents navigate ordinary online stores and fill in standard checkout forms without each business building a special integration, Decrypt reported. Three days before the Wallet launch, OpenAI also introduced business profiles that let companies update product information and connect with potential customers through ChatGPT. The Wallet launch places OpenAI in a growing set of companies building payment systems for AI agents.

OpenAI is entering a crowded field. Google introduced its Universal Commerce Protocol in January to connect shopping agents with retailers and payment providers, Decrypt reported. Google had already introduced its Agent Payments Protocol in 2025, while Visa and Mastercard have launched initiatives to support AI-initiated transactions, according to Cointelegraph. OpenAI said it is working with payment processors and fraud prevention firms so AI purchases can run through existing checkout systems.

On the crypto side, Coinbase launched Agentic Wallets in February, letting software spend USDC and pay for online services within preset limits. The Coinbase product is primarily developer infrastructure rather than a consumer checkout feature, Decrypt reported, and its x402 protocol lets programs pay for data or computing as part of a web request, often in USDC. Circle's Agent Stack, launched in May, includes stablecoin wallets and tools for payments as small as one millionth of a dollar.

Demand for agent-driven transactions remains small. In September, blockchain analytics firm TRM Labs estimated that agents accounted for only 0.6% to 7.5% of filtered x402 commerce by value, using models that cannot conclusively distinguish agents from scripts. Cointelegraph cited an April Juniper Research report projecting global agentic commerce transaction value of $1.5 trillion by 2030, up from an estimated $8 billion in 2026.

OpenAI's terms put responsibility for deliveries and returns on merchants, and users must generally contact their card issuers for billing disputes, according to Decrypt. The company also warned that merchant websites may block shopping agents or flag otherwise legitimate Wallet purchases as fraud.
