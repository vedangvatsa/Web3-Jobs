Hashi, the Bitcoin finance protocol built for Sui, went live on mainnet on November 19 with more than 20 custody, retail and DeFi partners, according to the [Sui Foundation announcement](https://www.sui.io/blog/hashi-mainnet-is-live). The launch opens bitcoin-backed borrowing and managed vaults on Sui while the coins themselves stay on the Bitcoin network.

Hashi keeps deposited BTC on the Bitcoin network and mints hBTC, a 1:1 Bitcoin-backed asset, on Sui. Holders can post that hBTC as collateral for lending, or deposit it into vaults, without wrapping the bitcoin through a single custodian's balance sheet. The Sui Foundation [described the system](https://www.sui.io/blog/hashi-mainnet-is-live) as decentralized by design and secured by validators rather than one entity. That framing matters for a product that issues a claim on bitcoin held elsewhere, because custody and minting logic are the points where wrapped-asset designs have failed in the past.

A [Chainwire release distributed on November 19](https://chainwire.org/2026/11/19/hashi-mainnet-is-live-with-20-plus-partners-bringing-bitcoin-finance-to-sui-at-scale/) named Anchorage Digital, Fluid, HoneyB and Upshift among the institutions and protocols supporting the rollout, with BitGo expected to follow in 2027. The newest additions include Merkl, an onchain incentive platform, and Current, a lending protocol. Blueprint Finance (Concrete), Bluefin, Cetus and Suilend were also listed as DeFi participants building on the collateral.

Fluid's lending market opened alongside the mainnet. Bitcoin holders bring BTC onto Sui as hBTC, then use it as collateral to borrow stablecoins, starting with USDC, with more borrowing pairs planned as the integration expands. "Bitcoin is the largest pool of idle capital in crypto," Fluid founder and CEO Samyak Jain [said in the release](https://chainwire.org/2026/11/19/hashi-mainnet-is-live-with-20-plus-partners-bringing-bitcoin-finance-to-sui-at-scale/). "With Hashi, it can now power deep, capital-efficient credit markets on Fluid."

Upshift vaults went live on the same day, with HoneyB acting as strategy partner on a USDC vault; an hBTC vault is set to follow. Ember Protocol plans to add Hashi vaults later this year, the Sui Foundation [said](https://www.sui.io/blog/hashi-mainnet-is-live). Among the first integrations, Upshift described itself as the stablecoin and BTC yield launch partner for the mainnet, with a USDC vault open for deposits. Upshift's vault tooling includes permissioned vaults with net asset value reporting aimed at institutions, alongside connections to retail exchanges and wallets.

For institutions, Anchorage Digital offers access to hBTC through its Porto self-custody wallet, with BTC held at Anchorage Digital Bank. Nathan McCauley, CEO and co-founder at Anchorage Digital, [said](https://chainwire.org/2026/11/19/hashi-mainnet-is-live-with-20-plus-partners-bringing-bitcoin-finance-to-sui-at-scale/) that "some of the world's largest Bitcoin holders are sitting on enormous amounts of untapped financial potential" and that Hashi gives clients a way to access Bitcoin finance on Sui while their BTC stays in custody at the firm. Cumberland and Wave Digital Assets were named as early participants providing liquidity to strategies on Sui.

HoneyB, a Bitcoin-focused strategy partner developed by Nectar Group and Amber Capital SPC, supplies the strategies behind the first Upshift vaults. Josh Gordon, CEO at Nectar Group, [said in the Sui Foundation post](https://www.sui.io/blog/hashi-mainnet-is-live) that "Bitcoin holders have spent years watching their assets appreciate without putting them to work" and that the firm is bringing income strategies to BTC and stablecoin holders through Sui. Aya Kantorovich, co-CEO and co-founder at Upshift, said the vault layer connects custodians, exchanges and DeFi protocols to professionally managed strategies.

[Crypto Briefing reported](https://cryptobriefing.com/hashi-live-sui-bitcoin-backed-lending/) that custody under the hood runs through multi-party computation combined with 2-of-2 multisig control, so no single party holds the full key and two separate sign-offs are needed to move funds. The same report said deposits require six Bitcoin confirmations before hBTC can be minted, a delay meant to guard against a deposit being reversed after tokens are issued. It also noted there is no HASHI token; governance uses Sui validator stake weight instead of a governance coin.

The mainnet had already been accepting real deposits for several days before the formal announcement. [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/hashi-live-sui-bitcoin-backed-lending/) reported that on November 13 the protocol held 14.43212362 hBTC in circulation, each backed 1:1 by BTC, a reserve worth roughly $994,000 at the time. That figure sits far below the more than $500 million in capital commitments Sui announced with partners in October; the launch materials present those commitments as pledges rather than deposited assets, and the gap between pledged and deposited capital remains the number to watch as the rollout continues.

Tri-party settlement and additional integration choices are scheduled for early 2027, alongside BitGo's institutional access, according to the [Chainwire release](https://chainwire.org/2026/11/19/hashi-mainnet-is-live-with-20-plus-partners-bringing-bitcoin-finance-to-sui-at-scale/). The Sui Foundation also pointed readers to protocol-level documentation on Hashi's design and to a Fenwick analysis concluding that Hashi's deposit and redemption mechanics should not constitute taxable events under U.S. tax law.
