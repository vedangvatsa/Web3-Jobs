The terms "Web3" and "cryptocurrency" are often used interchangeably, but they represent different, albeit deeply connected, concepts. Understanding their relationship is key to grasping the vision of a decentralized internet.

- **[Web3](/what-is-web3)** is the vision for the next era of the internet—a decentralized web built on **[blockchain technology](/how-blockchain-technology-powers-web3)** where users own their data and have direct control over their digital assets.

- **Cryptocurrency** is the digital money that acts as the native economic layer for this new internet.

In short, **cryptocurrency is the essential fuel that makes the Web3 engine run.** It's the mechanism that enables the decentralization, user ownership, and new economic models that define Web3. This guide breaks down the critical connections between the two.

### 1. Cryptocurrency as an Incentive for Decentralization

A public blockchain is a global, decentralized network of computers (nodes) that needs to be maintained and secured. Who pays for this?

- **The Connection:** Cryptocurrencies provide the economic incentive for people around the world to contribute their computing power to secure the network.
  - In **Proof-of-Work** (like Bitcoin), "miners" are rewarded with newly created cryptocurrency (e.g., BTC) for validating transactions.
  - In **Proof-of-Stake** (like Ethereum), "validators" are rewarded with cryptocurrency (e.g., ETH) for staking their own coins as collateral to secure the network.

- **The Impact:** Without a native cryptocurrency to reward participants, it would be nearly impossible to coordinate a large, decentralized network of actors. The cryptocurrency is the payment for securing the blockchain.

### 2. Cryptocurrency as the "Gas" for Transactions

Every action on a blockchain, from a simple payment to a complex **[DeFi](/what-is-defi)** trade, requires a small transaction fee, known as "gas."

- **The Connection:** This gas fee is paid in the blockchain's native cryptocurrency (e.g., ETH on Ethereum). It compensates the validators for the computational effort required to process and record the transaction.
- **The Impact:** Cryptocurrency acts as the "fuel" or "gasoline" for the Web3 world computer. It creates a market for computation, ensuring that the network's resources are allocated efficiently and preventing spam.

### 3. Cryptocurrency as the Foundation of the Ownership Economy

One of the core promises of Web3 is a shift from the "attention economy" of Web2 to an "ownership economy." Cryptocurrency is the asset that represents this ownership.

- **The Connection:**
  - **Fungible Tokens (ERC-20):** A project's governance token allows users to become co-owners of the protocol, with the right to vote on its future.
  - **Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs):** **[NFTs](/what-are-nfts)** use crypto technology to create verifiable, unique digital assets, giving users true ownership of their digital items, from art to in-game items.

- **The Impact:** Cryptocurrency provides the native asset class for this new economy. It allows value to be created, owned, and transferred directly between users without intermediaries, forming the basis of **[DeFi](/decentralized-finance-is-changing-the-crypto-world)** and the creator economy.

### 4. Cryptocurrency as a Bridge to the Global Economy

Web3 applications are global and permissionless. To participate, you need a form of money that is equally global and permissionless.

- **The Connection:** Cryptocurrencies like ETH or stablecoins like USDC can be sent anywhere in the world, 24/7, in seconds, without needing a bank or payment processor.
- **The Impact:** This creates a native, internet-based financial system that is accessible to anyone with a **[crypto wallet](/how-to-choose-a-crypto-wallet)**, unlocking global commerce and financial services for billions of people who are underserved by the traditional banking system. This is particularly transformative for applications like **[cross-border payments](/the-future-of-web3-cross-border-payment-systems-and-jobs)**.

### Conclusion: Two Sides of the Same Coin

Web3 and cryptocurrency are inextricably linked. Web3 provides the vision and the architectural framework for a decentralized internet, while cryptocurrency provides the economic incentives and the liquid value layer that make this vision possible. You cannot have a truly decentralized, user-owned internet without a native, decentralized form of money to power it. They are two sides of the same revolutionary coin, working together to build a more open and equitable digital future.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can you have Web3 without cryptocurrency?
No, not in its true form. The core principles of Web3, such as decentralization and user ownership, are made possible by the economic incentives provided by cryptocurrencies. Without a native crypto asset to reward network participants, a blockchain would need to be centrally controlled.

### 2. What is the difference between a coin and a token?
A "coin" (like BTC or ETH) is the native currency of a **[Layer 1 blockchain](/what-is-a-layer-1-blockchain)** and is used to pay for transaction fees. A "token" (like UNI or AAVE) is built on top of an existing blockchain (most commonly as an ERC-20 token on Ethereum) and is used within a specific dApp.

### 3. Do I need to buy crypto to use Web3 applications?
Yes. To perform any action on a blockchain, you need to pay a "gas" fee in the network's native cryptocurrency. However, on **[Layer 2 networks](/guide-to-layer-2s)**, these fees can be a fraction of a cent, making Web3 highly accessible.

### 4. What is the "ownership economy"?
The ownership economy is a core concept of Web3 where users, creators, and builders are also the owners of the platforms and communities they participate in. This is typically enabled through the distribution of governance tokens or NFTs.

### 5. What is the most important cryptocurrency for Web3?
While Bitcoin was the first cryptocurrency, **Ethereum (ETH)** is the most important for the Web3 application ecosystem. Its introduction of **[smart contracts](/what-are-smart-contracts)** made it a programmable platform, and the vast majority of dApps, DeFi protocols, and NFTs are built on the Ethereum network.
