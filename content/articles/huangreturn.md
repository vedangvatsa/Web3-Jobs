Nvidia's board of directors is not considering bringing back co-founder Jensen Huang as chief executive, a person familiar with the matter [told CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang.html). The person, who spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss internal matters, said the directors were not discussing a return and remained focused on the investigation the company handed to Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz. [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/nvidia-shares-fall-4-as-board-reportedly-rules-out-huang-return) that the chipmaker's shares fell 4.1% on Monday alongside the account, and that ABC News separately confirmed the same no-return position with its own source familiar with the matter.

Huang resigned last week after 33 years leading the company he co-founded. The exit came days after he acknowledged an affair, and news stories alleged a relationship with Alice Huang, an executive at a customer company whom US intelligence officials suspect of contact with Chinese intelligence, as well as relationships with employees at Nvidia. Chief financial officer Colette Kress took over as interim chief executive, and the shares are down 27% from a month ago, according to the same report.

A campaign to reverse the resignation has run in parallel. The petition, organized under the name "Stand with Jensen" and set up after Huang stepped down, had drawn more than 187,000 signatures, according to its organizers, who list Elon Musk, Y Combinator chief executive Garry Tan and Epic Games chief executive Tim Sweeney among supporters; [CNBC put the figure at 180,000](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang.html). Signatures kept climbing through Monday as the board's position circulated, and the two published totals were counted at different points in the day.

President Donald Trump backed the idea of a return on Monday, answering a reporter's question about national security concerns tied to the allegations. "I would certainly not mind seeing that," he said as he departed the White House for a trip to China, adding that "he could return" and that "there's a big group of people that would like him to return." On the security question itself, Trump said the issue "was not put at risk, and our country is now stronger than ever before." 

The sequence of events around the weekend matters for reading the reports. On Friday, a representative for the directors said the company was in the process of selecting a search firm for the chief executive role. Over the following two days the Financial Times reported that Huang had discussed a possible comeback with allies, and a representative for Huang declined to comment on that speculation on Sunday. Brad Gerstner of Altimeter Capital then said Monday that Huang had not talked about a return with him.

Wachtell Lipton's mandate covers whether Huang's conduct put national security or Nvidia's secrets at risk. Kress holds the interim role while the inquiry and the search for a permanent chief executive both continue.

Monday's session reflected the surrounding strain. Shares fell as Bitcoin and US equities faced pressure ahead of Nvidia's quarterly earnings and following Anthropic's initial public offering, adding to nerves around the artificial intelligence sector, Cointelegraph reported. The company's next quarterly earnings report is still ahead of it.


The reports do not specify a timetable for either the investigation or the permanent appointment, and they describe no talks about reinstatement at this stage. A person familiar with the board's thinking was the source for the no-return position in each account, and both outlets stopped short of describing any candidate lineup for the permanent job.

For shareholders the near-term fixed point is the quarterly earnings report, which arrives with Kress serving as interim chief executive and with the chief executive question still open. The two figures in circulation, 187,000 from the organizers and 180,000 in the CNBC check, differ by about 7,000 signatures counted at different moments on the same day.
