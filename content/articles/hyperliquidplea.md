Huaisong Xiang, a former Robinhood software engineer, pleaded guilty on Monday to one count of wire fraud, admitting that he used confidential information about upcoming crypto listings to place trades on the decentralized exchange Hyperliquid before those listings were announced. Xiang, 30, of Jersey City, New Jersey, entered the plea before U.S. District Judge Colleen McMahon in Manhattan and agreed to forfeit $125,523 in proceeds from the scheme, according to [TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/regulation/58827).

The plea resolves the government's case against Xiang roughly two months after prosecutors charged him and another Robinhood engineer. Between 2025 and early 2026, Xiang repeatedly bought perpetual futures tied to tokens on Hyperliquid ahead of Robinhood's public listing announcements, prosecutors said. Trading ahead of at least 11 announcements is attributed to him in the original complaint, [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/robinhood-engineer-guilty-hyperliquid-insider-trades/) reported.

Xiang worked at Robinhood from around 2024 until September 2026, where he was classified internally as a "Coin Aware Individual," a designation that gave him direct access to a private Slack channel carrying information about which tokens the company planned to list and when, the report said. Robinhood's policies prohibited employees in that group from trading the affected tokens on any platform before a listing announcement and for 24 hours afterward.

One of the tokens involved was POPCAT, the memecoin, Crypto Briefing reported. The complaint also described a March 2025 trade and at least 10 further occasions through February 2026 in which Xiang's account opened positions on Hyperliquid before Robinhood told the public that a token would be available, TokenPost reported.

"No matter the trading platform, corporate insiders will be held accountable when they trade based on misappropriated information," U.S. Attorney Jamie McDonald said in a statement quoted in [the report](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/regulation/58827). "Over the course of a year, Xiang repeatedly traded in the derivatives market in advance of Robinhood's public announcements."

Robinhood could make a token available for trading up to an hour before publicly announcing the listing, and listing news could create upward price pressure on the asset, so information about listing timing was commercially valuable to the company, the earlier complaint alleged, according to [TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/regulation/58827). By closing positions in that gap, Xiang captured moves that public traders had no way of anticipating, prosecutors said.

Hyperliquid is a decentralized platform where traders take positions in perpetual futures, derivative contracts that track an asset's price without requiring ownership of the asset itself. Unlike standard futures, perpetuals carry no expiration date, and positions stay open as long as the trader keeps funding them through periodic payments that align the contract price with the spot price, both reports noted.

Xiang did not face charges alone. Prosecutors charged him in September 2026 alongside Hefu Chai, another Robinhood engineer, with commodities fraud and wire fraud connected to the alleged scheme. The complaint alleged that both men belonged to the internal "Coin Aware Individuals" group and had access to the private listing channel. Chai's case remains pending and he has not entered a comparable plea, [the report](https://cryptobriefing.com/robinhood-engineer-guilty-hyperliquid-insider-trades/) said.

Robinhood said at the time of the charges that it had investigated the matter and reported it to law enforcement and regulators. A company spokesperson said the firm "takes market integrity seriously and has zero tolerance for insider trading."

Prosecutors chose to charge wire fraud rather than a securities-specific insider trading count, a route that avoids litigating whether particular crypto tokens qualify as securities, according to Crypto Briefing. Xiang worked on the listing of new digital assets at Robinhood and routinely had access to nonpublic information about whether and when the platform would support additional cryptocurrencies, the complaint alleged.

Xiang agreed to forfeit $125,523 as part of the plea. The wire fraud count carries a maximum sentence of 20 years in prison, though the judge determines the final term. McMahon is scheduled to sentence Xiang on February 25, 2027, TokenPost reported.
