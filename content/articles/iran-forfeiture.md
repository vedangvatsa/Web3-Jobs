Federal prosecutors in Manhattan filed a civil forfeiture complaint on Sept. 14 seeking about $61 million in cryptocurrency allegedly from black-market sales of sanctioned Iranian crude oil and petroleum products. [Iran International reported](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202609151291) the filing on Sept. 15, identifying the US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York as the office bringing the case.

"Today's action demonstrates our determination to deprive the Government of Iran and its terrorist proxies of the illegal money they rely on to threaten the lives and safety of the citizens of the United States and elsewhere," Deputy US Attorney Sean Buckley said. Buckley added that Iran used what he called a network of cryptocurrency actors in China and elsewhere to wash more than $1.5 billion in illicit oil money meant to benefit the Iranian military and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps.

The forfeiture target is Tether's USDT stablecoin, [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/doj-61m-usdt-forfeiture-iranian-oil) on Sept. 15. Prosecutors allege the funds were intended to finance the Iranian government and military parts, including the Revolutionary Guards, and the complaint treats the tokens as proceeds of sanctions evasion and money movement tied to oil deals.

At the center of the complaint are two companies named Blessed Trust and Hexa Whale, [the Straits Times reported](https://www.straitstimes.com/world/united-states/doj-says-millions-in-iran-oil-proceeds-laundered-on-binance) on Sept. 15. Prosecutors said the two used trading accounts on Binance to move oil proceeds to Iran, its agents and proxies, where the money would support military and terrorist acts. Both companies are incorporated in Hong Kong and moved proceeds from oil sold to buyers in China, [according to Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/doj-61m-usdt-forfeiture-iranian-oil).

The broader network handled far more than the amount now targeted. Related addresses received and passed along more than [$1.5 billion](https://cointelegraph.com/news/doj-61m-usdt-forfeiture-iranian-oil), including transfers to money-transfer businesses linked to the Revolutionary Guards, other cryptocurrency addresses and an Iranian exchange. The two entities presented themselves as a wealth management firm and a commodities broker, [the Straits Times reported](https://www.straitstimes.com/world/united-states/doj-says-millions-in-iran-oil-proceeds-laundered-on-binance).

Prosecutors also allege the pair moved tens of millions of dollars through the US financial system. Accounts holding the oil proceeds [sent crypto to an Iranian crypto exchange](https://www.straitstimes.com/world/united-states/doj-says-millions-in-iran-oil-proceeds-laundered-on-binance) and to money transmitters described as fronts for the Revolutionary Guards. Iran's mission to the United Nations did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

## The frozen tokens

The $61 million at issue is a slice of that larger flow. Tether froze about 61.19 million USDT across 10 addresses on the Tron network in 2025, [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/doj-61m-usdt-forfeiture-iranian-oil), citing the complaint. Prosecutors allege those addresses held black-market oil proceeds tied to the Iranian military network.

The complaint describes a specific custody path: a seizure warrant authorizes the FBI to take custody by having Tether destroy the frozen tokens and issue replacements of equal value for transfer to an FBI-controlled hardware wallet. That step would move control of the value from the frozen addresses to government wallets while the case proceeds.

The filing does not establish ownership yet. A civil forfeiture complaint is only an allegation, and the claims have not been proven, [Iran International noted](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202609151291). The United States would obtain permanent ownership only if a court enters a forfeiture judgment in its favor. Cointelegraph said it had contacted Tether for comment and had not received a response by publication.

## Binance response

Prosecutors did not accuse Binance of wrongdoing, [the Straits Times reported](https://www.straitstimes.com/world/united-states/doj-says-millions-in-iran-oil-proceeds-laundered-on-binance). The exchange said it did not permit transactions with sanctioned people, would keep cooperating with law enforcement, and would investigate, restrict or freeze accounts where sanctions or illicit-finance risk appeared. A Binance spokesperson gave [the same account to Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/doj-61m-usdt-forfeiture-iranian-oil): the case was not filed against the exchange and alleges no wrongdoing by it.

Binance has said it removed both named entities before press reports about Iran-linked flows appeared earlier in 2026. Blessed Trust did not immediately respond to a request for comment, and contact details for Hexa Whale could not immediately be found.

The filing follows earlier press scrutiny. In February, the New York Times and the Wall Street Journal reported on an internal Binance review that found more than $1 billion had moved through the platform to Iranian entities with links to terrorist groups. After those reports, Binance said in blog posts that it cooperated with law enforcement officials and that its compliance program was sound.

Binance has faced US enforcement before. In 2023 the Justice Department [charged the company and its co-founder](https://www.straitstimes.com/world/united-states/doj-says-millions-in-iran-oil-proceeds-laundered-on-binance) over anti-money laundering controls, saying terrorist groups, people in sanctioned countries and cybercriminals had used the platform to move hundreds of millions of dollars. Binance paid a $4.3 billion fine to resolve that case, co-founder Changpeng Zhao pleaded guilty to violating banking laws and served four months in prison, and President Donald Trump granted Zhao a pardon in 2025.

## Sanctions backdrop

The forfeiture claim arrives during a wider US pressure campaign on Iranian oil revenue. In August the Treasury Department expanded its Iran sanctions framework to cover the country's digital asset sector, letting US authorities target foreign people and companies operating in or supporting that sector. At the time, Treasury alleged that UAE-based broker Ivan Obukhov had processed more than $100 million in crypto payments since 2023 to help Iranian oil sales for the Quds Force of the Revolutionary Guards, [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/doj-61m-usdt-forfeiture-iranian-oil).

The Justice Department filing is a civil action against the assets rather than a criminal charge against the named companies. That form of case asks a judge to decide whether the property is linked to the alleged acts and should pass to the government. No court ruling on the $61 million had been reported as of Sept. 15, and the complaint materials cited in press coverage did not state a hearing date.

For now, the tokens remain frozen while prosecutors pursue the court order. The complaint asks the court to grant forfeiture of the 61.19 million USDT, with FBI custody through the token destruction and reissue process Tether would carry out. Blessed Trust and Hexa Whale have not publicly responded to the allegations in the coverage reviewed, and Tether had not responded by publication time.
