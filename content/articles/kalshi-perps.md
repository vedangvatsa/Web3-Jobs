Kalshi plans to seek U.S. regulatory approval for roughly 60 perpetual futures tied to individual stocks and exchange-traded funds, including Tesla, Apple, and Nvidia, according to a [Sept. 11 CoinDesk report](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/kalshi-wants-24-7-tesla-and-nvidia-perps-as-wall-street-fights-over-who-regulates-them). CoinDesk reported that the proposed contracts could trade around the clock and, if approved, would be the first regulated U.S. perpetual futures on individual stocks.

The [report](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/kalshi-wants-24-7-tesla-and-nvidia-perps-as-wall-street-fights-over-who-regulates-them) describes a proposal, not a listed market. It does not publish a Kalshi filing, contract rulebook, listing date, complete set of underlyings, reference-price methodology, funding schedule, position limits, or market-halt procedures.

[CoinDesk reported](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/kalshi-wants-24-7-tesla-and-nvidia-perps-as-wall-street-fights-over-who-regulates-them) that Kalshi received CFTC approval in May for a bitcoin perpetual contract, which the agency classified as a futures contract, while warning that the structure might not fit every asset class and that other assets should receive individual review. That reported decision does not determine how a stock-linked perpetual would be regulated.

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission has separately considered continuous trading and perpetuals in energy markets. On July 9, it [stayed CME's self-certified proposal](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9265-26) for 24/7 crude-oil futures while it reviewed consistency with the Commodity Exchange Act and CFTC regulations. On July 23, it [extended a comment period](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9271-26) concerning 24/7 standard futures and perpetuals referencing physically delivered or storable energy commodities. Neither release concerns Kalshi's reported stock or ETF products.

Citadel Securities told the SEC and CFTC that equity-linked perpetuals should remain under SEC oversight, according to [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/kalshi-wants-24-7-tesla-and-nvidia-perps-as-wall-street-fights-over-who-regulates-them). Its position is not an agency decision. The current public record does not establish whether Kalshi has made a submission or which regulator would oversee a particular equity-linked perpetual.
