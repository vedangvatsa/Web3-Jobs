The Commodity Futures Trading Commission ordered a temporary halt to Kalshi prediction markets that let traders bet on words or phrases President Donald Trump might say, and the exchange has until Dec. 11 to respond. In a Nov. 13 order, the regulator stayed Kalshi's "What nicknames will Trump say before 2027?" market and its TRUMPSAY contract series, [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/cftc-kalshi-trump-mention-markets-review). The agency confirmed to the outlet that it had stayed the listing of certain mention markets pending further proceedings, with no final determination yet on whether the contracts comply with federal law.

Kalshi stopped trading in several Trump-related markets late last week following the agency's request, according to a Tuesday CNBC report [relayed by Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/regulation-and-legal/kalshi-axes-trump-mention-markets-cftc-raises-concerns/). The affected contracts covered which nicknames Trump might use before the end of 2026, which subjects he would mention during November, and which countries and corporations he might reference before the month ends. CFTC spokesperson Brooke Nethercott said the agency suspended the certification of certain Kalshi contracts because of concerns about their vulnerability to manipulation, and at least one affected contract had been self-certified by the exchange in Feb. 2025. Kalshi spokesperson Dani Lever confirmed the company was complying with the directive. The halt does not reach every mention market: a contract on Vice President JD Vance's appearance at the New York Times DealBook Summit was still trading on Tuesday.

The order follows a Sept. 22 staff advisory warning that mention markets carry manipulation risks. CFTC staff wrote that people whose actions determine a contract's outcome, or those close to them, could influence the result or exploit advance knowledge of it. [Cointelegraph noted](https://cointelegraph.com/news/cftc-kalshi-trump-mention-markets-review) that Kalshi had listed the nickname market after that advisory without updating its certification to address the concerns.

Kalshi chief executive Tarek Mansour pushed back. "We disagree with this decision," he told Axios on Tuesday, arguing that markets predicting Trump's words have economic value because the president's remarks can move financial markets.

An insider-trading case underlies the scrutiny. Gabriel Perez, a former White House teleprompter operator, used advance knowledge of Trump's prepared speeches to profit from Kalshi contracts, according to the CFTC. The regulator's Aug. 28 enforcement order found that Perez generally had access to the speeches about an hour before delivery and bet on whether specific words would be spoken. He profited on 39 of 43 contracts across 14 mention markets and, on one occasion, changed his position after Trump skipped or deviated from part of a prepared speech. The CFTC ordered Perez to return $107,539 in profits and pay a $65,000 civil penalty; he settled without admitting the agency's findings. [Bitcoin.com News added](https://news.bitcoin.com/regulation-and-legal/kalshi-axes-trump-mention-markets-cftc-raises-concerns/) that the order also banned him from trading on regulated markets for three years. Kalshi's own surveillance system had flagged Perez's trades and referred them to the CFTC in July, [reported at the time](https://cointelegraph.com/news/cftc-kalshi-trump-mention-markets-review).

The category has drawn months of regulatory scrutiny. Mention markets let participants speculate on particular words or phrases appearing in speeches, television appearances and earnings calls, and unlike contracts tied to broader political or economic outcomes, they can be influenced directly by the person speaking or by people with advance knowledge of prepared remarks, [Bitcoin.com News wrote](https://news.bitcoin.com/regulation-and-legal/kalshi-axes-trump-mention-markets-cftc-raises-concerns/). When Kalshi removed the sports mention contracts in August, political and corporate mention markets stayed up, and Trump-related contracts made up a substantial majority of the trading volume in the remaining mention markets examined at that point.

The Trump-mention action is not the first limit Kalshi has accepted on the category. In August the exchange eliminated its sports-related mention markets during a CFTC review of whether such contracts could be manipulated, and it had previously dropped other mention markets at the regulator's request, including sports-related contracts, according to a person familiar with the matter cited in [Cointelegraph's report](https://cointelegraph.com/news/cftc-kalshi-trump-mention-markets-review).

The intervention sits alongside the CFTC's defense of prediction markets against state regulators. Last week the agency and the Department of Justice sued Arkansas to stop state gambling enforcement against Kalshi, arguing that contracts on federally regulated exchanges fall under commodities law. Kalshi also faces a separate CFTC investigation into its communications with regulators about proposed new products, CNBC reported, and the company said it was working to address those concerns.
