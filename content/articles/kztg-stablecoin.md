Kazakhstan has launched the KZTg stablecoin on Telegram. The first issuance of the token took place at the Central Asia Fintech Summit 2026 in Almaty on Sept. 11, [according to](https://kz.kursiv.media/en/2026-09-14/engk-nknk-kazakhstan-launches-tenge-pegged-kztg-stablecoin/) independent financial analyst Andrey Chebotarev, as reported by Kursiv Media. Kursiv published its account on Sept. 14, citing Chebotarev's report on his FINANCEkaz Telegram channel. The launch marks the first stablecoin denominated in a country's fiat currency to operate inside Telegram's mini-app ecosystem in the region.

The project, which Kursiv identifies as TG Coin, is positioned as the first stablecoin denominated in a national currency operating on Telegram in Central Asia, and the first non-U.S.-dollar stablecoin on the TON network. KZTg is pegged 1:1 to the tenge.

Chebotarev's report is the sole published account of the launch so far, and it does not name the company or entity that developed the KZTg token or operates the underlying service beyond Alt Coin LLP's role in the sandbox. No project website, whitepaper, or issuer announcement has been published that Kursiv cites, which leaves the operating details, ownership structure, and reserve arrangements dependent on the analyst's description.

The absence of project documentation matters because the token is explicitly presented as a stored-account product rather than a permissionless asset. In that model, every token is tied to a verified user, and reserve verification is the one variable that would give the public evidence the peg is backed by real tenge rather than by an opaque operator balance. Whether Alt Coin LLP or another entity holds the reserves, how the funds are custodied, and whether the tenge are segregated from the operator's operating funds are all unanswered in the published reporting.

## How the token works

Users can top up their accounts with a bank card or bank transfer at a rate of 1 tenge = 1 KZTg and withdraw funds back into tenge via Telegram. Identity verification is required to use the service. According to Chebotarev, transactions are also screened against sanctions lists and politically exposed person (PEP) lists.

The mechanics place KZTg in the category of stored-account digital money rather than a permissionless stablecoin. The requirement for identity verification and transaction screening means every issuance, transfer, and withdrawal is tied to a verified user. That design matches the regulatory sandbox structure the project operates under, which the National Bank oversees.

## Regulatory sandbox and the May 2026 framework

The project operates within the National Bank's regulatory sandbox, operated by Alt Coin LLP. The National Bank [introduced comprehensive regulation](https://nationalbank.kz/en) of digital asset circulation on May 1, 2026. The new framework specifically covers digital financial assets, which include stablecoins backed by money.

The sandbox arrangement is significant because it gives KZTg a defined legal lane that did not exist before the May framework took effect. Digital assets that fall outside the framework would have no clear issuance or operational basis in Kazakhstan. By operating inside the sandbox, the project is subject to National Bank oversight from the outset rather than seeking retroactive approval after operation.

Kazakhstan's approach differs from the pattern in some other markets where stablecoins launch first and regulators respond later. The May 2026 framework preceded the KZTg issuance by roughly four months, so the National Bank had a stablecoin-capable class of digital financial assets defined before the first token was minted.

## Reserve verification is the open question

The Central Asia Fintech Summit in Almaty drew more than 4,000 participants from the financial and technology sectors, according to Kursiv Media.

Chebotarev said the key issue for the project will be verifying the reserves backing the issued tokens. Regular public audits of those reserves, he said, will be an important indicator of the project's credibility. Because the stablecoin promises a 1:1 redemption to tenge, the operator must hold equivalent fiat reserves. Whether those reserves sit with a commercial bank, the National Bank, or a custody arrangement has not been disclosed.

Public reserve attestation is standard practice for dollar stablecoins issued under frameworks such as MiCA in Europe, where issuers must maintain reserves and report on them. KZTg, operating under Kazakhstan's newer framework, does not yet have a published reserve disclosure or an audit schedule. The absence of a published schedule is not itself a violation, but it leaves the peg's backing in the category of operational variables that will be settled by future disclosures.

## KZTg is separate from the digital tenge

The KZTg launch is separate from Kazakhstan's digital tenge central bank digital currency, which the National Bank [put into circulation on July 18](https://astanatimes.com/2026/07/kazakhstan-to-officially-launch-digital-tenge-on-july-18/). The digital tenge is issued exclusively by the National Bank and carries the same legal status as cash and non-cash money. KZTg, by contrast, operates within a regulatory sandbox and is operated by a private entity under the sandbox framework.

The two assets serve different purposes. The digital tenge is central bank money designed for the broader payments system. KZTg is a privately issued token designed for use within Telegram's mini-app ecosystem. Both are denominated in tenge and pegged to it, but one carries the central bank's full backing while the other depends on the reserves held by its private operator.

## Position as the first national-currency TON stablecoin

The TON network is the blockchain associated with Telegram, which gives KZTg a direct distribution channel through the messaging platform. TON's existing stablecoin volume is dominated by U.S. dollar assets, so a tenge-pegged token extends the network's utility beyond dollar settlements. The "first non-U.S.-dollar stablecoin on TON" claim from the project, if it holds against the network's history, would put KZTg in a niche with limited comparable assets for users to benchmark against.

Whether the token gains traction will depend on demand for tenge-denominated digital transactions inside Telegram, which has no established base in Kazakhstan the way it does in markets where Telegram-based payments are already common. The projected issuance, total supply, and user activation numbers have not been released. What is known is the first issuance occurred and the redemption path back to tenge is operational. Whether the National Bank will expand the sandbox capacity, require additional disclosures, or impose limits on issuance is not specified by the framework documents published so far.
