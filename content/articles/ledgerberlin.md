Hardware wallet maker Ledger said Wednesday it has confirmed tampered devices in Berlin and is investigating two suspected units in Dubai, extending a probe that began with a Southeast Asian reseller in October. Customers who bought a Ledger device in Berlin since January 2026 should contact the company's support team, [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/ledger-warns-berlin-customers-hardware-wallet-investigation).

In an update on its support site, Ledger said its investigation into a hardware wallet tampering campaign had identified several suspicious devices in Berlin. The company also acknowledged reports of tampered devices in Dubai, where two customers recently reported losing a combined $604,000 in cryptocurrency. "Our findings since then show tampered devices outside Southeast Asia. It is the same incident," the company said, [according to Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/383586/ledger-confirms-tampered-bitcoin-wallets-berlin-spreads-beyond-asia).

The incidents trace back to the distribution-channel tampering campaign first investigated in Southeast Asia in October, when Ledger asked authorized reseller CryptoBilis to suspend sales and shipments amid reports of thefts. The investigation began after customers who bought devices from CryptoBilis reported losses, and onchain investigators traced a suspected drain of about $87 million from hundreds of victim wallets across Ethereum, Tron and Bitcoin. Ledger said at the time that all confirmed cases were tied to CryptoBilis, and it later asked resellers in seven Asian markets to pull devices from sale. The company has confirmed one affected Nano Gen5 among the resellers it previously paused, Decrypt reported.

Until this week Ledger had described the problem as a localized incident in Southeast Asia tied to a single reseller. The Wednesday update was the first time the company confirmed tampered devices outside that region, and it is now handling the Berlin and Dubai reports through the same investigation rather than as separate cases.

Every device Ledger has confirmed shows the same kind of physical modification, designed to capture a user's recovery phrase during setup. A recovery phrase, typically a string of 24 words, is the master key to a crypto wallet, and anyone who has it can drain the funds. The company said it found no flaw in its secure chip or operating system and no confirmed case involving a device bought directly on ledger.com.

In Germany, Ledger said a few tampered units were localized to Berlin, and it urged anyone who bought a device there since January 2026 to contact support. In Dubai, the company is working with distribution partners to learn how the two suspected units entered circulation; those devices were bought at a [Virgin Megastore location](https://decrypt.co/383586/ledger-confirms-tampered-bitcoin-wallets-berlin-spreads-beyond-asia), according to multiple reports. The company's Donjon security team is examining the suspected devices, and Ledger has asked resellers to pause sales wherever inspections are ongoing.

Ledger expects more tampered devices to surface as the checks continue, though it said the number remains small overall. Customers worried about a device should stop using it, the company said, and move funds to a new wallet created on a device bought directly from ledger.com without reusing the old recovery phrase or passphrase. A tampered device can capture both strings during setup, so a phrase typed into a suspect unit should be treated as exposed. Ledger also recommends filing a report with local authorities.

For a limited time, Ledger is offering physical inspections, including X-ray analysis, at its factory in Vierzon, France. "We will never ask for your recovery phrase," the company said in a [Wednesday post on X](https://decrypt.co/383586/ledger-confirms-tampered-bitcoin-wallets-berlin-spreads-beyond-asia), and it repeated that users should move holdings only to a device bought directly from its website.

The warning comes as rival manufacturer Trezor said this week that hackers who breached a third-party shipping partner likely took information tied to about 27,000 orders, Decrypt reported. That breach involved shipping records rather than device hardware, and it was Trezor's third such incident in as many months.
