Maharashtra is developing a policy that could place some state-owned assets on blockchain-based tokens, with electricity-transmission infrastructure presented as a possible first use case, according to [a Sept. 11 CoinDesk report](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure). The report says proceeds from any sale of tokens linked to transmission assets could be directed toward new power lines and energy-storage facilities.

The proposal was described by Praveen Pardeshi, chief executive of the Maharashtra Institution for Transformation, or MITRA, during a keynote address at an invite-only Mumbai event hosted by real-estate tokenization firm RealX and MST Blockchain, [CoinDesk reported](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure). Pardeshi is also a chief economic adviser to Maharashtra's chief minister, according to the publication. His comments describe work in progress, rather than an approved transaction, enacted law, or published investment product.

[CoinDesk reported](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure) that Pardeshi said Maharashtra was working toward a policy that would allow the state to bring assets under tokenization. The report does not publish a policy draft, name a specific transmission asset, give a valuation, or state an issuance date. It does not identify an investor-eligibility rule, token price, trading venue, custody arrangement, or legal document that would set out a holder's rights.

The transmission idea sits beside a separate, more narrowly described official initiative concerning land and other immovable property. In a [July 20 statement](https://x.com/Dev_Fadnavis/status/2079237226664542606), Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis said he had chaired a meeting on the draft Maharashtra Digitisation and Exchange of Land Token Asset Act, known as the DELTA Act. He said he directed officials to propose the legislation and form an expert committee that includes representatives of the Securities and Exchange Board of India, the Bombay Stock Exchange, the National Stock Exchange, and other professionals and domain specialists.

That statement confirms that the state is considering a legislative framework for property tokenization. It does not establish the terms of a program for power assets. CoinDesk describes the transmission proposal as a state policy under development, while Fadnavis' statement concerns a proposed law for immovable-property tokens. Neither source says the DELTA Act has been enacted or that it authorizes a sale of transmission-asset tokens.

## Transmission assets were the example

Pardeshi used electricity transmission as the principal example of how the policy could work, according to [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure). Transmission lines generate fee income for carrying power through the grid, but the capital spent to build them remains committed to the assets. He argued that tokenizing part of that infrastructure could give the state new capital while allowing token holders to receive part of the related income.

[CoinDesk reported](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure) that Pardeshi connected the proposal to a grid constraint in Maharashtra. He said the state has surplus solar generation but lacks enough transmission capacity to move electricity to places and times where it is needed. His proposed use of proceeds was direct: more transmission lines and solar-energy storage centers.

The reported mechanism is limited to an idea, not a disclosed security structure. Pardeshi said the state could tokenize part of a transmission asset, mentioning 40% or 50% as possible proportions, and that holders could receive some of the income earned by the Maharashtra transmission company, according to [CoinDesk's account of the speech](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure). The report does not say whether that percentage refers to ownership, revenue-sharing rights, a financing interest, or another legal arrangement. It also does not identify the company by its formal legal name, the assets that could be included, or the accounting treatment for the transaction.

As described in CoinDesk's reporting, tokenization would be the use of blockchain-based digital tokens to represent an interest connected to an existing asset. That label does not by itself determine what a buyer owns, what cash flows are owed, whether tokens can be transferred, or which regulator would supervise an offering. Those points depend on the instrument's legal and contractual terms, none of which were published in the report or in the chief minister's statement.

Pardeshi's explanation centered on redeploying capital rather than selling the whole asset. [CoinDesk quoted him](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure) as saying that tokenization did not mean wholesale privatization and described his position as keeping the underlying assets under state control while broadening participation in infrastructure financing. That is the policy rationale he presented at the event. The report does not include a transaction structure showing how public control, investor claims, governance, and revenue allocation would operate together.

The difference between a share of income and ownership of infrastructure is especially unresolved here. A token can be designed to reflect many different arrangements, including a contractual right to payment, a fractional economic interest, or a record of an interest held through another entity. CoinDesk did not report which arrangement Maharashtra would choose. Readers therefore cannot infer from the word "token" that a holder would own part of a transmission line, have voting rights, receive a fixed payment, or be able to sell the token in a secondary market.

Pardeshi also used a sharp contrast in electricity prices to make the case for more capacity and storage. [CoinDesk reported](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure) that he said distribution companies can pay 16 to 18 rupees per unit during peak demand, while electricity may be available on an exchange at 2 paisa during surplus hours if it cannot be moved or stored in time. Those figures were remarks by Pardeshi reported by CoinDesk; the article does not provide market data, periods, or contract details that would allow an independent comparison of the prices.

His argument was that additional transmission and storage could connect lower-cost surplus power with later demand. It does not establish that tokenization would fund those assets, reduce the stated price gap, or deliver a return to investors. No financing target, construction schedule, capacity addition, expected yield, or public-budget comparison was disclosed.

## A property bill has an official statement

Fadnavis' [July statement](https://x.com/Dev_Fadnavis/status/2079237226664542606) gives the public a clearer record of one part of Maharashtra's tokenization work. He said officials were asked to prepare the DELTA Act to draw value from immovable properties for public benefit. He also said the proposed framework would tokenize immovable properties through blockchain technology, with transactions carried out through tokens linked to property values.

The chief minister described the work as part of Maharashtra's [stated aim](https://x.com/Dev_Fadnavis/status/2079237226664542606) of becoming a $1 trillion economy by 2030 and said new revenue sources would be relevant to that goal. That is a government objective described in his statement, not a forecast confirmed by a legislative or budget document in the sources reviewed for this article.

The [statement](https://x.com/Dev_Fadnavis/status/2079237226664542606) also says Fadnavis directed officials to study overseas laws, regulations, and practices as they prepare the framework. He said the state's goal was to become the first in India to introduce legislation of this type. The wording remains prospective. It reports instructions for drafting and study, not a passed statute, an operating registry, or a functioning token market for property.

An expert committee that includes SEBI, BSE, and NSE representatives may help officials consider regulatory and market questions, but the statement does not say those bodies have approved a state token offering or adopted rules for one. It also does not identify committee members, a timetable, consultation process, draft text, or meeting record. No official policy document was linked from the statement.

The DELTA Act's stated focus also should not be expanded beyond its wording. Fadnavis referred to immovable properties and tokens linked to their values. CoinDesk's reporting on transmission assets refers to a different potential category of state asset and an intended use of proceeds. There may be common technical or policy questions, but the sources do not publish one legal design that covers both.

[CoinDesk reported](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure) that the DELTA Act remained at the drafting stage. The chief minister's statement is consistent with that status: it describes a draft, directions to officials, and a proposed legislative framework. Until a bill is published and passed, its definitions, scope, administrative authority, investor protections, and relationship to existing property records remain unspecified in the cited material.

## No offer or policy terms have been published

The facts reported so far stop well before an investable product. [CoinDesk says](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure) Maharashtra is developing a policy and recounts a senior official's proposed use of tokenized transmission assets. It does not report that the state has issued tokens, opened a public sale, appointed an underwriter, selected a blockchain network, or named a custodian. It also does not report a decision on whether participation would be limited to institutions, open to retail investors, or subject to location, suitability, or other eligibility conditions.

The missing terms are material because they would establish the practical meaning of the proposal. An offering document would need to say what assets or revenues back a token, who holds title, who bears operating and maintenance costs, how revenue is calculated, whether payments have priority over other claims, and whether holders have any redemption or transfer rights. CoinDesk's report contains Pardeshi's policy case, not those terms.

The sources also leave open the treatment of downside risk. Transmission revenue can depend on operating conditions and the rules that govern the power sector. A property-linked token can raise separate questions about valuation, title, record accuracy, and the legal effect of a token record. These are categories of questions that published rules and contracts would need to resolve; they are not answers supplied by the reported proposal.

[Fadnavis said](https://x.com/Dev_Fadnavis/status/2079237226664542606) the proposed property framework would be fully digital and blockchain enabled. The statement does not identify a network, technical operator, data standard, token standard, access model, wallet provider, or process for correcting erroneous records. It also does not explain how a blockchain record would relate to the government property records that establish or evidence rights in immovable property. Those omissions are normal for a public statement about draft work, but they prevent a technical description of the eventual system.

[CoinDesk reported](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure) that RealX and MST Blockchain used the event to introduce REDbox and WHITEbox initiatives intended as localized ecosystems for real-world-asset tokenization standards, with Liminal Custody as a primary partner for wallet and storage infrastructure. The report does not say that Maharashtra selected any of those companies for a government project or that the event's initiatives are part of the state's future policy. Their presence at the event is not evidence of a public contract, official partnership, or approved infrastructure choice.

Pardeshi also cited Express Towers, a commercial building near his office, as an example of a property that had been sold and later tokenized under a REIT structure, [CoinDesk reported](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure). He described it as an arrangement in which the original seller and token-holding investors could benefit from rental income. The account was an illustration in his speech. The report does not present it as the legal template for a Maharashtra infrastructure program, nor does it supply the transaction documents needed to compare the two arrangements.

## The current record remains narrow

The two sources establish related but separate developments. CoinDesk reports that Maharashtra is developing a policy that could use tokenization for state assets, with electricity transmission offered as a possible source of capital for grid expansion and storage. Fadnavis' official statement says the state is drafting a proposed DELTA Act for blockchain-based tokenization of immovable property and has ordered further expert and comparative work.

Neither development has reached the stage of a published offering or enacted statute in the material reviewed here. The official statement does not set policy terms for transmission assets, and the CoinDesk report does not show a final law for property tokenization. The public record therefore supports the existence of exploration and drafting, while leaving the legal structure, funding scale, technology, oversight, investor rights, and implementation timetable to later official documents.

For the transmission proposal, the most concrete reported use of any capital is new lines and solar-energy storage. For the property proposal, the most concrete official action is the direction to draft legislation and assemble expert input. Further claims about token ownership, public participation, revenue distributions, or a launch date would require disclosures that neither [CoinDesk's report](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure) nor [the chief minister's statement](https://x.com/Dev_Fadnavis/status/2079237226664542606) provides.
