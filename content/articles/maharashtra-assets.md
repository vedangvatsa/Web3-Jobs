Maharashtra is developing a policy to tokenize state assets, with electricity-transmission infrastructure identified as a possible use case, according to a [Sept. 11 CoinDesk report](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure). CoinDesk reported that proceeds could help fund new power lines and energy-storage facilities.

The proposal was described by Praveen Pardeshi, chief executive of the Maharashtra Institution for Transformation, at a Mumbai event hosted by RealX and MST Blockchain, [CoinDesk reported](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure). Pardeshi said the state could tokenize 40% or 50% of transmission assets and that holders could receive part of the income earned by Maharashtra's transmission company. The [report](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure) does not publish a policy draft, identify an asset, or set out the legal rights a token would convey.

[CoinDesk reported](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure) Pardeshi's comments that Maharashtra has surplus solar generation but insufficient transmission capacity, and that distribution companies may pay 16 to 18 rupees per unit at peak demand while electricity may be available for 2 paisa during surplus hours. Those figures are his reported remarks, not independently documented market data in the report.

The transmission proposal is separate from the draft Maharashtra Digitisation and Exchange of Land Token Asset Act, or DELTA Act. In a [July 20 statement](https://x.com/Dev_Fadnavis/status/2079237226664542606), Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis said he had directed officials to propose the legislation and form an expert committee including representatives of SEBI, BSE, and NSE. He described a prospective framework for tokenizing immovable property through blockchain technology and said officials would study overseas laws and practices.

Neither [CoinDesk's report](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/09/11/india-s-richest-state-is-exploring-tokenizing-its-own-assets-to-fund-new-infrastructure) nor [Fadnavis' statement](https://x.com/Dev_Fadnavis/status/2079237226664542606) says that a transmission-token offering has been approved or that the DELTA Act has been enacted. The available material does not specify a token network, offering terms, investor eligibility, custody arrangement, or implementation date.
