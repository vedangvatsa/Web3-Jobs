MARA Holdings priced a $185 million debt offering on November 17 to help pay for its $1.5 billion purchase of Long Ridge Energy & Power, a company that runs a 485-megawatt natural gas power plant in Ohio. The notes carry an 8.750% coupon and mature in 2032, [the company said in a Form 8-K filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission](https://ir.mara.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000950142-26-003100/eh260856708_8k.htm).

The subsidiary MARA USA Corporation sold the notes of Long Ridge Energy LLC to qualified institutional buyers under Rule 144A and to certain non-U.S. persons outside the United States under Regulation S, at a price of 99.000% of principal amount plus accrued interest from August 15, 2026, the filing states. The offering is expected to close on November 27, concurrently with the acquisition itself, subject to customary closing conditions.

## A combined $785 million stack

The new notes will sit alongside $600 million of 8.750% senior secured notes that Long Ridge Energy LLC already issued under an indenture dated February 19, 2025, so the combined principal reaches $785 million, [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/mara-long-ridge-debt-offering-bitcoin-sales). Once issued, the new notes will be treated as additional notes under that same indenture, the 8-K says. Annual interest obligations on the stack come to roughly $68.7 million.

Proceeds from the offering, plus cash on hand, will fund the acquisition, repay part of the issuer's outstanding debt and cover fees, commissions and expenses tied to the deal, according to the 8-K. The notes were not registered under the Securities Act or any state securities laws and can be sold in the United States only with registration or an applicable exemption, the filing says; the sale was conducted only through a private offering memorandum. MARA USA signed an equity purchase agreement on April 29, 2026 with Ohio River Partners Holdco LLC, Ohio River Partners Finance LLC and FTAI Infrastructure Inc. for all of the membership interests in Long Ridge Energy & Power LLC, at a base price of about $1.5 billion subject to customary purchase price adjustments, the filing shows. MARA announced the deal the following day as part of its expansion into artificial intelligence and high-performance computing hosting, and the company has said the Long Ridge site could support more than 1 gigawatt of data center capacity. No major AI tenant for the proposed development has been announced.

## Repaying loans with bitcoin sales

The financing follows MARA's November 9 disclosure that it sold 13,597 bitcoin after the end of the third quarter for approximately $1.1 billion. The company used $750 million of that to repay bitcoin-backed loans, which released 17,477 BTC that had been pledged as collateral, Cointelegraph reported. In the same shareholder letter, MARA said it had cut debt by about $1.4 billion, or 37%, since the start of the year.

The company has sold billions of dollars of bitcoin through 2026. In March it sold 15,133 BTC for roughly $1.1 billion and used part of the proceeds to repurchase $1 billion of convertible notes at about a 9% discount, saving roughly $88 million before transaction costs, according to the report. Even with that cleanup, the notes issued for Long Ridge will bring fresh debt onto MARA's consolidated balance sheet when the acquisition closes.

Long Ridge generated about $144 million in annualized adjusted EBITDA, extrapolated from the second half of 2025, per MARA's April 30 acquisition announcement. The roughly $68.7 million in yearly interest on the secured notes would equal nearly half of that figure.

MARA still faces a near-term maturity wall. Holders of approximately $924 million of convertible notes can demand repayment in 2027, its third-quarter 10-Q disclosed, a potential cash requirement as the company finances the AI buildout.

MARA shares traded at $6.57 on the evening of November 18, up 2.34% on the day but down 46% over the prior six months, according to Yahoo Finance data cited in the report.
