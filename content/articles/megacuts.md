MEGA, the organization behind the Ethereum layer-2 network MegaETH, has cut 20% of its workforce after months of restructuring, days after it adopted the new name and set aside $30 million to build products on its own chain. Co-founder Shuyao Kong [disclosed](https://cointelegraph.com/news/megaeth-parent-cuts-20-percent-staff-ai-push) the reduction on Nov. 14, following her Nov. 13 post on X that [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/mega-cuts-20-percent-staff-ai-restructuring) reported the same day.

Kong said the people leaving had worked on the project's blockchain, wallet, ecosystem support and communications teams since MegaETH's earliest days. She did not say how many employees are affected or when the cuts took place. "We've brought together new capabilities and reorganized our teams, a process that has resulted in a 20% reduction in our staff," Kong wrote, adding that these are "people who helped build MEGA from its earliest days."

Founders, early employees and the core engineering team remain committed to the organization, Kong said, describing work that began three years ago with MegaETH. She thanked departing colleagues and [offered](https://crypto.news/mega-cuts-20-percent-staff-ai-restructuring) to introduce them to prospective employers. "We believe cryptography and distributed systems will change how the world produces and accesses purified intelligence," she wrote, and, "We have a lot to prove, and a lot of work ahead."

The staffing cut lands alongside a pivot toward artificial intelligence products. MEGA announced the rebrand on Nov. 10 and committed $30 million from its existing treasury to develop products on MegaETH, [according to](https://crypto.news/mega-cuts-20-percent-staff-ai-restructuring) the report. Cointelegraph noted that Kong did not directly link the layoffs to the new strategy. The organization has not published a headcount, a breakdown by team, or a timeline for the reduction.

Product plans center on an AI inference market. Inference is the step of running a trained model to produce an answer or output. A white paper by MegaETH co-founders Yilong Li and Lei Yang, cited by both outlets, describes a market where users could source inference from competing providers and check which model ran and how customer data was handled. The paper's "Purified Intelligence" approach aims to make inference interchangeable across suppliers that customers might otherwise have little reason to trust, the [report](https://crypto.news/mega-cuts-20-percent-staff-ai-restructuring) said. Kong said a public launch of the inference market is planned for December.

Kong [told](https://crypto.news/mega-cuts-20-percent-staff-ai-restructuring) The Block that details about the marketplace's operators, providers and models are being held until launch, and that all new products are intended to run on MegaETH, with network upgrades planned to support the inference product. The MEGA website still lists MegaETH as its blockchain division and describes MOSS as an account layer that offers one wallet across applications with programmable permissions for people and AI agents, [the same account](https://crypto.news/mega-cuts-20-percent-staff-ai-restructuring) reported.

The shift follows earlier moves away from ecosystem grant work. In July, [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/mega-cuts-20-percent-staff-ai-restructuring) reported that MegaETH was closing its accelerator after two Mega Mafia cohorts supported roughly 20 teams that collectively raised $80 million. Kong said at the time that most of the successful applications backed by the program were no longer being built on MegaETH, and that resources would go toward first-party consumer products. Teams from the program scattered: Global Token Exchange decided to build its own chain, Noise chose Base, and HelloTrade moved toward Monad, while Cap stayed on MegaETH with a multichain strategy. The network had provided technical, management and market-making help without taking equity, governance rights or ownership in participating projects, Kong said.

MegaETH launched its MEGA token on April 30 with a fixed supply of 10 billion, of which 53.3% was allocated to performance-based staking rewards, [per the report](https://crypto.news/mega-cuts-20-percent-staff-ai-restructuring). In May, the foundation activated a stablecoin-funded buyback program tied to income generated by the USDm issuer through April 30. USDm's reserves leaned on BlackRock's tokenized U.S. Treasury fund BUIDL through Securitize, alongside liquid stablecoins held for redemptions, and the foundation said the stablecoin was neither issued nor operated by the foundation or MegaLabs.

Neither outlet reported the absolute number of employees leaving. Cointelegraph said Kong did not disclose when the layoffs occurred, and the organization has not answered questions about the total.
