Megaspeed International issued its first direct statement about Alice Huang on Nov. 15, 2026, saying she left the company effective Aug. 30, 2025 and holds no role today. The Singapore-based cloud firm and Nvidia customer said it would not comment on her "personal and private matters", according to [the Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/16/megaspeed-company-breaks-slience-nvidia-jensen-huang-alice-huang), which reported the statement along with the Associated Press.

The company said it is "aware of recent media reports containing assertions about our business, supply chain and global operations that are not supported by verifiable evidence", and that it has retained external legal counsel to conduct a thorough internal investigation. Megaspeed described itself as a "legitimate and lawful business" that designs its global operational and supply-chain controls to comply with US export requirements and local rules in every market where it operates.

[Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/megaspeed-confirms-alice-huang-departure/) called the release the company's first direct word on Huang's status since she became the focus of an international dispute over her ties to Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang. Corporate records cited by both outlets show she served as managing director while the company restructured out of 7Road International in early 2023, remained a director until November 2023 and then stepped fully away in 2025.

The statement arrives in the middle of competing official accounts, with Washington describing an espionage question and Singapore describing a compliance matter. US intelligence agencies have assessed Huang as a potential intelligence asset for China, an assessment built on the claim that she was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security with two aims: access to Nvidia's AI chip development and influence over US export policy through her relationship with Jensen Huang, Crypto Briefing reported. US officials have alleged she may have been passing commercial information to Beijing, the Guardian reported, citing the New York Times.

Singapore's Ministry of Home Affairs has described a narrower case. Its investigation concerned export and declaration issues, explicitly excluded espionage, and it has said Huang is not a suspect in espionage matters. The ministry said on Thursday that her passport was held from June 2025 until February 2026, that police believed she had given inaccurate information to downplay her role at the company, and that she was released for lack of evidence, issued a stern warning and barred from re-entering Singapore. Taiwan has also barred her from re-entry, the Guardian reported.

The corporate fallout has already reached the top of Nvidia. Jensen Huang acknowledged an affair with Alice Huang in a letter to employees on Nov. 8, 2026 and resigned as chief executive four days later, on Nov. 12, Crypto Briefing reported. Nvidia's board had opened an independent investigation into national security concerns tied to her influence and relationships. The Megaspeed statement landed three days after the resignation.

Nvidia's board investigation is not the only open matter. Singaporean authorities are running a separate probe into Megaspeed over export compliance that follows allegations the company shipped Nvidia hardware to Chinese companies in violation of US embargo rules, allegations that remain unresolved in the material reviewed by Crypto Briefing. A US senator asked the commerce department to investigate whether Megaspeed had diverted Nvidia chips to China, the Guardian reported, and on Friday Malaysia said it was investigating a subsidiary of the company over possible diversion of advanced AI chips to other countries.

Megaspeed has been one of Nvidia's largest foreign customers over the past three years, buying nearly US$5 billion in AI chips and advanced hardware, according to the Guardian. Short seller Culper Research quoted a Megaspeed employee in May as saying Huang was "still our boss" in a report accusing Nvidia of having a China problem.

The Justice Department has not decided whether Alice Huang was a Chinese spy, attorney general Todd Blanche said last week, and President Donald Trump asked about the question said he did not know whether it had been determined. Beijing has rejected the allegations as unsubstantiated, and the Associated Press said it was unable to reach Alice Huang for comment.
