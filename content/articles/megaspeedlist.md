The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security added Megaspeed International and six affiliated companies to the Entity List on Nov. 23, restricting their access to American technology amid an investigation into suspected diversion of advanced AI chips to China. The [final rule](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-23999.pdf), filed Monday afternoon and scheduled for Federal Register publication on Nov. 25, names seven businesses across Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand.

The listed companies are MSFusion Sdn. Bhd., PT Megaspeed International and Speedmatrix Sdn Bhd in Malaysia; Apex Enterprise Solutions (Singapore) Private Limited, Megaspeed International Pte. Ltd. and Swiftdata Pte. Ltd. in Singapore; and Megaspeed (Thailand) Co., Ltd. The rule states the additions were made because of "the significant risk of these entities being or becoming involved in the diversion of advanced computing commodities to unauthorized end uses or end users." Each entry carries a license requirement for all items subject to the Export Administration Regulations, with applications reviewed under a presumption of denial. The Singapore entry records Megaspeed's former name, 7Road International, among its aliases.

Apex Enterprise Solutions is a Singapore-based subsidiary of Alibaba, [The Register reported](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/11/24/us-blacklists-megaspeed-hours-after-jensen-huang-returns-to-nvidia/5311901). The additions were decided by the End-User Review Committee, which is chaired by Commerce and includes representatives of the State, Defense and Energy departments, with Treasury participating when appropriate, according to the rule text.

Megaspeed is the Singapore-based cloud firm at the center of the dispute over Nvidia AI chips that cost Jensen Huang his job as Nvidia chief executive earlier this month. Huang was reinstated on Nov. 23, the same day the blacklist was filed, after 11 days out of the role. The Justice Department now leads a criminal investigation into the company.

The government's interest in Megaspeed predates the recent turmoil at Nvidia. In a joint statement issued the prior week, the Departments of Justice and Commerce said Commerce opened an investigation into Megaspeed in 2024 after spotting suspicious activity while monitoring potential violations of semiconductor export controls, The Register reported. Investigators requested records from Nvidia relating to direct and indirect sales to Megaspeed in May 2025, and Nvidia placed the company on its internal "no sell" list the following month. US investigators, accompanied by Singaporean law enforcement, later visited Megaspeed's premises and interviewed its then-chief executive, Alice Huang, who left the company in August 2025.

Megaspeed has denied any violations of export controls, [Crypto Briefing reported](https://cryptobriefing.com/us-blacklists-megaspeed-over-suspected-nvidia-ai-chip-diversion-to-china/). The same report said Nvidia's own investigation had found no evidence of chip diversion, while the Justice Department's criminal investigation continues. No charges had been announced against the company as of the rule's filing. Megaspeed maintains that its business is lawful and says Alice Huang formally left the company in August 2025; it has also enlisted outside lawyers to conduct an internal investigation.

Under the Entity List rules, US suppliers must obtain licenses to ship covered items to the listed companies, and those licenses are reviewed under a presumption of denial, which in practice makes approvals rare. The rule does not accuse Megaspeed of a completed smuggling crime; the administrative listing rests on the diversion risk assessment, while any criminal exposure stays with the separate Justice Department investigation.

The listing lands as congressional scrutiny of Nvidia's export practices intensifies. Huang is due to sit down with three congressional committees on Dec. 1, with Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter scheduled to face lawmakers the following day, [The Register noted](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/11/24/us-blacklists-megaspeed-hours-after-jensen-huang-returns-to-nvidia/5311901). Nvidia has also established a board-level committee to oversee national security and export control compliance, placing its trade compliance personnel under the supervision of three directors. The Bureau of Industry and Security said in the rule that the seven entities were determined to be acting contrary to the national security or foreign policy interests of the United States.
