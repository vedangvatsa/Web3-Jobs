The Securities and Exchange Commission's Division of Corporation Finance issued a no-action letter to decentralized science platform Molecule AG and its U.S. subsidiary LabsCo, confirming staff will not recommend enforcement action if the company sells utility-focused biopharma Project Tokens without federal securities registration.

The relief, issued by the SEC Office of Chief Counsel on Nov. 24, responds to an incoming request filed by legal counsel Cahill Gordon & Reindel on Nov. 19. The letter marks a rare formal staff accommodation for token sales under Section 5 of the Securities Act of 1933 and Section 12(g) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, providing the emerging decentralized science (DeSci) sector with a concrete regulatory reference point.

Under the framework presented to the regulator, Molecule operates what it terms a Coin-to-Company (C2C) structure. The model draws a strict legal and operational boundary between consumptive software tokens used to steer scientific communities and traditional corporate equity.

Project Tokens issued through the platform serve as membership credentials for research networks and allow holders to access protocol tooling. Token holders can lock their assets to cast non-binding advisory votes on scientific directions, research milestones, and community grant distributions. Crucially, the tokens confer no equity ownership, no debt claim, no dividend rights, and no entitlement to corporate revenue or profits generated from any downstream commercial discoveries.

The SEC staff response emphasizes that the relief rests strictly on the specific facts and representations submitted by Molecule. Under those conditions, if an underlying biotech project subsequently seeks commercial development, patent monetisation, or equity financing, that capital formation must occur through independent corporate offerings under traditional securities exemptions or registration. Holding or locking Project Tokens grants participants no preferential allocation, discounted warrant, or automatic conversion into company shares.

The decision arrives as institutional interest in decentralized science accelerates across biomedical research hubs. Traditional early-stage drug development frequently faces prolonged funding bottlenecks, often dubbed the valley of death, where high-potential laboratory concepts struggle to secure capital before Phase 1 clinical trials. DeSci initiatives aim to bridge that gap by pooling distributed capital, open-sourcing data, and coordinating research agendas through decentralized autonomous organizations.

By confirming that non-equity advisory tokens marketed strictly for consumptive network access do not trigger enforcement action, the SEC staff position offers builders a clearer compliance path for open scientific coordination while maintaining protections against unregistered speculative offerings.
