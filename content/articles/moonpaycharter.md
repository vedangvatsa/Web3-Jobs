MoonPay has applied to the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency for a national trust bank charter that would let the crypto payments firm offer stablecoin issuance and redemption, reserve management and digital asset custody under federal supervision. The company submitted its application on November 17, 2026 to establish MoonPay National Trust Bank, a non-depository institution headquartered in Miami Beach, Florida, according to an [OCC filing reported by Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/moonpay-seeks-us-bank-charter-for-stablecoin-custody-services).

The proposed bank would not accept traditional deposits or issue loans. MoonPay already provides stablecoin issuance infrastructure to businesses, including support for branded digital dollars, and the charter would allow its banking subsidiary to provide those services plus custody under federal oversight. [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/383688/moonpay-applies-for-national-trust-bank-charter-to-grow-custody-and-stablecoin-business) reported that the application says the charter would help the company grow its custody business and expand its stablecoin services, citing Bloomberg's coverage of the filing.

MoonPay confirmed the move on November 19, posting on X that it was "proud to share" that it had applied for a US national bank charter with the OCC. The company was founded in 2019 and is best known for providing on- and off-ramps between fiat currencies and crypto. It has expanded into AI products, including a crypto wallet designed for AI chatbots and purchases through ChatGPT, and acquired the AI trading copilot startup Dawn Labs.

The application arrives as US regulators prepare to implement the GENIUS Act, which takes effect on January 18, 2027 and establishes federal standards for stablecoin issuers, including reserve backing and redemption requirements, [Cointelegraph noted](https://cointelegraph.com/news/moonpay-seeks-us-bank-charter-for-stablecoin-custody-services). A national trust bank can hold and manage assets for clients but cannot take deposits or lend money, a narrower mandate than a full-service bank.

MoonPay joins several crypto and payments companies seeking federal trust bank charters, including Rain, Modern Treasury and Block. Coinbase's charter took effect earlier in November 2026, and Circle received final OCC approval in July, according to [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/moonpay-seeks-us-bank-charter-for-stablecoin-custody-services). [Decrypt added](https://decrypt.co/383688/moonpay-applies-for-national-trust-bank-charter-to-grow-custody-and-stablecoin-business) that Ripple won approval for a trust bank behind its RLUSD stablecoin, Jack Dorsey's Block has applied to custody Bitcoin, and World Liberty Financial won conditional approval for its own trust company in August.

The wave of approvals has drawn pushback. In October, the Independent Community Bankers of America sued the OCC, arguing that the regulator exceeded its authority by allowing non-depository trust banks to conduct activities traditionally associated with commercial banks, [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/moonpay-seeks-us-bank-charter-for-stablecoin-custody-services). A banking group has separately sued over Block's application, and Senator Elizabeth Warren has argued that the OCC's approvals of Coinbase and Ripple were illegal, a claim the crypto industry has disputed, [Decrypt reported](https://decrypt.co/383688/moonpay-applies-for-national-trust-bank-charter-to-grow-custody-and-stablecoin-business).

In April, MoonPay acquired Sodot, a Tel Aviv-based crypto key management firm, to form the basis of MoonPay Institutional, a unit serving banks, asset managers and trading firms. The unit is led by former acting Commodity Futures Trading Commission chair Caroline Pham. The OCC did not immediately respond to Bloomberg's request for comment on the MoonPay application.

The charter would give MoonPay a federal landing point for services it currently offers through state licenses and partner banks. Stablecoin issuance and redemption under a national trust charter would place the activity under the same regulator that has approved Coinbase, Circle and Ripple for similar mandates, and it would put the firm inside the GENIUS Act framework before that law takes effect on January 18, 2027. Neither the company nor the OCC has disclosed a decision timeline for the application.

Community banks have fought the broader charter wave in court. The Independent Community Bankers of America argued in its October lawsuit that non-depository trust banks are being allowed to conduct activities that belong to commercial lenders, and a separate banking group has sued over Block's application. Warren, who is expected to chair the Senate Banking Committee from January, has said the OCC's approvals of Coinbase and Ripple were illegal and has signaled she will examine World Liberty's charter as part of a wider look at Trump-linked crypto ventures, according to [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/383688/moonpay-applies-for-national-trust-bank-charter-to-grow-custody-and-stablecoin-business).

For MoonPay, the filing is a request, not a permission. The OCC has not approved the application, the charter would only take effect if it does, and the non-depository structure means the firm could not fund itself with customer deposits even if it wins.
