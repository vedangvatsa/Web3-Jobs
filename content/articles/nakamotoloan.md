Nakamoto Inc. signed an amendment on Nov. 11 that pushes 60 million USDT of principal on its Kraken loan out to December 2027, leaving none of the 165 million USDT balance scheduled to mature this year, according to an [8-K filing with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1946573/000149315226051437/form8-k.htm). The Bitcoin treasury company, which trades on Nasdaq as NAKA, entered the Second Amendment to its Master Loan Agreement with Payward Interactive, which operates Kraken.

The June 2026 fixed-term loan had 165 million USDT outstanding. The amendment extends 60 million USDT of that principal from Dec. 4, 2026 to Dec. 4, 2027, while the remaining 105 million USDT keeps its June 30, 2027 maturity, the filing states. Nakamoto Holdings Inc. is the borrower under the original agreement dated Dec. 3, 2025. Payward Oceanic Ltd. joined the amendment for two sections covering the derivatives trading arrangement.

Nakamoto announced the extension on Nov. 12, saying the net value of the Bitcoin derivatives positions it holds with Kraken in a pledged trading account now counts toward collateral, subject to limits, with negative values treated as zero. "This amendment extends our runway and makes our collateral position more resilient," chief investment officer Tyler Evans said in the [company statement](https://www.nakamoto.com/updates/nakamoto-extends-maturity-of-kraken-loan). The company said no principal under the loan is scheduled to mature in 2026.

The collateral change cuts both ways. Under the revised terms, Kraken values the derivatives positions daily and counts them only for testing collateral call and liquidation thresholds, sizing collateral calls, and similar calculations, not for returning collateral, the 8-K shows. The intent is to let protective positions such as Bitcoin put options offset a drop in pledged Bitcoin before Nakamoto has to post more collateral or repay part of the loan. Kraken may still require the positions to be closed out after a default or a liquidation trigger.

The timing matters because the debt now sits above the company's stock market value. [crypto.news reported](https://crypto.news/nakamoto-165-million-kraken-loan-bitcoin-collateral/) on Nov. 13 that Nakamoto shares closed the previous day at $5.79, valuing the equity at nearly $104 million against the 165 million USDT owed. The loan originally stood at 210 million USDT when it was arranged in December 2025; a 45 million USDT reduction in June followed Bitcoin and derivatives sales, according to the same report.

Collateral coverage has thinned as Bitcoin traded lower. Nakamoto held 4,283 Bitcoin at Sept. 30, of which 3,030 were pledged to Kraken, crypto.news reported, citing the quarterly filing. Those pledged coins were worth roughly $253.2 million when Bitcoin traded near $83,565 at quarter-end and about $208.5 million at Friday's price near $68,800, which is about 126% of the outstanding principal. The company kept 1,253 unpledged Bitcoin. If collateral falls below the maintenance threshold, Nakamoto must repay part of the loan, pledge more Bitcoin, or both; a drop past the liquidation threshold can let Kraken sell the pledged coins.

Derivatives held at quarter-end give a sense of what the new collateral credit is worth. The company reported put options covering 2,000 Bitcoin with a $55,000 strike expiring Dec. 25, 2026, plus written calls on 2,150 Bitcoin at strikes from $80,000 to $110,000, and a net derivatives liability of about $6.45 million, meaning the positions would have added no positive collateral value under the amended calculation, according to crypto.news. The puts expire before the first extended maturity date.

Nakamoto has been shrinking its treasury to manage the borrowing. It sold 284 Bitcoin in the first quarter of 2026 and roughly 600 more around the June repayment, the report said, and disclosed $19.4 million in cash at Sept. 30 while operating activities used $36.8 million over the first nine months of the year.
