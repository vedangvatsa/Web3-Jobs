New Jersey's Bureau of Securities rescinded its June 2023 summary penalty and cease-and-desist order against Coinbase on Oct. 30, 2026, lifting a ban that had stopped state residents from staking new funds on the exchange for more than three years. The [notice of rescission](https://assets.ctfassets.net/sygt3q11s4a9/UP8DDH65wMBLgeI2RbWRR/90945f6360884dfa75aadcbac57d4f9f/Coinbase_Withdrawal_Order_10_30_26.pdf), signed by Acting Bureau Chief Keith A. Alt, withdraws the order without prejudice and without an adjudication on its merits, and it does not foreclose the bureau from bringing a fresh enforcement action later.

The 2023 order had required Coinbase Global and Coinbase, Inc. to pay civil penalties of $5,000,000 and to stop offering the Coinbase Staking Securities to or from New Jersey unless those securities were registered, covered, or exempt under the state Securities Law. Staking lets customers commit cryptocurrency to help validate transactions and secure a blockchain, with the exchange taking a share of the rewards as a fee. New Jersey's rescission cancels both the penalty and the staking restriction.

Coinbase disclosed the change in its third-quarter filing, stating that the regulator informed the company on Oct. 29 of its intent to rescind the order. The [10-Q filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001679788/000167978826000122/coin-20260930.htm) says the company disputes the state regulators' claims and intends to vigorously defend against them, and that the final outcome of the remaining state matters remains uncertain. The filing does not estimate a potential financial impact while those cases are unresolved. The notice itself cites N.J.S.A. 49:3-67(a), which allows the bureau chief to make, amend and rescind rules, forms and orders needed to carry out the state Securities Law, and it records the original June 6, 2023 order against both Coinbase Global, Inc. and Coinbase, Inc.

Coinbase chief executive Brian Armstrong said New Jersey users would regain staking access "very soon," [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/new-jersey-coinbase-staking). The wording of the Oct. 30 notice leaves the Bureau free to open a new case against Coinbase in the future. Before the rescission, the July 2023 agreement with New Jersey had already kept residents from staking new funds on the platform while the matter ran, matching the terms California, South Carolina and Wisconsin accepted the same month.

New Jersey was one of ten states whose regulators moved against Coinbase's staking services in 2023. The ten-state group, led by California, covered more than 600,000 accounts held by Californians, the [California DFPI release from June 2023](https://dfpi.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/337/2023/06/Coinbase-Securities-Action-PR-June-2023.pdf) says. In July 2023 Coinbase entered agreements with regulators in California, New Jersey, South Carolina and Wisconsin under which customers in those states could no longer stake new funds pending final adjudication, the company's third-quarter filing states. A similar agreement with Maryland followed in October 2023. Alabama, Kentucky, Illinois, South Carolina and Vermont dismissed, vacated, rescinded or withdrew their actions in March and April 2025, the filing states.

California, Maryland and Wisconsin still have open cases against the exchange's staking services, the filing shows. Washington's regulator permits staking, but that state's ongoing case seeks a $4 million fine. Coinbase estimates that California users have missed more than $89 million in staking rewards since 2023, against more than $15 million in New Jersey, the [Cointelegraph report said](https://cointelegraph.com/news/new-jersey-coinbase-staking).

The rescission arrived three days before the Nov. 3 U.S. midterm elections. [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/regulation/new-jersey-rescinds-coinbase-staking-order-clearing-path-for-restart) reported that the state withdrew the penalty and the staking restrictions without prejudice, and that Coinbase says customer access will return "very soon." New Jersey's Democratic governor, Mikie Sherrill, voted for both the CLARITY and GENIUS bills as a House member, and the state's Democratic senator, Cory Booker, helped negotiate market-structure legislation though he opposed advancing CLARITY in September, Cointelegraph reported. Prediction markets favored Democrats to win control of the Senate ahead of the vote, a result that could put Sen. Elizabeth Warren in line to chair the Senate Banking Committee while Sen. Cynthia Lummis, a leading Republican advocate for the industry, is set to retire in January 2027. The same report said Fairshake and its affiliated super PACs were backing 19 Republicans and 14 Democrats in House races, and that the number of members of Congress with an A rating from Stand With Crypto had risen to 332, up from 96 when the group launched in 2023, a figure it attributed to Reuters.
