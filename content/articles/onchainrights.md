Coinbase and Broadridge published a jointly developed open standard on Nov. 16 that gives digital wallets holding tokenized shares a common format for shareholder notices, and the companies submitted the proposal for review as an Ethereum Improvement Proposal. Broadridge announced a supporting data oracle the same morning, designed to help participating firms show, in aggregate, that notices were distributed.

The Onchain Rights Standard gives issuers, custodians and wallet providers a shared framework for communications about dividends, shareholder meetings and other corporate actions, [the companies said in their release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/coinbase-and-broadridge-publish-proposed-standard-for-onchain-shareholder-communications-broadridge-launches-data-oracle-302939893.html). Compatible wallets would then be able to display notices from different issuers using the same format, [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/coinbase-broadridge-tokenized-stock-shareholder-notices), which reduces the need for separate integrations for each issuer. The technical documentation behind the proposal describes an onchain announcement that links a token to a standardized corporate document. A dividend notice could specify the payment date, the terms and any choices available to holders, and a wallet that understands the format could show that notice without a bespoke connection to the issuer.

"Shareholder communications should be interoperable across the platforms investors use," Molly Abraham, general counsel at Coinbase, said in the release. Abraham said the company and Broadridge developed a shared framework that issuers, custodians and wallet providers can evaluate and implement instead of each participant building its own approach. The release says the approach offers issuers a path toward reaching holders of tokenized shares through onchain infrastructure, and that investors need access to information required to exercise shareholder rights.

The standard addresses how notices reach holders, not the rights those holders carry. "The proposed standard is a communications framework; it does not itself create shareholder rights," Mark Nichols, Broadridge's co-president of digital assets, [told Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/coinbase-broadridge-tokenized-stock-shareholder-notices). Nichols said implementation would depend on the structure of each tokenized stock, including whether holders have direct shareholder rights. The release likewise says implementation depends on the applicable share structure, intermediaries and legal requirements.

Doug DeSchutter, president of investor communication solutions at Broadridge, said in the release that governance works regardless of how securities are tokenized and where they are held, and that the company is making trusted governance capabilities available for tokenized securities however those assets are structured or owned. The oracle that launched alongside the standard is meant to give participating firms an aggregate view of distribution across their systems, supporting evidence of delivery where the ecosystem is fragmented.

The companies said they submitted the proposal for review as an EIP and did not name additional adopters or give an implementation timetable. The documentation site for the standard is [onchainrights.org](https://onchainrights.org), which the release points readers to for more information. By publishing the technical foundation openly, the companies invited broader industry adoption. The release was issued from New York at 7:00 a.m. ET, and the oracle arrived as a separate Broadridge service rather than part of the standard itself.

Broadridge launched its onchain shareholder governance platform in April, naming Galaxy as an intended user for its May shareholder meeting, [the Cointelegraph report said](https://cointelegraph.com/news/coinbase-broadridge-tokenized-stock-shareholder-notices). That platform integrates Broadridge's ProxyVote system with digital wallets so investors can receive materials, confirm holdings and submit votes, with voting records kept on an Avalanche-based blockchain. Broadridge's April 6 announcement says the company's tokenized equity capability already processes $8 trillion in tokenized assets per month, and that Galaxy would use the platform for its upcoming annual meeting and shareholder vote in May.

The release frames shareholder communications as the starting point of the collaboration, and says the next phase will explore expanding from onchain proxy voting to other forms of corporate actions. Broadridge says its Distributed Ledger Repo platform settles an average of $345 billion of repo per day, and that its systems process over 8 billion communications annually. The company did not say how many firms have agreed to use the new standard or when a first wallet would display a notice under it, and no issuer or wallet provider beyond the two authors was named as an implementer.
