Papertrade, the HyperEVM perpetual futures protocol that launched on Oct. 10, said on Oct. 19 that its PAPER reward token will remain locked inside its smart contract with no off-contract transfers, no exchange listings and no release date. Holders can stake PAPER for USDC distributions but cannot move it between wallets.

PAPER is minted only for traders who take losses or get liquidated. Profitable traders earn none. The starting rate is 100 PAPER for every $1 lost, a rate that declines as the protocol's liquidity pool grows past $2 million. Supply began at zero with no pre-mine and no team or investor allocation. The protocol offers synthetic perpetual futures on Bitcoin and Ethereum with up to 1,000x.

[Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/papertrade-paper-token-non-transferable/) reported that staking participation sits between 94% and 98% of all PAPER, which is expected when selling is impossible. Stakers receive USDC from protocol fees plus surplus gains above a $5 million soft cap. The protocol has reportedly processed more than $1 trillion in notional volume and generated tens of millions in revenue since launch, though notional figures at 1,000x mostly measure position size rather than deposits.

The transfer ban is a design choice, not a temporary pause. Papertrade argues that a free market in PAPER would let outside strategies attack solvency, so losses can earn tokens and tokens can earn USDC while the secondary market loop stays sealed. [Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/read/why-papertrade-wont-let-paper-trade) reported the same rationale and the non-transferable design.

Aster said it would delist PAPER perpetuals after the transferability decision, according to separate reporting, which removes one of the few external price references that had formed around the asset. Without transfers, PAPER currently has no market price. Early losers hold the most generous mint ratios, and anyone who treated the token as a sellable consolation prize now faces a payout that only arrives as slow USDC streaming while protocol fees keep flowing.

The declining mint rate adds a time dimension to the design. Early participants earned PAPER at the most generous ratio; later losers receive fewer tokens per dollar as the liquidity pool grows. That structure rewards first movers who lost first and leaves later traders with a thinner claim on the same USDC distribution stream.

Notional volume above $1 trillion at up to 1,000x is a poor proxy for deposits. With that much gearing, a small collateral base can generate enormous notional figures. The revenue numbers reported by Crypto Briefing are more informative: tens of millions of dollars in protocol revenue since launch, much of it from trader losses, with a matching amount flowing to PAPER stakers.

Aster's decision to delist PAPER perpetuals after the transferability announcement removes one of the few external price references that had formed around the asset. Without transfers and without a listed perp, PAPER holders have no secondary market path at all. The only exit is the USDC distribution stream, which depends on protocol fees continuing at current levels. If volume fades or traders win more consistently, distributions shrink and there is no sell side to exit into.

The protocol launched Oct. 10 on HyperEVM with synthetic perpetual futures on Bitcoin and Ethereum. The loss-mint design is unusual in that it inverts the usual points or airdrop program: winners get nothing, losers get a claim on future revenue. That structure has produced extremely high staking participation, which is rational when the token cannot move. Whether the design survives contact with a full market cycle, including a stretch where traders profit and fee revenue drops, remains untested.

HyperEVM as the host chain matters because of its connection to the Hyperliquid ecosystem and its EVM compatibility. Papertrade launched Oct. 10 and had processed enormous notional volume within days, which speaks to both risk appetite and the marketing power of a loss-minted reward. The protocol has not published an audit report or a formal security review, which is unusual for a venue handling this much notional.

Stakers receive USDC from protocol fees plus surplus above a $5 million soft cap. That soft cap is the buffer between operating revenue and staker distributions. Whether it is sized correctly against smart contract risk and potential oracle manipulation is untested. The transferability decision is partly an answer to that risk: by keeping PAPER inside the contract, the team removes one class of external attack surface around the token.
