Robinhood Chief Executive Vlad Tenev said companies should not have a general right to approve or block third-party token instruments tied to their shares. In a [September 11 post](https://x.com/vladtenev/status/2098508615183438157), he said an issuer controls the rights attached to its securities, while owners of freely transferable shares should generally be able to decide how they hold and use them.

The statement responds to AMC Entertainment Chief Executive Adam Aron, who [called on Robinhood to stop trading AMC-linked tokens](https://x.com/CEOAdam/status/2095761359388520460). Aron called the product a "fictitious synthetic equity market," said it could affect a company's capital-raising efforts, and said token holders do not own AMC shares or receive the rights that accompany share ownership. He said AMC had asked securities counsel whether it could compel Robinhood to stop and that the company would ask the Securities and Exchange Commission about the issue.

In his [September 11 post](https://x.com/vladtenev/status/2098508615183438157), Tenev distinguished among issuer-issued onchain shares, intermediary tokenization of share ownership, and third-party instruments backed by or linked to shares. He said Robinhood Stock Tokens use the third approach: separately issued instruments backed one-for-one by underlying shares that provide economic exposure without changing an issuer's capitalization table, shareholder record, or rights attached to its shares. Those are Robinhood's descriptions of the product, not a regulatory determination.

Tenev said an issuer should be involved if a product changes rights attached to underlying shares, replaces the official stock ledger, or imposes new obligations on the issuer or its transfer agent. He said a separate instrument that holds or references freely transferable shares without making those changes should not require issuer consent in the same [post](https://x.com/vladtenev/status/2098508615183438157).

In its [June 30, 2025 announcement](https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/robinhood-launches-stock-tokens-reveals-layer-2-blockchain-and-expands-crypto-suite-in-eu-and-us-with-perpetual-futures-and-staking/), Robinhood said eligible EU customers would receive exposure to U.S. equities through more than 200 U.S. stock and ETF tokens, with dividend payments in the app. The company said the tokens would initially be issued on Arbitrum and that eligibility and restrictions vary by region.

Robinhood Chief Legal Officer Dan Gallagher [replied to Aron on September 4](https://x.com/DanGallagherDC/status/2095878611852984451) that the company would not stop offering the product. Tenev [reposted the response](https://x.com/vladtenev/status/2095878993245598134), writing: "We stand behind Stock Tokens."

Neither executive's [post](https://x.com/vladtenev/status/2098508615183438157) announces a negotiated resolution, a product change, or a regulatory decision. Tenev said Robinhood could adapt its model as regulatory guidance develops; [Aron said](https://x.com/CEOAdam/status/2095761359388520460) AMC would seek the SEC's attention and explore whether it could force Robinhood to stop.
