The Securities and Exchange Commission charged eight Colorado-based entities on November 19, 2026 with defrauding US investors out of more than $37 million through fake investment advisers and fake crypto asset trading platforms that the agency said were "simply fronts to steal investor money." The [SEC press release](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-115-sec-eight-entities-used-fake-crypto-asset-trading-platforms-steal-more-37-million-us-investors) says the defendants are likely operated by individuals located overseas.

The complaint, filed in the US District Court for the District of Colorado, names Advisor WeChat Capital, Tech Brokerage Asset Alliance Ltd., HB Wealth Advisors, IdeaCrypto Exchange LLC, Convergent Wealth Advisors Inc., RealSimple Crypto Exchange Inc., Associated Wealth Advisors Ltd., and Essential Crypto Exchange. All eight face charges under Section 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and Rule 10b-5. Four of them, the adviser entities, are additionally charged under Sections 206(1) and 206(2) of the Investment Advisers Act of 1940, and Convergent Wealth Advisors and Essential Crypto Exchange face a further charge under Section 207 of that act, according to the [filing](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-115-sec-eight-entities-used-fake-crypto-asset-trading-platforms-steal-more-37-million-us-investors).

The scheme ran in four adviser-and-platform pairs from August 2024 through May 2026, with the perpetrators abandoning one pair for the next after a few months. [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/sec-charges-eight-colorado-firms-scam), citing the complaint, reported that Advisor WeChat Capital and Tech Brokerage Asset Alliance operated from about August to December 2024; HB Wealth Advisors and IdeaCrypto Exchange from January to July 2025; Convergent Wealth Advisors and RealSimple Crypto Exchange from about July to December 2025; and Associated Wealth Advisors and Essential Crypto Exchange from January to May 2026.

The SEC alleges that social media ads drove victims into group and private chats where impersonators posed as advisers, first discussing US stocks and later steering people toward supposed crypto trading platforms controlled by the defendants. The [complaint](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/sec-charges-eight-colorado-firms-scam) describes fabricated account balances in a companion app, blocked withdrawals unless victims paid extra crypto “fees,” and threats against people who refused. Hashtag Web3 is reporting regulators’ allegations, not endorsing or repeating how-to payment instructions from the scheme.

To look legitimate, the defendants made fake SEC certificates and filed documents with the SEC, FINRA and FinCEN, then pointed skeptical investors to those records. Registering for EDGAR access is ministerial and Forms ADV are not reviewed before publication, so the filings carry no regulatory approval, the complaint states. Convergent Wealth and Essential Crypto also filed false Forms ADV listing regulatory contacts who do not appear to be real and Colorado offices that did not exist. The SEC said a Form D filed by Tech Brokerage Asset Alliance and Forms ADV filed by Convergent Wealth Advisors and Essential Crypto Exchange have been removed from its website.

[Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/383752/fraudsters-fake-crypto-exchanges-steal-37-million-sec) reported that the case targets more than 1,500 investors and reflects a wider "pig butchering" pattern that regulators have traced across borders. The SEC thanked the Hong Kong Securities and Futures Commission, the Monetary Authority of Singapore, the China Securities Regulatory Commission and the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority for their assistance.

Three unnamed investors in the complaint illustrate the losses. One lost nearly $500,000, including money borrowed from a 401(k), a son and a neighbor. Another lost about $850,000, partly by liquidating a 401(k), and a third lost close to $1.6 million, including the investor's house and IRA, [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/sec-charges-eight-colorado-firms-scam) reported from the filing.

The SEC is seeking permanent injunctions, conduct-based injunctions, disgorgement of ill-gotten gains with prejudgment interest, and civil penalties, along with bars keeping the advisers from acting as or being associated with any investment adviser. "The SEC continues to seek out and expose fraudulent investment scams that prey on U.S. investors," said Laura D'Allaird, chief of the agency's Cyber and Emerging Technologies Unit, who urged the public to keep reporting such schemes through the online portal.
