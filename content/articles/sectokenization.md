The SEC's Division of Examinations published its [fiscal 2027 examination priorities](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-109-sec-announces-fiscal-year-2027-examination-priorities) on Friday, Oct. 23, and the document names tokenization in two separate places: among the high-risk areas examiners will weigh at clearing agencies, and again as an emerging trend under Regulation Systems Compliance and Integrity.

The clearing-agency section of [the priorities](https://www.sec.gov/files/2027-exam-priorities.pdf) states that examinations "will focus on high-risk areas, including risk management of defaults, recovery and wind-down, securities lending, operations, and tokenization." The word does not appear anywhere in [the division's 2026 priorities](https://www.sec.gov/files/2026-exam-priorities.pdf), which listed liquidity risk, default management, recovery and wind-down, collateral management and operations among its clearing-agency focus areas instead. Unchained [reported](https://unchainedcrypto.com/sec-examiners-set-sights-on-tokenization-at-clearing-agencies-for-fiscal-2027/) that securities lending and tokenization are the two new entries against that 2026 list. The same section says the division will also prioritize exams for compliance with new rules tied to expanded Treasury clearing, clearing agency governance, recovery and orderly wind-down plans, and straight-through processing by central matching service providers.

Tokenization shows up a second time in the Regulation SCI section, where [the document says](https://www.sec.gov/files/2027-exam-priorities.pdf) exams under the rule "will focus on emerging trends, including SI, tokenization of securities, extended trading hours, and quantum computing." SI is the document's shorthand for super intelligence, a term it introduces in the leadership message: "Registrants are increasingly incorporating super intelligence (SI) into their businesses," using the tools for research, drafting, coding, client communications, portfolio management and compliance monitoring. Examiners will also review how SCI entities manage their reliance on cloud and other third-party vendors, and how they identify vendor systems that qualify as SCI systems or indirect SCI systems.

The accompanying [press release](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-109-sec-announces-fiscal-year-2027-examination-priorities) sorts both items into its own summary of key focus areas, listing "Emerging technologies, including super intelligence and other advanced technologies that may affect market operations, compliance, fraud prevention, and investor protection" and, separately, "Risks associated with evolving market infrastructure, including Treasury clearing reforms, tokenization, and related developments." The rest of that list covers advisers' fiduciary obligations, retail investor protection for complex and illiquid products, private funds, broker-dealer financial responsibility and anti-money laundering, and operational resiliency and cybersecurity. Decrypt [reported](https://decrypt.co/381745/sec-ai-super-intelligence-tokenization-2027-exam-list) that naming super intelligence as an area of heightened scrutiny is new for the division, and that earlier editions framed artificial intelligence as a business tool advisers were adopting rather than as a market-wide risk.

Chairman Paul S. Atkins called examinations "one of the Commission's most important tools for promoting compliance with the federal securities laws and helping protect investors," in the release. Division Director Keith Cassidy [said](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-109-sec-announces-fiscal-year-2027-examination-priorities) the program tracks a fast-moving market: "The markets we oversee continue to evolve rapidly, and the examination program must evolve with them." He added that the division assesses risks continuously so it can "deploy examination resources where they can have the greatest impact while remaining responsive to innovation and market change."

The priorities cover the division's more than 34,000 registrants, a list that includes investment advisers, investment companies, broker-dealers, self-regulatory organizations, clearing agencies, municipal advisors and transfer agents. The release states that the priorities are not exhaustive and that examiners will keep assessing risks through the year, adjusting focus in response to market events, investor concerns, emerging technologies and new products. The release also says the division is working to be more accessible to registrants on how they receive examination observations, and is strengthening its integration with the SEC's policymaking divisions and with fellow regulators.

The 2027 document does not use the words crypto, digital asset, blockchain or stablecoin, a pattern that also held in the 2026 edition. The standalone "Crypto Assets" section covering registrants that offer crypto asset-related services [was still in the 2025 priorities](https://www.sec.gov/files/2025-exam-priorities.pdf). Cointelegraph [wrote](https://cointelegraph.com/news/sec-adds-tokenized-securities-to-2027-examination-priorities) that cryptocurrencies remain absent as a standalone examination priority for the second consecutive year.

The SEC is not the only supervisor putting tokenization on its 2027 agenda. Unchained [reported](https://unchainedcrypto.com/eu-securities-watchdog-puts-ai-and-tokenization-on-supervisors-agenda-for-2027/) in September that the European Securities and Markets Authority had named AI and tokenization a Union strategic supervisory priority, directing national regulators to catalog how firms use both technologies in client-facing products and to run initial checks on the most exposed firms starting in 2027.

The document also states its own limits. It represents the views of the division's staff rather than the Commission, and "has no legal force or effect," creating no new obligations for any person.
