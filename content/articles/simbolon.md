The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) and the Deutsche Bundesbank said on Monday they have tested payment-versus-payment settlement of euro-Singapore dollar trades using wholesale central bank digital currencies running in separate test environments on either side of the world. The exercise, named Project Simbolon, is the first major milestone under a memorandum of understanding the two central banks signed in November 2025 on tokenization and cross-border settlement, [the announcement](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2026/mas-and-deutsche-bundesbank-advance-cross-border-digital-asset-settlement-connectivity) said.

With DBS and Deutsche Bank acting as the commercial banks and Partior as the technology provider, the two central banks settled the euro-SGD foreign exchange transactions across their respective test environments. Payment-versus-payment means the final transfer of one currency happens only if the final transfer of the other currency also happens. Zero-knowledge proof technology allowed each environment to confirm that settlement conditions were met without any direct technical integration between the two central bank ledgers, according to [the press release](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2026/mas-and-deutsche-bundesbank-advance-cross-border-digital-asset-settlement-connectivity).

"Project Simbolon paves the way for eliminating settlement risk in global foreign exchange transactions," Sabine Mauderer, first deputy governor of the Bundesbank, said in the release. "Beyond the technological progress made, this is a decisive step towards strengthening the financial system and efficient and liquid markets."

Leong Sing Chiong, MAS deputy managing director for markets and development, said financial infrastructures will need to interoperate effectively across borders as markets become increasingly tokenised, and that the collaboration will help establish pathways for more efficient cross-border settlement that better supports trade, investment and real economic activity.

Cointelegraph [reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/singapore-german-central-banks-settlement-cbdcs) that the two central banks used wholesale CBDCs to test settlement of euro-SGD trades across their separate digital asset test environments, and that the project aims to make cross-border transfers between Singapore and Germany faster and cheaper while promoting common standards for cross-border payments, foreign exchange and securities transactions tied to tokenized assets.

Beyond the core settlement test, the release said arrangements of this kind could let liquidity be obtained and committed closer to the moment it is needed, producing funding efficiencies for banks that span the two markets.

The memorandum behind the milestone was signed on Nov. 13, 2025, and it commits the two central banks to collaborate on cross-border digital asset settlement. The Deutsche Bundesbank joined the Guardian Policymaker Group in November 2024, placing it inside the policy side of MAS's tokenization program before the pilot began. The pilot settled trades across different currencies and different operating hours as well, the release said, and the partners examined whether comparable connectivity could help institutions on both sides manage funding across time zones, including securities transactions and intraday liquidity management.

MAS published an Insights Note alongside the announcement summarizing the business and technical lessons from the collaboration. A fuller report, titled "Project Simbolon: Cross-Border FX Settlement Connectivity Across Independent Digital Infrastructures," will set out the business use cases, the technical solution, the testing carried out and detailed findings, and will be published on the websites of MAS and the Bundesbank in due course, the release said.

The project name draws on the ancient Greek word "symbolon," a token formed by two matching pieces, an analogy for the cryptographic proofs that verify the two settlement legs across independent central bank ledgers, according to the release. It sits under Project Guardian, the tokenization initiative MAS launched in May 2022.

MAS and the Bundesbank plan to keep working with industry partners on broader applications for interoperable cross-border digital asset settlement, including settlement performance optimization and the legal, regulatory, operational and governance questions tied to wider use. The test comes after the Eurosystem launched Pontes in September, a system that lets financial institutions settle wholesale tokenized asset transactions in central bank money, Cointelegraph noted.
