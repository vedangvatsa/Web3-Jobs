A lone Bitcoin miner solved block 977057 on Nov. 22, collecting a reward of 3.1287 BTC, or about $191,000, from a machine contributing roughly one-millionth of a percent of the network's computing power. The block was found through the Solo CKPool service at about 7:54 a.m. ET, [Bitcoin Magazine reported](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/solo-bitcoin-miner-191000-block-reward).

The payout combines the 3.125 BTC block subsidy with about 0.0037 BTC in transaction fees, and was worth roughly $191,000 at Bitcoin's price when the block landed, according to [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/383818/solo-bitcoin-miner-scores-again-191000-jackpot). The block carried 584 transactions. Its coinbase output paid 3.0661 BTC to one address and 0.0626 BTC, about 2% of the reward, to a second output. That split matches the service's 2% fee and leaves the miner with a little over $186,000, [the report shows](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/solo-bitcoin-miner-191000-block-reward).

CKpool developer Con Kolivas congratulated the miner on X, abbreviating the receiving address as bc1q~zy9j and calling the win the 323rd block found on the service. The winning setup had about 11.1 TH/s of hashrate on a 3.47 TH worker, and "a miner of this size has about a one in 1650 chance of solving a block in a year," Kolivas [wrote](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/solo-bitcoin-miner-191000-block-reward). One in 1,650 works out to roughly 0.06% over a full year.

Solo CKPool lets independent miners point machines at the network and keep the entire reward minus the fee if they find a block, instead of pooling hash power and splitting payouts with an industrial operation. That structure is what makes a win this size possible for a small rig, and it is also why the odds stay steep: the network's three-day average hashrate was about 1.04 zettahashes per second, or roughly 1,043 exahashes, on Monday, [the report says](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/solo-bitcoin-miner-191000-block-reward), citing mempool.space. At 11.1 TH/s, the winning miner supplied about 0.000001% of that total.

It is not the first long shot to land on the service this month. Solo CK has found five blocks since Nov. 6, with the previous one, block 976642, arriving on Nov. 19, followed by blocks on Nov. 11, Nov. 7 and Nov. 6, each paying between 3.13 and 3.15 BTC, according to [the pool's explorer page](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/solo-bitcoin-miner-191000-block-reward). Decrypt [noted](https://decrypt.co/383818/solo-bitcoin-miner-scores-again-191000-jackpot) that solo miners have also taken down rewards worth about $300,000, $225,000, $210,000 and $200,000 over the past year, some using only modest or rented hardware.

The timing sits against a harder mining business. Large operators have been selling holdings and shifting infrastructure toward AI data centers as margins thin, the post-halving subsidy shrinks and Bitcoin's price has fallen well off its highs, [Decrypt reported](https://decrypt.co/383818/solo-bitcoin-miner-scores-again-191000-jackpot). Mining difficulty stands at about 134.8 trillion after the last adjustment cut it by about 9.1%, and the next reset, expected around Nov. 26, is estimated to raise it by about 9.2%, [per the report](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/solo-bitcoin-miner-191000-block-reward).

Solo CK describes itself as a service that lets miners chase full solo blocks without running their own node, since a miner cannot point specialized hardware directly at a Bitcoin Core node, [the service says](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/solo-bitcoin-miner-191000-block-reward). Miners on the pool get nothing unless they find a block, and then keep the full reward minus the 2% fee, which is why a single small machine can run for years with no payout and still occasionally hit. The winning block was tagged to Solo CK on the mempool.space explorer, and its coinbase carries the pool's signature.

The pattern of wins stretches back through the year. Earlier in 2026 a different solo miner won block 943411 on the same service on April 2 for about 3.139 BTC, and in October a FutureBit Apollo miner found block 973262 for 3.154 BTC, [the report adds](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/solo-bitcoin-miner-191000-block-reward). Difficulty resets every 2,016 blocks to keep blocks arriving about every 10 minutes.

The subsidy stays fixed at 3.125 BTC until the next halving, so the dollar value of the win tracks fees and the market price. The same 3.1287 BTC block was worth about $196,000 at Monday's price near $62,700, according to Bitcoin Magazine Pro. Bitcoin had plunged to about $55,100 over the weekend before rebounding to near $64,000 on Monday morning, the outlet reported, so the reward's dollar value moved with it.
