Switzerland's Federal Council approved 57 partner jurisdictions on Nov. 25 for the automatic exchange of information on cryptoassets, setting up cross-border tax data sharing that begins in 2028. The [press release](https://www.admin.ch/en/newnsb/hy3YlIU2OxQn) states that Switzerland will share cryptoasset information with any of those jurisdictions that express interest and comply with the OECD standard.

The approved list covers all European Union member states, the United Kingdom and various G20 countries, the release said. [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/switzerland-clears-57-jurisdictions-crypto-tax-data-sharing) and [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/switzerland-57-countries-crypto-tax) reported the same composition. The Federal Council did not publish the full list of names alongside the announcement, Bitcoin Magazine noted, so which of the 57 states ultimately participate remains open.

Data will not flow automatically to every country on the list. Under the release, an exchange takes place only where a partner jurisdiction wants the arrangement and meets the conditions for compliant implementation of the OECD's Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework, including requirements on confidentiality and data security. Before any transfer, the Federal Council said it will run a targeted assessment of each partner's compliance, using a verification mechanism that Parliament approved on Sept. 28, 2026.

## A delayed start date

The domestic legal basis for the exchange entered into force on Jan. 1, 2026, but the rules do not apply this year. [The State Secretariat for International Finance](https://www.sif.admin.ch/en/framework-for-the-automatic-exchange-of-information-aeoi-on-crypto-assets) said earlier in November that the framework cannot be implemented before Jan. 1, 2027, because the partner list still needed approval and notifications had to go to the OECD. Crypto service providers could begin collecting data as early as Jan. 1, 2027, with the first exchanges between tax authorities in 2028, according to Cointelegraph.

The final roster is smaller than first proposed. [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/switzerland-approves-57-jurisdictions-crypto-tax-sharing/) reported that earlier projections pointed to 74 potential partners, leaving roughly a fifth of that roster out of this cut. A June 2025 proposal listed 74 countries and territories, and the release issued this week did not say which ones were dropped, Bitcoin Magazine reported. Crypto Briefing also noted that the United States has not joined the multilateral agreement; the OECD's Global Forum lists a U.S. commitment to begin exchanges in 2029, as Bitcoin Magazine reported.

The crypto rules plug into an older system. Switzerland introduced the legal framework for exchanging financial account information in 2017 and exchanged data with 113 jurisdictions in 2026, according to Cointelegraph, which cited the State Secretariat for International Finance as saying the crypto framework supplements that network and closes gaps in tax transparency. The SIF had said on Nov. 2 that the Federal Council was expected to approve the partner jurisdictions at the end of November and submit the notifications to the OECD.

The Federal Council paired the crypto decision with two additions for the older exchange of financial account information. Armenia and Rwanda were approved as new partners, with first exchanges due in 2028, and the council signed off on two notifications needed to carry out the exchange. The release said both partner lists drew broad support during public consultations in 2024 and 2025.

## Scope of the reporting

Reporting obligations follow the OECD framework, which requires exchanges, brokers and other covered service providers to report customer identities, tax residency and transaction data to tax authorities. [The European Union](https://cointelegraph.com/news/switzerland-clears-57-jurisdictions-crypto-tax-data-sharing) introduced its own crypto reporting requirements in January, with the first international exchanges scheduled for 2027, and 76 jurisdictions worldwide had committed to the framework as of March, according to the European Commission.

Crypto Briefing reported that the agreement covers tax transparency obligations only, is limited to reportable cryptoasset transactions, and carries no retroactive component, so pre-existing transactions will not be swapped. The first reporting period will cover activity after the system goes live.

Switzerland's decision lands during an active European debate over wallet visibility. Bitcoin Magazine reported that France's Senate rejected a rule on Nov. 24 that would have forced bitcoin holders to declare self-custody wallets, while EU states began collecting crypto transaction data under the bloc's DAC8 directive this year.

Under the framework as described by Bitcoin Magazine, regulated crypto providers in Switzerland will have to collect information on users who are tax residents of activated partner jurisdictions and report it to Swiss tax authorities, which pass it on to those partners once the exchange begins.
