Switzerland's Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) approved a bitcoin-only investment fund for retail investors, clearing the path for the country's first spot bitcoin ETF open beyond qualified purchasers. The decision was published on the regulator's site on Oct. 20.

FINMA said the approval rests on diversification rules under Swiss fund law rather than an exemption limited to professional investors. Until now, Swiss retail access to bitcoin exposure had run through vehicles that held derivatives or restricted the fund to qualified clients. An earlier 2021 structure used that professional-investor path, [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/finma-approves-switzerlands-first-bitcoin-etf-retail) reported. [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com) reported the same decision, noting that FINMA framed the approval as consistent with existing collective investment scheme rules rather than a special waiver.

The issuer has not been named in FINMA's announcement. [Finews](https://www.finews.com) reported that signs point to BIT Asset Management, formerly part of Matrixport Asset Management, but said the identification remains unconfirmed. Other providers have filed applications with the regulator, according to Cointelegraph, without naming them. [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com) also covered the decision, describing it as the first green light for a country-specific retail bitcoin fund of this type in Switzerland.

Retail distribution will still have to satisfy point-of-sale duties under the Swiss Financial Services Act (FinSA), which require suitability and risk assessments when selling complex products to non-professional clients. The fund structure itself must continue to meet Swiss diversification and custody requirements for collective assets. FINMA's notice does not set a listing date on SIX Swiss Exchange and does not name a custodian for the underlying bitcoin.

Switzerland has hosted crypto ETP products for qualified investors since 2018, but retail access to direct spot bitcoin exposure through a domestically approved fund has remained closed until this decision. The approval does not automatically open the door for US-listed spot bitcoin ETFs to distribute into Switzerland, nor does it change how Swiss banks treat crypto custody for private clients. Additional applications remain pending with FINMA, and the regulator has not signaled how quickly further retail bitcoin funds will clear.

The approval does not convert Swiss-listed crypto ETP products into retail ETFs by default. Most existing Swiss crypto ETPs are issued as debt securities under the Swiss Debt Security regime or as collective assets limited to qualified investors. The newly approved fund is structured as a collective investment scheme under the Federal Act on Collective Investment Schemes and was cleared under the diversification and investment rules that apply to retail-capable vehicles. That distinction is why FINMA could approve a retail bitcoin fund without opening a general waiver for all crypto products.

Custody of the underlying bitcoin remains a separate regulatory question under Swiss financial market infrastructure law. FINMA's notice does not name a qualified custodian, and Swiss banks have been cautious about offering bitcoin custody to retail clients without clear capital and operational guidance. Any SIX Swiss Exchange listing would still need exchange approval, a prospectus or listing document, and ongoing disclosure arrangements. None of those steps has been announced.

European peers have moved in different directions. Germany's BaFin has approved spot bitcoin funds for retail distribution under the KAGB, while other EU jurisdictions still route retail crypto exposure through MiCA-compliant crypto-asset service providers rather than UCITS funds. Switzerland's path outside the EU framework lets FINMA act under national fund rules without waiting for MiCA product categories to catch up. Additional Swiss applications for retail bitcoin vehicles are pending, according to Cointelegraph, but the regulator has not published a queue or timeline.

BIT Asset Management, if confirmed as the issuer, would be extending a product line that already serves qualified and professional channels. Matrixport's asset management arm has distributed crypto ETP products in Europe and Asia. A retail Swiss fund would need different marketing materials, risk disclosures and distribution partners than the institutional products it spun out of. FINMA's approval letter does not name marketing partners or set a minimum investment.

Retail bitcoin funds in other markets have faced secondary questions about custody insurance, staking exclusions and creation-redemption mechanics. A Swiss fund holding spot bitcoin directly must arrange institutional-grade custody and publish how creations settle. None of those operational details appear in FINMA's public decision summary. The regulator's approach so far has been to approve under existing fund law and leave commercial terms to the issuer's prospectus and the exchange's listing rules.
