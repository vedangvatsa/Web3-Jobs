Thailand's Securities and Exchange Commission issued 11 notifications on Oct. 8 setting the framework for locally listed crypto ETFs, with the rules taking effect Oct. 16. Bitcoin and ether are the only eligible assets at launch, and every fund must list and trade exclusively on the Stock Exchange of Thailand.

The package, numbered 216/2026, covers fund formation, delegation of digital-asset management by asset managers, and which custodians and operators may act as mutual-fund supervisors, according to a mirror of the [SEC release](https://www.pubt.io/view/97F806AE0C70A0D5C3DE243398D3DEB111DAFCD2). The regulator ran public hearings on the principles in April and May and on the draft notifications in August and September, with most respondents in support.

Each ETF must be passive, track a single crypto asset, and keep average net exposure to that asset at no less than 80 percent of net asset value across each accounting year. Funds must trade only on the Thai exchange, [as reported Oct. 9](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/10/09/thailand-opens-door-to-locally-listed-bitcoin-and-ether-etfs). Future assets beyond bitcoin and ether will be judged on liquidity, broad market acceptance, network security and investor protection, the [release says](https://www.pubt.io/view/97F806AE0C70A0D5C3DE243398D3DEB111DAFCD2).

## Custody and investor limits

Coins must sit with digital-asset custodians regulated by the Thai SEC. A supervisor may appoint a sub-custodian, but digital-asset custody itself must stay with a licensed custodian, and the SEC may later permit qualified foreign custodians where appropriate, [as reported Oct. 9](https://cointelegraph.com/news/thailand-finalizes-rules-paving-way-for-bitcoin-ether-etfs). Asset managers may outsource digital-asset investment management only to licensed digital-asset fund managers.

Retail protection runs through education and borrowing limits. Investors must receive risk information and confirm they understand it before trading, and brokers cannot offer margin loans on the products, which mirrors an existing ban. Securities firms must stress suitable position sizes, warn against concentration, and weigh each client's risk tolerance, according to the [release](https://www.pubt.io/view/97F806AE0C70A0D5C3DE243398D3DEB111DAFCD2).

Thai mutual funds and private funds may now buy the local products within existing investment limits. Until now they could reach crypto ETFs only through listings abroad, a Yahoo syndication of BeInCrypto's [Oct. 9 report](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/thailand-opens-door-crypto-etfs-063411772.html) noted.

New supervisors face a narrow license. Digital-asset custodians and other qualified operators may apply for registration under section 121 of the Securities and Exchange Act, but only to supervise crypto ETFs, and they must meet and maintain financial, personnel and systems requirements, the [release says](https://www.pubt.io/view/97F806AE0C70A0D5C3DE243398D3DEB111DAFCD2). Crypto Briefing's [Oct. 8 account](https://cryptobriefing.com/thailand-sec-finalizes-bitcoin-ether-etf-rules/) confirms the supervisor track as part of the 11-notification framework.

The rules follow two consultation rounds. The SEC heard views on the principles in April and May and on the draft notifications in August and September, with most respondents supporting the framework. That history leaves open how fast issuers move, since the regulator names no application queue or approval count.

## The foreign-product restriction

The framework shuts the side door at the same time. In the initial phase the SEC will not permit depositary receipts or other products referencing foreign crypto ETFs, and Thai brokers may not arrange foreign crypto ETF purchases for clients outside institutions and ultra-high-net-worth investors. CoinDesk framed this as retail investors remaining barred from indirect foreign access, in its [Oct. 9 coverage](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/10/09/thailand-opens-door-to-locally-listed-bitcoin-and-ether-etfs).

"For many investors, particularly those who have been cautious about opening an exchange account or managing a wallet, an ETF through their existing brokerage account removes a real barrier," Binance Thailand CEO Nirun Fuwattananukul said in an [Oct. 9 report](https://cointelegraph.com/news/thailand-finalizes-rules-paving-way-for-bitcoin-ether-etfs). He added that drawing real investment will depend on competitive fees, tight tracking of the underlying asset, and investor education.

No fund has been approved yet. Issuers still need fund registration, product approval, a SET listing and custody arrangements before anything trades, [as noted Oct. 9](https://cointelegraph.com/news/thailand-finalizes-rules-paving-way-for-bitcoin-ether-etfs). CoinDesk's version ran under Olivier Acuna and Jamie Crawley's bylines, Cointelegraph's under Felix Ng and Yohan Yun, Crypto Briefing's under Vivian Nguyen, and the BeInCrypto original under Harsh Notariya. The Block's take on the same framework could not be opened directly. Crypto Briefing's [Oct. 8 account](https://cryptobriefing.com/thailand-sec-finalizes-bitcoin-ether-etf-rules/) likewise describes a gate opening Oct. 16 rather than a first trading day. The release names no fee caps, asset thresholds in baht, or launch timetable beyond the effective date.
