In the Web3 job market, a traditional resume often falls short. Hiring managers are looking for verifiable proof of your skills and a genuine passion for the decentralized ecosystem. Your resume needs to be a bridge between your traditional experience and your crypto-native "proof of work."

### Key Sections for a Web3 Resume

1.  **Header with Web3 Links:**
    *   Include your name, title, and contact info.
    *   **Crucially, add hyperlinked icons for your:** Portfolio/Website, GitHub (for technical roles), Twitter, and your ENS Address (e.g., `yourname.eth`).

2.  **Web3 Summary:**
    *   Replace a generic objective with a 2-3 sentence narrative about your "rabbit hole" story and what excites you about Web3.

3.  **Web3 Contributions & Portfolio (The Star of the Show):**
    *   This section should come *before* your traditional work experience.
    *   Use bullet points to list your public contributions: DAO participation, personal projects on GitHub, articles on Mirror, Dune dashboards, etc. **Link everything.**

4.  **Professional Experience (Web3-Framed):**
    *   Reframe your Web2 accomplishments using the language of Web3. "Managed a team" becomes "Led a remote-first, cross-functional squad."

5.  **Skills:**
    *   Create a dedicated "Web3/Blockchain" subsection to list skills like Solidity, Foundry, Ethers.js, Tokenomics, and DAO Governance.

By building your resume around verifiable proof of work and tailoring your language to the industry's culture, you show that you're a true participant, not just a spectator.
