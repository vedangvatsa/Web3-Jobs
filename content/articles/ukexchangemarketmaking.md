United Kingdom financial regulators have moved to prohibit centralized cryptocurrency exchanges from operating proprietary market-making desks on their own trading platforms.

Under the new market-integrity framework, trading venues must separate exchange matching operations from proprietary liquidity provision to eliminate conflicts of interest, front-running risks, and artificial volume inflation. Exchanges operating in the UK will be required to rely on independent, third-party liquidity providers rather than internal trading arms.

For years, critics and institutional investors have raised concerns that vertically integrated crypto exchanges—which simultaneously act as venue operator, custodian, broker, and market maker—face inherent conflicts when their internal desks trade against retail and institutional clients. Regulators noted that proprietary desks with privileged visibility into order flow or latency advantages can undermine fair price discovery.

The policy aligns digital asset trading venues more closely with traditional equities and derivatives exchanges, where venue operators are strictly barred from trading against their own customers. Under the UK rules, exchanges must establish clear legal and operational separation between order-matching infrastructure and any affiliated trading entities.

Compliance teams at affected platforms will need to restructure affiliated market-making entities, overhaul internal surveillance systems, or ring-fence order-book access ahead of enforcement deadlines. Industry analysts expect the shift to benefit independent quantitative market makers while raising compliance standards for exchanges seeking to maintain UK market access.
