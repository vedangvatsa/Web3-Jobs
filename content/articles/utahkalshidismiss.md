Utah's attorney general filed a motion Monday to dismiss the 14 criminal charges against Kalshi and its co-founders, after the prediction market blocked users in the state from trading. The [Office of the Utah Attorney General](https://attorneygeneral.utah.gov/utah-dismiss-kalshi-criminal-case/) said the motion, filed in Third District Court, asks that the case be dropped without prejudice, which leaves the state free to refile every count if Kalshi returns to Utah.

The charges had been filed on Oct. 28 against KalshiEX, Kalshi Trading, chief executive Tarek Mansour and chief operating officer Luana Lopes Lara, [Unchained reported](https://unchainedcrypto.com/utah-asks-court-to-toss-kalshi-criminal-case-but-the-charges-could-come-back/). The state opened its criminal investigation in September, alleging the company was offering illegal gambling to Utah residents, and the dismissal request came 26 days after the counts landed.

Kalshi stopped new trades for its more than 177,000 Utah users on Monday, leaving those accounts able only to close positions and withdraw funds, according to [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/utah-asks-court-to-toss-kalshi-criminal-case-but-the-charges-could-come-back/). Spokesperson Jacki McGavick said the company paused because of "the adverse final judgment in federal court, and the federal court not yet having granted the CFTC's emergency request to enjoin Utah's enforcement actions," [KUTV reported](https://kutv.com/news/local/utah-moves-to-drop-kalshi-gambling-case-after-company-blocks-state-users). She said Kalshi "will continue to fight this in court" and looks forward to returning once its legal position is vindicated.

After the charges were filed, the attorney general's office said, Kalshi agreed to geofence Utah, blocking access to its site in the state, and to monitor for connection attempts coming from Utah-based IP addresses. The office then reevaluated its position and moved to dismiss.

Attorney General Derek Brown framed the withdrawal as conditional. "Kalshi has pulled out of Utah, and if it returns, so will the charges," he said in the [release](https://attorneygeneral.utah.gov/utah-dismiss-kalshi-criminal-case/). "Every company in this space should take note. If they promote, support, or enable gambling, we will hold them accountable." The motion does not change the office's position that prediction markets are gambling under Utah law, which along with Hawaii is one of two states with no legal gambling of any kind.

Kalshi general counsel Yaakov Roth welcomed the move in a post on X and noted that the state's civil suit against the company is still running. "Good faith jurisdictional disagreements should never be criminalized," he wrote, adding that Kalshi is willing to work with state attorneys general on "reasonable interim measures until courts resolve the dispute over who regulates prediction markets," according to [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/utah-asks-court-to-toss-kalshi-criminal-case-but-the-charges-could-come-back/).

The criminal case was one of several tools the office is using. It also sent cease-and-desist letters to several of Kalshi's competitors, and including Kalshi, seven companies have now agreed to stop operating in Utah, the [attorney general's office said](https://attorneygeneral.utah.gov/utah-dismiss-kalshi-criminal-case/). The office will keep monitoring compliance and may bring civil or criminal cases against companies it believes offer gambling to residents.

The civil lawsuit the state filed against Kalshi and two Susquehanna International Group entities on Oct. 20 continues, [Unchained noted](https://unchainedcrypto.com/utah-asks-court-to-toss-kalshi-criminal-case-but-the-charges-could-come-back/). In that case the state seeks restitution, disgorgement and damages, and the complaint estimates that Kalshi collected between $30.6 million and $61.2 million in fees from Utah-based accounts as of Oct. 19, [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/utah-moves-to-dismiss-criminal-charges-against-kalshi-after-trading-halt).

The state's retreat on the criminal counts does not settle who regulates prediction markets. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission sued Utah on Oct. 27, asking a federal judge to bar the state from treating CFTC-registered exchanges as gambling, and the regulator has sued at least 12 states on the same theory, according to [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/utah-moves-to-dismiss-criminal-charges-against-kalshi-after-trading-halt). That case is still awaiting a ruling from U.S. District Judge Robert J. Shelby, [Unchained reported](https://unchainedcrypto.com/utah-asks-court-to-toss-kalshi-criminal-case-but-the-charges-could-come-back/).

Kalshi has said it expects to come back. The company "looks forward to returning to Utah once our legal position is vindicated," McGavick told KUTV, and Roth's post left the door open to interim geofencing deals with other state attorneys general while the courts sort out jurisdiction. Utah's motion now sits with Third District Court for a decision.
