Utexo opened early access to Utexo Mint on Oct. 22, letting professional operators test a bridged version of USDT on the Bitcoin Signet test network ahead of a public launch the company has scheduled for Nov. 28.

The announcement, dated Oct. 22 and datelined Dubai, opens onboarding to exchanges, wallets, market makers, OTC desks and trading firms. Inside the sandbox, those operators can test minting, confidential transfers and redemption through RGB, the protocol Utexo uses to issue and move assets on Bitcoin, [the company's post says](https://www.utexo.com/blog/utexo-opens-early-access-to-usdmnt-on-bitcoin-through-utexo-mint). The testing period is meant to let operators validate transaction flows and finish integrations before the launch.

Utexo Mint is a platform for verified operators to mint and redeem the bridged token. Utexo also plans tools to manage liquidity across supported networks and to convert between fiat and its bridged USDT, according to the post.

Signet is a Bitcoin test network, so the current phase does not touch mainnet funds. The testing period is meant to give operators time to validate transaction flows and finalize integrations ahead of the launch, and existing partners are already preparing on that schedule, ChainCatcher reported. Onboarding requests from professional operators are open now, and approved operators get access to the sandbox alongside those partners.

The company is also making a commercial pitch to early integrators. A press release shared with Bitcoin Magazine said operators who integrate early can be among the first to offer the token at launch, "positioning themselves to capture initial demand and build market share as adoption grows." That framing matches the company's broader claim that the return of USDT to Bitcoin creates an opportunity to bring dollar liquidity into the ecosystem and open markets for trading and BTC-backed lending. The announcement itself carries no liquidity or adoption figures to size that opportunity.

The launch does not make this a Tether product. Utexo Mint is not owned or operated by Tether, and the USDT on Bitcoin is backed by the stablecoin through Tether's USDT0 protocol, [ChainCatcher reported](https://www.chaincatcher.com/article/2298304) on Oct. 23. The outlet quoted Utexo as saying USDT on Bitcoin is launched by Utexo rather than by Tether, a point the company repeated to [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/utexo-opens-early-access-to-usdt-on-bitcoin-through-utexo-mint).

The company frames privacy as the point of the design. Utexo says its bridged token will be private by design, letting people hold and transfer funds without exposing transaction details publicly while keeping controls for regulatory compliance. Bitcoin Magazine reported that RGB's implementation avoids address reuse, which it described as a privacy practice that may even expose holders to quantum computer risks, and that businesses can protect sensitive commercial activity while keeping the compliance controls regulators expect. Asked how privacy can sit alongside sanctions and law enforcement duties, Utexo gave the outlet a direct answer: "We monitor all source USDT coming in, and check AML score + we monitor the risk on the RGB side too. We have a partnership with Crystal Intelligence for the AML."

The rollout runs in two phases. The first begins Nov. 28 with the public launch on Bitcoin through partner wallets and exchanges, bringing liquidity to swap providers and lending platforms, [the post says](https://www.utexo.com/blog/utexo-opens-early-access-to-usdmnt-on-bitcoin-through-utexo-mint). The second phase is planned for the first quarter of 2027 and would extend the bridged token to the Lightning Network for near-instant payments. Until Nov. 28 the token exists only for the operators now inside the Signet sandbox.

Utexo frames the product as a way to bring dollar liquidity to Bitcoin and open markets for trading and BTC-backed lending, describing USDT as first launched on Bitcoin in 2014 and now returning through its bridged version. Operators that integrate early can be among the first to offer the token at launch, the company said, and Utexo is accepting onboarding requests from professional operators that want to take part. Approved operators can then reach the sandbox and prepare integrations alongside the partners already working toward the launch.
