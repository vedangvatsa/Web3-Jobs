VanEck will not go ahead with its Lido staked ether exchange-traded fund. VanEck Digital Assets [said in an SEC filing on Oct. 14](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2091159/000209115926000005/vanecklidostakedethereumet.htm) that it does not intend to proceed with the planned distribution of the trust shares. Cointelegraph [reported the move on Oct. 15](https://cointelegraph.com/news/vaneck-seeks-to-withdraw-application-for-lido-staked-ether-etf) as a decision to shelve the product built around the liquid staking token.

The filing is a request for withdrawal under Rule 477. It covers the VanEck Lido Staked Ethereum ETF, file number 333-290918. The letter says the registration was never declared effective, no securities were issued or sold, none will be issued, no preliminary prospectus was distributed and there are no shareholders. Those facts close the file rather than pause it.

VanEck set up the product as a Delaware statutory trust around Oct. 2, 2025. The S-1 registration statement followed on Oct. 16, 2025. Crypto Briefing [dated the trust](https://cryptobriefing.com/vaneck-withdraws-lido-staked-ethereum-etf/) to about Oct. 2 and the S-1 to the Oct. 16 to Oct. 20 window. The trust route is the standard path for a commodity-style crypto fund before the agency reviews the full filing.

The fund would have held Lido staked ether, known as stETH, and would have accrued staking rewards through that holding. Investors would have received exposure to Ethereum staking yield without running validators, holding keys or touching the chain. The design aimed at daily liquidity and on-chain transparency inside a brokerage account. Cointelegraph [described that setup](https://cointelegraph.com/news/vaneck-seeks-to-withdraw-application-for-lido-staked-ether-etf) from the original filing.

The idea was aimed at institutions that cannot easily stake directly. A pension fund or wealth manager could collect staking yield through shares rather than wallets, validators and compliance memos about private keys. The structure was also pitched as tax-efficient. Crypto Briefing [laid out that pitch](https://cryptobriefing.com/vaneck-withdraws-lido-staked-ethereum-etf/) while noting the fund never reached the market.

The filing gave no reason for the withdrawal. VanEck did not explain what prompted the decision or whether it would seek a replacement product. Cointelegraph [said it asked](https://cointelegraph.com/news/vaneck-seeks-to-withdraw-application-for-lido-staked-ether-etf) VanEck Digital Assets for comment. Crypto Briefing [gave the same account](https://cryptobriefing.com/vaneck-withdraws-lido-staked-ethereum-etf/), writing that the firm left both the rationale and the timing unexplained.

At filing the bet was large. Lido total value locked stood near 38 billion to 40 billion dollars, with more than 2 billion dollars in cumulative staking rewards paid since launch. Crypto Briefing [reported those figures](https://cryptobriefing.com/vaneck-withdraws-lido-staked-ethereum-etf/) as the backdrop to the proposal. Lido governance token LDO rose about 7 percent after the initial ETF news, a move tied to hopes for future institutional flows rather than any money entering through the fund.

The product was a first. VanEck was the first firm to file in the United States for an ETF tied to stETH. That label matters because other staking products use different designs, including ether funds that later sought permission to stake part of their own ether. A fund holding the liquid staking token itself is a separate structure with its own pricing, redemption and risk questions.

The withdrawal lands as SEC thinking on staking keeps shifting. The agency has issued guidance that treats certain liquid staking activities as outside the securities laws. Interest in liquid staking funds has not gone away with this exit, in the United States or in Europe. The VanEck letter does not address that wider field, and its silence on reasons leaves the next step unclear.

For LDO holders the practical point is narrow. The 7 percent rally after filing reflected expectations about flows that will now not arrive through this vehicle. Lido protocol figures reported at filing did not depend on VanEck, and the protocol keeps running without the fund. The filing and the withdrawal are about access to yield through shares, not about whether staking itself continues.

The registration file shows a short life. Trust formation around Oct. 2, 2025, S-1 in mid-October 2025, withdrawal on Oct. 14, 2026, with no shares sold in between. The agency file number 333-290918 now points to a withdrawn registration.
