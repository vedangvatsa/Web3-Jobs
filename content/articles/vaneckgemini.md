VanEck plans to terminate its custody and clearing agreements with Gemini for three crypto exchange-traded funds, according to filings with the Securities and Exchange Commission on November 19, 2026. The asset manager notified Gemini on November 16 that the agreements for its Bitcoin ETF (HODL), Ether ETF (ETHV) and Solana ETF (VSOL) would end "on or about" February 16, 2027, [Crypto Briefing reported](https://cryptobriefing.com/solana-contract-vaneck-etf-gemini-termination-2026-11/).

Gemini will continue providing custody, clearing and settlement services until that date. Coinbase Custody, which already serves the funds under separate agreements, will remain a custodian for all three. The termination also covers a Gemini staking services agreement tied to the Solana ETF's SOL holdings. The filings did not disclose a reason for the terminations or identify a replacement for Gemini's staking services, [Cointelegraph reported](https://cointelegraph.com/news/vaneck-gemini-etf-custody-end-keep-coinbase).

The move ends a custody relationship that began with VanEck's first spot Bitcoin ETF. Gemini became the original Bitcoin custodian for HODL when the fund launched in January 2024, before VanEck added Coinbase Custody in June of that year. Coinbase now serves as custodian for many of the largest US spot crypto funds, and the filings leave Gemini's position in that market open to question.

Cointelegraph contacted VanEck and Gemini for comment on the reason for terminating the agreements and the potential impact on the Solana ETF's staking operations, but had not received a response from either company by publication. VanEck's Bitcoin ETF holds roughly $880 million in assets under management, compared with $47.6 billion for BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust and $10.6 billion for Fidelity's Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund, according to Wallet Pilot data cited by the [report](https://cointelegraph.com/news/vaneck-gemini-etf-custody-end-keep-coinbase) as of November 20.

In the United States, Gemini continues to serve as a custodian for at least one smaller fund, the 7RCC Spot Bitcoin and Carbon Credit Futures ETF (BTCK), which identified Gemini and BitGo as its Bitcoin custodians in a September 2026 regulatory filing. The exchange has also provided custody services for crypto ETFs in Canada and Australia, the [coverage says](https://cointelegraph.com/news/vaneck-gemini-etf-custody-end-keep-coinbase).

The termination extends beyond custody for the Solana fund. The VSOL filing covers the Gemini staking arrangement, and the filings give no timeline for how the fund will handle staking after February. Staking allows tokenholders to earn rewards by helping secure a proof-of-stake blockchain such as Solana, and for ETFs that stake their holdings the service can provide an additional source of returns, according to the [same filing coverage](https://cointelegraph.com/news/vaneck-gemini-etf-custody-end-keep-coinbase). VanEck has not named a firm to take over that staking work.

The [Crypto Briefing account](https://cryptobriefing.com/solana-contract-vaneck-etf-gemini-termination-2026-11/) states that Coinbase will continue serving as a custodian for the Solana fund's SOL holdings under a separate agreement, alongside its roles on the Bitcoin and Ether products. Whether Gemini will keep any role with VanEck after the effective date is not addressed in the filings.

Gemini's founders, Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss, filed to launch the Winklevoss Bitcoin Trust in July 2013, more than a decade before US regulators approved the first spot Bitcoin ETFs in January 2024. Gemini became the original Bitcoin custodian for VanEck's HODL when the fund launched that month, before Coinbase Custody was added later in 2024. The exchange has kept at least one other US crypto ETF client since, the 7RCC fund named above, while the three VanEck agreements end on the same day in February.

Neither VanEck nor Gemini has said who will take over Gemini's roles after February 16, 2027, and the filings do not specify whether the two firms will continue talking about a future arrangement. The termination notices cover all three funds on the same effective date, so the transition will land in a single step rather than a staggered handoff across the products. HODL, ETHV and VSOL each name Gemini in the custody agreements being ended, and each names Coinbase Custody in the agreements staying in place.
