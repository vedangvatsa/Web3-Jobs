Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh will deliver opening remarks at a San Francisco conference Monday that brings together technology executives, economists and employers to examine how artificial intelligence is changing inflation, productivity and hiring, according to the [San Francisco Fed's event page](https://www.frbsf.org/news-and-media/events/2026/11/productivity-and-jobs-task-force-conference). The conference, hosted by the Federal Reserve Board and the San Francisco Fed, runs from 9:00 a.m. to 12:15 p.m. Pacific at the bank's San Francisco campus, and the public can watch it on a livestream on the same page. In-person attendance is limited and by invitation only.

The agenda starts with 15 minutes of opening remarks from Warsh, then moves into three panels and a closing fireside chat. Warsh moderates the first panel, "At the Frontier: What Is the Pace and Scope of SI Adoption?", with Palo Alto Networks Chairman Nikesh Arora, Stripe co-founder Patrick Collison and Affirm founder Max Levchin, the [agenda shows](https://www.frbsf.org/news-and-media/events/2026/11/productivity-and-jobs-task-force-conference). He also leads a second panel featuring Stanford economist Nick Bloom, MIT dean Dan Huttenlocher, Stanford President Jonathan Levin and Brookings Institution President Cecilia Rouse. San Francisco Fed President Mary C. Daly takes over for the third panel on business adoption, and Warsh returns for the 11:45 a.m. fireside chat with Andreessen Horowitz co-founder Marc Andreessen and former Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo.

The conference informs the Fed's task force on productivity and jobs, one of five the central bank announced in June to study areas that feed directly into monetary policy. The task force is assessing the economic impact of new general-purpose technologies, including what the Fed calls super intelligence, to inform its policy judgments. The [San Francisco Fed summary](https://www.frbsf.org/news-and-media/events/2026/11/productivity-and-jobs-task-force-conference) said the event is expected to give the task force input from a wide range of stakeholders on technology's implications for the economy and the labor market.

Warsh will try to understand the risks and benefits he and his colleagues may have to balance, from possible large-scale job losses to the promise of rapid growth and falling prices, a [Reuters report on the forum](https://allwork.space/2026/11/fed-chair-kevin-warsh-to-examine-ais-impact-on-jobs-and-inflation-as-concerns-grow) said. The event is the only scheduled public appearance among the five task forces Warsh established early in his tenure this year, the report added. It arrives as the AI buildout is pushing prices in the opposite direction of its promised productivity gains, a trend that already concerns many Fed officials, according to the same report.

Warsh has argued that AI could let companies produce more goods and services at lower costs, easing inflation without keeping borrowing expensive, [Decrypt reported](https://decrypt.co/383259/fed-chair-warsh-wants-answers-ai-inflation-bitcoin). Some colleagues see a different problem: the AI boom is already making things more expensive while the productivity benefits remain largely theoretical. Building data centers requires electricity, computer chips, construction materials and skilled workers, and when technology companies compete for those limited resources, prices rise. Fed Governor Lisa Cook warned in September that AI investment was adding inflationary pressure and delaying the central bank's return to its 2% inflation target, Decrypt said.

Job losses are the other half of the dilemma. Fed Governor Michael Barr warned on Thursday that businesses replacing human tasks with AI systems could cause serious short-term disruptions to the labor market, the [Reuters report](https://allwork.space/2026/11/fed-chair-kevin-warsh-to-examine-ais-impact-on-jobs-and-inflation-as-concerns-grow) noted. Richmond Fed President Tom Barkin called AI investment "truly historic," comparing it to railroads and interstate highways, and said a pullback would put growth at risk. Warsh himself acknowledged in August that AI represents "a new variable" for monetary policy.

The crypto market is watching the same variables from the other side. Bitcoin dropped below $69,000 on Friday as stronger-than-expected inflation figures fueled concerns about another Fed rate hike, according to [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/383259/fed-chair-warsh-wants-answers-ai-inflation-bitcoin). AI data centers and Bitcoin mining operations both compete for large amounts of electricity, and some miners have already switched to serving AI customers. Hyperscale Data [shut down mining at its Michigan facility](https://decrypt.co/383259/fed-chair-warsh-wants-answers-ai-inflation-bitcoin) in September for an AI deal potentially worth more than $1.2 billion. If AI spending keeps inflation high, the Fed could hold interest rates up or raise them further, which tends to drain money from speculative investments including cryptocurrencies.

The task force Warsh picked to study these questions is led by Andreessen, with Stanford professor Chad Jones, who is on leave at AI firm Anthropic, and Microsoft Xbox chief Asha Sharma as the other members, the [Reuters report](https://allwork.space/2026/11/fed-chair-kevin-warsh-to-examine-ais-impact-on-jobs-and-inflation-as-concerns-grow) said. Monday's conference will not determine the Fed's next rate decision, which is scheduled for December 9. It ends at 12:15 p.m. Pacific with the fireside chat, and the full session will be available on the San Francisco Fed's livestream.
