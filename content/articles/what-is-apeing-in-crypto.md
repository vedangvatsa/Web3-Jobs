The language of the crypto world is filled with colorful and often animal-themed slang, and one of the most common terms you'll encounter is "apeing in."

**What does it mean?**

To "ape in" means to invest in a cryptocurrency or NFT project with a high degree of conviction and often with a significant amount of capital, without necessarily having done extensive research. It's a decision driven more by gut feeling, community hype, and a strong sense of FOMO (Fear of Missing Out) than by careful analysis.

The term conjures the image of an ape, acting on instinct and brute force, throwing themselves into a situation with enthusiasm.

**When is the term used?**

-   "The art for that new NFT project looks amazing, the Discord is buzzing. I'm going to ape in."
-   "I just got a good feeling about this new meme coin. Time to ape."

**The Psychology Behind Apeing In**

Apeing in is the opposite of [DYOR (Do Your Own Research)](/what-is-dyor-in-crypto). It's an emotional, high-risk investment style that is a core part of the "degen" (degenerate) trading culture in Web3.

-   **High-Risk, High-Reward:** The strategy is to get into a project very early, before it becomes mainstream. If the project is successful, the returns can be astronomical (10x, 100x, or even more). However, the risk of the project going to zero is also extremely high.
-   **FOMO-Driven:** It's often a reaction to seeing others make huge profits on similar projects. The fear of missing out on the next big thing can be a powerful motivator.
-   **Community Signal:** In some ways, "apeing in" is a bet on the strength and hype of a community. The investor is betting that the collective belief and enthusiasm of the community will be enough to propel the project to success.

While "apeing in" can sometimes lead to incredible gains, it is an extremely risky strategy and is not recommended for beginners. It's a style of investing that is deeply embedded in the high-stakes, fast-moving culture of the crypto frontier, and it often results in losses. A more prudent approach always involves thorough research and a clear understanding of the risks involved.
