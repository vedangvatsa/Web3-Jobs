Patrick Witt is leaving his post as executive director of the White House crypto council. Witt [wrote on X on Thursday](https://x.com/patrickjwitt/status/2110579591073104355) that serving in the administration had been the privilege of a lifetime, and said he looks forward to supporting American technology work in the private sector. Cointelegraph [reported the exit on Oct. 15](https://cointelegraph.com/news/white-house-crypto-council-director-steps-down) as a move to a new private-sector opportunity.

The White House confirmed the departure to crypto journalist Eleanor Terrett. A White House official said in an email on Oct. 14 that his detail had expired. Terrett [published that reply](https://x.com/EleanorTerrett/status/2110451463876980957) on X. The statement thanked Witt for his service to the administration and wished him well, without naming anyone to take over the role.

Witt led the President Council of Advisers for Digital Assets, known as the White House Crypto Council. He took the job in August 2025 after Bo Hines left. The council coordinated administration crypto policy, including talks around the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act. Cointelegraph [described that role](https://cointelegraph.com/news/white-house-crypto-council-director-steps-down) in its account of the exit.

His employment ran through a temporary assignment from the Defense Department. Crypto.news [reported on Oct. 15](https://crypto.news/patrick-witt-white-house-clarity-act/) that Witt was a Defense Department employee on detail to the crypto council, and that the assignment expired on Oct. 14. The same report says he is expected to resume Georgia Army National Guard commitments. Witt had once expected to leave in July for mandatory Guard training, but the training was postponed so he could stay through the Senate debate.

No successor has been named. The White House did not say who would handle day-to-day crypto policy or when a replacement might be announced. Cointelegraph [said it asked](https://cointelegraph.com/news/white-house-crypto-council-director-steps-down) about a potential replacement and will update when the White House replies. The absence of a named successor does not by itself show the council has been closed.

The exit leaves a gap at the top of the crypto staff. David Sacks, the White House artificial intelligence and crypto adviser, left in March 2026 after reaching the limit for his special government employee arrangement. Deputy crypto director Harry Jung left at the end of July. Witt was then the only remaining known staff member on the council, according to Terrett reporting [summarized by crypto.news](https://crypto.news/patrick-witt-white-house-clarity-act/). Hines, the first executive director, had departed in August 2025 and later joined stablecoin issuer Tether as a strategic adviser on American expansion.

Witt was the administration point person on the Clarity Act, the bill meant to set the first full American regulatory framework for crypto markets. The House passed its version in July 2025. Witt worked with lawmakers and industry groups on the Senate version through the summer. Cointelegraph [tied his tenure](https://cointelegraph.com/news/white-house-crypto-council-director-steps-down) directly to that bill.

The Senate failed to advance the bill on Sept. 15. The cloture motion drew 49 votes in favor and 50 against, short of the 60 needed to move forward. The vote blocked floor consideration at that stage rather than rejecting the full bill on its merits. Further progress this session looks unlikely, though a motion to reconsider left a procedural path open. No successful second vote had been reported by Oct. 14.

Disputes around the bill covered stablecoin rewards, decentralized finance, financial crime controls and ethics rules for officials with crypto interests. Democratic critics said the draft did not handle potential conflicts tied to President Donald Trump and family crypto businesses. Republican supporters said it would give clearer rules for trading and stronger customer protections. Negotiators added revisions on state attorney general enforcement, but opponents questioned whether the ethics limits could be enforced.

Industry groups praised Witt after the news. Blockchain Association chief policy officer Lindsay Fraser credited him with holding together difficult talks between lawmakers and firms. Solana Policy Institute president Kristin Smith thanked him for work on market structure talks and said building consensus was no small task. Crypto.news [collected those reactions](https://crypto.news/patrick-witt-white-house-clarity-act/) without reporting any new deal on the bill.

The bill text would divide duties between the Securities and Exchange Commission and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, with registration for brokers and dealers, rules for digital commodity exchanges and customer safeguards. The [congressional text](https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr3633/BILLS-119hr3633rfs.pdf) runs through those assignments in detail. Without passage, the two agencies keep working under current law while weighing further rule moves.

The November 2026 midterms have shortened the calendar for any new push. Senate remarks recorded on Sept. 16 show members still split after the failed vote, with Senator John Cornyn defending the bill as clearer rules and investor protection. Witt departure does not change the vote count, but it removes the White House staffer who sat through months of those talks.

The White House has not set a timetable for naming a new crypto council lead.
