The World Bank Group said Tuesday it will explore using Circle's USDC and blockchain-based payment rails, alongside Citi's banking connections, to disburse development funds faster and more cheaply than correspondent banking allows. The institution's [press release](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/11/17/world-bank-group-explores-regulated-stablecoins-to-modernize-payments-redirect-res) describes "a commitment to explore regulated stablecoin infrastructure" and names Citi's existing connections to banking rails and Circle's payments infrastructure to mint and settle funds in USDC as central to the initiative.

The effort builds on FundsChain, a World Bank blockchain platform that tracks disbursements across investment projects in developing countries. More than 350 projects across 96 countries are already onboarded to its digital ledgers, allowing all parties involved in a project to see how funds are being used, the release said.

The World Bank framed the work as a response to cost pressure. Development finance institutions operate under mounting resource constraints, the release said, and modest efficiency gains achieved at scale have the potential to redirect meaningful resources toward development impact.

"This initiative is about exploring ways to make the financial plumbing of development work better for the people who need it most," World Bank Group President Ajay Banga said. "Enabling money to move faster and at lower cost means real development impact - a health clinic gets built on time, a farmer gets paid before the harvest season ends, and a government can be more responsive to pressing needs."

Citi head of services Shahmir Khaliq said the collaboration would deliver "24/7 capabilities at scale so that critical funds move in real time." He said Citi connects clients across more than 180 jurisdictions and framed the work as a way to give clients choices in how they move value while complying fully with local regulations. Circle co-founder and chief executive Jeremy Allaire said money that moves "at the speed of the internet shouldn't be reserved for the world's biggest banks and corporations; it should reach the people and places that need it most, with full visibility into where every dollar goes."

Allaire added in the release that the World Bank Group has always worked to make development finance more efficient and accountable, and that with the initiative "they're helping set the standard for how global institutions put stablecoin technology to work responsibly."

[Decrypt's Nov. 17 report](https://decrypt.co/383485/world-bank-circle-citi-test-stablecoins-development-payments) said Citi would supply connections to established banking rails while Circle provides the infrastructure to mint and settle funds in USDC. Under the arrangement, the stablecoin exploration would focus initially on an intermediated, interoperable model, limited to borrower countries where digital assets are permitted, with plans to involve other banks over time.

[CoinMarketCap's Nov. 18 report](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/world-bank-circle-usdc-stablecoin-payments) stressed that nothing has been committed to production: the initial phase is exploratory, and no live rollout has been announced. The same report noted USDC is the fifth-largest cryptocurrency by market capitalization, worth about $68.9 billion at publication.

The World Bank has run blockchain-based tracking through FundsChain for some time. The lender could track funds paid to governments but found it difficult to follow money as it passed through implementing agencies and contractors to final recipients, because records sat scattered across organizations, [Digital Today reported](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/127233/world-bank-group-considers-using-usdc-for-development-fund-disbursements-in-partnership-with-citi-and-circle). The platform records transactions and supporting documentation at each step on a shared ledger so authorized project participants can check fund flows in one place, giving the example of a $500 million flood management project in Metro Manila jointly financed by the World Bank and the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank. The bank is also testing separate functions to manage payment timing and conditions, linking payments to project stages through local payment systems.

The USDC review focuses on whether blockchain can also be used for actual fund transfers and settlement, according to that report. The World Bank said it plans to first consult with borrowing countries where digital assets are permitted and later seek cooperation with other banks. It has not disclosed participating countries, payment recipients, amounts or timelines, including any actual disbursements via USDC.

For Circle, a role in the World Bank's payment plumbing marks a step toward mainstream use of its dollar token. [Decrypt noted](https://decrypt.co/383485/world-bank-circle-citi-test-stablecoins-development-payments) the move fits a broader push by traditional finance into the technology, with Goldman Sachs, Citi and other banks committing roughly $270 million to a joint stablecoin venture and Deutsche Bank recently going live with crypto custody after a five-year buildout.
