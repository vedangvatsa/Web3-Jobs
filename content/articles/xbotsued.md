London's High Court entered a default judgment on Nov. 20 against two men accused of running a network of Bitcoin-focused accounts that collected payouts from X's now-retired Creator Revenue Sharing program. Master Teverson made the order in claim BL-2026-001161 for X Internet Unlimited Company and X Corp against Vivek Kumar Sen and Zamyang Sherpa after neither man filed an acknowledgment of service or a defense. [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/383985/elon-musk-x-court-judgment-bitcoin-bot-farm-operators) and [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/x-wins-bitcoin-creator-payout-fraud-lawsuit/) both reported the ruling.

The [order itself](https://transparency.x.com/assets/legal/2026-11-23-X-v-Sen-and-Sherpa-Judgment.pdf) records the sequence. X issued its claim form and particulars of claim on Sept. 21, sent them to both defendants by first-class post the same day, and copied them by email; service was deemed complete on Sept. 23 under CPR 6.14, certificates of service were filed on Sept. 28, and X applied for default judgment on Oct. 21. Because the defendants did not respond within the times prescribed by CPR 10.3 and CPR 15.4, the court ordered judgment for the claimants against the first and second defendants, "with the amount of damages and interest to be decided by the Court."

No money has been awarded. The order sets a Disposal Hearing before the Master with a time estimate of one hour and a date still to be fixed, and it requires X to serve witness statements supporting its damages claim 14 days before that hearing, with the defendants allowed seven days before it to reply. Either defendant may apply within seven days of service of the order to have it varied or set aside, the order says. A third defendant listed as Persons Unknown is named in the claim but is not covered by the Nov. 20 judgment, according to [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/x-wins-bitcoin-creator-payout-fraud-lawsuit/).

X's claims against the two men have not been tested at trial. The company alleges that six accounts enrolled in Creator Revenue Sharing between August 2023 and February 2026, including @Vivek4real_, @Bitcoin_Teddy and @saylordocs, published substantially similar crypto content within seconds or minutes of each other and used three further accounts to like, reply and repost in order to inflate engagement and the resulting payments. X estimates it paid at least £207,384 to the disputed accounts, of which about £74,332 went to @Vivek4real_ and around £49,442 to @saylordocs, and it seeks at least £75,000 more for investigation and remediation work. Those figures come from X's particulars of claim and are not sums the court has granted. The causes of action include deceit, unlawful means conspiracy, unjust enrichment and breach of contract.

A default judgment of this kind means the court entered the order because the defendants failed to engage, not because the allegations were heard and proven at trial, Decrypt noted. The complaint identified nine accounts in total: the six enrolled in the program, plus @BTC_Vibes, @MrSuperBitcoin and @Laserlump in an annex, which X alleges liked, replied to and reposted the enrolled accounts' output to drive the engagement metrics that determined payments, according to [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/x-wins-bitcoin-creator-payout-fraud-lawsuit/).

The dispute runs alongside changes to how X pays creators. X suspended the disputed accounts on Aug. 18, stopped accepting new Creator Revenue Sharing participants on Aug. 7, and retired the program on Sept. 7, replacing it the next day with Original Content Rewards, which pays for eligible views of original content and excludes artificial views and fraudulent engagement from the calculation. X's general counsel James Burnham announced the ruling on X, writing that the company had "sued people who abused Creator Revenue Sharing by operating a network of accounts to manipulate engagement" and that the court had entered judgment against two of them, [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/383985/elon-musk-x-court-judgment-bitcoin-bot-farm-operators) reported. Elon Musk reposted the statement with the line "Don't mess with X."

The claims are large but untested: X puts the disputed payouts at no less than £207,384, about $271,000, and says it is seeking at least £75,000, about $98,000, in investigation and remediation costs, figures Decrypt records as the company's own estimates rather than court-awarded sums. Recovery would depend on whatever assets Sen and Sherpa hold, and the order leaves the amount, interest and the costs of the October application for the disposal hearing.
