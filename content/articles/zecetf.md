Bitwise Asset Management filed a registration statement with the US Securities and Exchange Commission to list a spot Zcash exchange-traded fund on the Texas Stock Exchange, according to [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitwise-zcash-etf-texas-stock-exchange-listing). The S-1 names TXSE as the anticipated listing venue and the ticker ZEC, and the fund would hold Zcash directly rather than futures or other derivatives.

Coinbase Custody would safeguard the fund's ZEC tokens, while Bitwise Investment Advisers would serve as its manager and sponsor, the filing shows. The fund has not begun trading, the registration statement has yet to become effective, and the proposed annual sponsor fee is still unspecified, according to [Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/read/news/bitwise-files-for-a-zcash-etf-on-the-texas-stock-exchange), which also reviewed the filing.

The venue choice marks a change from earlier drafts. Bitwise's two confidential drafts, filed in September and October, both named NYSE Arca, and the public S-1 does not explain the switch, Bankless reported. The Texas Stock Exchange opened for trading on July 31, and the SEC approved its generic listing standards for commodity-based trust shares on Oct. 29, the report said. TXSE is a newer venue than the NYSE Arca platform where Grayscale's fund trades, and the S-1 does not spell out what drew Bitwise to it.

The structure of the proposed fund keeps several variables plain. Shares would be created in cash or in kind, and Zcash has no staking, so there is no yield component, per Bankless. The fund's value would rest on a CF Benchmarks price built from Coinbase and Kraken trades, two of the largest US crypto venues, so the benchmark draws on a narrow slice of ZEC trading rather than a single exchange's order book. Zcash's privacy features stay out of the product: the filing says the trust "does not engage in shielded transactions," and it warns that analytics tools have a harder time vetting coins that have passed through a shielded pool. About 29 percent of ZEC supply sat in shielded pools as of Oct. 5, per the S-1 as recounted by Bankless.

That exclusion matches how the incumbent product works. Grayscale's Zcash ETF, ticker ZCSH, has traded on NYSE Arca since Aug. 25 at a 2.50 percent fee, and the Winklevoss Zcash ETF filed on Oct. 6 for Nasdaq under the ticker WINK at a 0.25 percent fee, according to Bankless. Bitwise's own S-1 concedes the trust "will not be the first exchange-traded product holding Zcash," the report said. Its proposed fee has not been published yet, so the cost comparison with those two products stays open until Bitwise amends the filing.

Cointelegraph placed the filing in the wider rush of asset managers expanding Zcash-linked investment products in the United States. Grayscale launched its fund in August as one of the first issuers to offer US-listed ETF exposure to the cryptocurrency, and Winklevoss Asset Services followed in October, that outlet reported. The Winklevoss product would trade on Nasdaq, while Grayscale's fund already sits on NYSE Arca, so a Bitwise listing on TXSE would put three separate US venues in play for the same underlying asset. The Bitwise filing lands while ZEC trades near $925, down about 18 percent over the prior 30 days, Bankless reported.

Shares in the proposed fund would represent direct claims on ZEC held in custody rather than exposure through futures or other derivatives, and Zcash's lack of staking means the trust produces no yield for holders, per the filing summaries in both reports. The public filing follows the Oct. 16 draft that identified NYSE Arca, Cointelegraph noted, so the venue named to the SEC has changed inside a single month of registration work.

The fund would seek to reflect the value of its ZEC holdings minus operating expenses and liabilities, per Cointelegraph. The registration statement has been made public, but the fund cannot list until the SEC declares the S-1 effective.
