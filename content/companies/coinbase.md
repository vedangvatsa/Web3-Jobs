Coinbase is an American cryptocurrency exchange founded in June 2012 by Brian Armstrong and Fred Ehrsam. Armstrong, a former Airbnb engineer, entered Y Combinator in summer 2012. Ehrsam, a former Goldman Sachs trader, noticed Armstrong's posts on Reddit and joined as co-founder. The name comes from coinbase transactions, the special transactions that create new coins in proof of work networks. As of 2024, Coinbase is the largest U.S. based exchange and the largest bitcoin custodian among public companies.

## Company overview

Coinbase serves customers in more than 100 countries. Public filings and company pages describe more than 100 million verified users. The 2023 Form 10-K reported 108 million verified users. The Y Combinator and Coinbase history pages note the Summer 2012 batch and the early focus on making bitcoin easy to buy with bank transfers.

The company became public on April 14, 2021 through a direct listing on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under ticker COIN. The SEC declared its S-1 effective on April 1, 2021, and trading began as planned on April 14. On May 12, 2025, S&P Dow Jones Indices announced that Coinbase would join the S&P 500 effective before the open on May 19, 2025, replacing Discover Financial Services which was being acquired by Capital One. Coinbase is the first crypto exchange in that index.

Coinbase is remote-first. In May 2020 it committed to remote-first work, and in February 2021 it stated it no longer maintains a formal headquarters. Its 10-K notes that it lists a placeholder address only to satisfy SEC requirements. About 95 percent of employees can work from home, an office, or a mix. The company keeps hub offices for periodic in-person work, notably San Francisco and New York, and opened a 58,600 square foot hub at 110 East in Charlotte, North Carolina in early 2025 with about 150 employees in customer experience and compliance and a plan to hire more than 130 locally as part of a broader plan to add about 1,000 jobs in the United States in 2025. It announced a hiring presence in India in March 2021 with an initial office planned in Hyderabad for engineering, software development and customer support.

## What Coinbase does

Coinbase provides trading, custody, and infrastructure for individuals, institutions, and developers. For retail users it offers spot buying and selling, recurring buys, and staking in eligible regions. For traders it offers order book trading. For institutions it offers prime brokerage, custody, and financing. For developers it offers node infrastructure, wallet as a service, and payments tooling.

Key products:

- **Coinbase app (Simple Trade):** Mobile and web app for buying, selling, and holding assets. Fees include a spread and a separate transaction fee shown on the preview screen.
- **Advanced Trade:** Order book trading with TradingView charts, limit and stop orders, and API access. It replaced Coinbase Pro and has no subscription fee. Fees are maker taker and volume based, with no spread on order book trades.
- **Coinbase Exchange:** The underlying exchange for professional and institutional order book trading.
- **Coinbase Wallet, now Base App:** Self custody wallet for holding keys, connecting to apps, and managing assets across chains. In July 2025 Coinbase rebranded Coinbase Wallet as the Base App, described as an all-in-one app for trading, USDC payments, messaging with XMTP, social with Farcaster, and mini apps. The company said the app runs on Base and that existing wallet users could switch between legacy and Base modes.
- **Coinbase Custody:** Cold storage custody for institutions, including qualified custody for ETF clients. The Q4 2024 shareholder letter reported $220 billion in assets under custody at year end 2024.
- **Coinbase Prime:** Trading, custody, reporting, and financing for institutions and large traders.
- **Coinbase Commerce and Coinbase Business:** Payments and treasury tools for merchants, including USDC settlement.
- **Coinbase One:** Paid membership with benefits such as lower trading fees and boosted USDC rewards. The company reported more than 600,000 subscribers in December 2024.
- **USD Coin (USDC):** A dollar backed stablecoin issued by Circle. Coinbase and Circle launched USDC in September 2018 through the Centre Consortium. In August 2023 they dissolved Centre. Circle became sole issuer and Coinbase took an equity stake in Circle and signed a collaboration agreement. Under that agreement Coinbase receives 100 percent of reserve income on USDC held on its platform and a share of residual reserve income from USDC in circulation elsewhere. On the Q2 2026 earnings call, CFO Alesia Haas said conditions for renewal were met and the agreement would renew on the same terms. Reports dated August 2026 placed the renewal as an automatic three year extension from August 18, 2026. Circle disclosed $908 million in Coinbase related distribution costs in 2024.

## Technology: Base

Coinbase launched Base on February 23, 2023 as a testnet, later moving to mainnet. Base is an Ethereum layer 2 built on the MIT licensed OP Stack from Optimism. Coinbase joined as the second core development team on the OP Stack and contributes to its Superchain vision. The launch post stated the goal of bringing one billion users onchain and noted that Coinbase has no plan to issue a new network token for Base.

In July 2025 Coinbase introduced upgrades alongside the Base App rebrand. The company said Base App brings together trading, earning, and messaging, while Base Account provides cross chain identity and smart wallet functions and Base Pay provides USDC checkout, with early integration on Shopify and fee sponsorship for USDC sends on Base. Technical updates included a plan to cut Base block times from 2 seconds to 200 milliseconds.

## Compliance and regulatory history

Coinbase describes its approach as compliance first. The platform requires identity verification, screens for sanctions, and applies anti money laundering controls. It reports certain customer activity as required by U.S. tax rules.

On June 6, 2023, the SEC filed a civil enforcement action alleging Coinbase operated as an unregistered broker, exchange, and clearing agency and that it offered at least 13 tokens as unregistered securities. Coinbase opposed the claims and noted the SEC had declared its 2021 listing registration effective.

On March 27, 2024, the court granted in part and denied in part Coinbase's motion for judgment on the pleadings. On January 7, 2025, the court granted a motion to certify an interlocutory appeal. On January 21, 2025, the SEC announced a Crypto Task Force to develop a clearer framework. On February 27, 2025, the SEC announced it had filed a joint stipulation with Coinbase to dismiss the action with prejudice, with no costs or fees to either party and with Coinbase withdrawing its appeal. The SEC said the dismissal reflected its judgment about facilitating its efforts to reform its approach, not an assessment of the merits, and that it does not reflect its position on other cases. Reuters and CoinDesk reported the dismissal came under Chair Mark Uyeda after a vote that included a dissent from Commissioner Caroline Crenshaw.

## Financial performance

All figures below are from the 10-K filed February 13, 2025 for fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, and the Q4 2024 shareholder letter:

- 2024 total revenue $6.564 billion, net revenue $6.293 billion, net income $2.579 billion, adjusted EBITDA $3.348 billion. Total transaction revenue $3.986 billion, subscription and services revenue $2.307 billion. Stablecoin revenue $910.5 million, up 31 percent year over year.
- 2023 total revenue $3.108 billion, net income $94.9 million, net revenue $2.926 billion.
- The shareholder letter noted 2024 revenue more than doubled year over year, trading volume of $1.16 trillion, and $9.3 billion in USD resources at year end.

The company also reported 8.4 million monthly transacting users in 2024, up from 7.4 million in 2023, and 3,772 full time employees at year end 2024, up 10 percent year over year. The S&P 500 addition followed a requirement to report a profit in the latest quarter and cumulative profit over four quarters.

## Culture

In September 2020, CEO Brian Armstrong published "Coinbase is a mission focused company," stating the mission is to increase economic freedom by building an open financial system and that Coinbase would stay focused on that mission rather than broad social activism outside it. The post invited employees who preferred an activism focused workplace to discuss a severance option with their manager. In a follow up on October 8, 2020, Armstrong said about 5 percent of employees, 60 people at the time, accepted the exit package.

## Partnerships

- **BlackRock, August 4, 2022:** Coinbase Prime connected to BlackRock's Aladdin to give mutual institutional clients access to bitcoin trading, custody, prime brokerage, and reporting in their existing workflows. Access rolled out in phases to clients contracted with both firms.
- **Google Cloud, October 11, 2022:** A long term strategic partnership under which Coinbase selected Google Cloud as a strategic cloud provider for exchange and data services, including processing blockchain data and building its global data platform with Google's fiber and BigQuery, while Google said it would enable select customers, starting in Web3, to pay for cloud services with crypto via Coinbase Commerce and would explore using Coinbase Prime for custody and reporting. Coinbase said it would move data related applications to Google Cloud.
- **Kalshi, November 13, 2025:** Kalshi, the largest CFTC regulated prediction market in the United States, selected Coinbase Custody to safeguard USDC for event contracts. Leaked pages in November 2025 and later reporting indicated Coinbase plans to offer prediction markets to eligible customers through Coinbase Financial Markets via KalshiEX LLC, using cash held in the main Coinbase account. The company pointed to a December 17, 2025 livestream for product announcements and described the work as part of its everything exchange goal.

## Trade offs to weigh

Coinbase charges more than some rivals for the simplest way to buy. Standard app purchases include a spread of about 0.50 percent plus a transaction fee that varies by payment method, order size, and location, while Advanced Trade uses maker taker fees starting at 0.40 percent maker and 0.60 percent taker for under $10,000 in 30 day volume and falling with volume. Using Advanced Trade and ACH deposits instead of cards cuts those costs substantially.

Listing and feature speed can trail offshore exchanges. Asset additions require compliance review. Geographic restrictions apply to staking and other features. Custodied assets rely on Coinbase security controls, while self custody in Base App gives you key control but makes you responsible for backups and phishing risks. Base remains early in its decentralization path toward staged rollup maturity, so users should review current fault proof and upgrade key arrangements before treating it as equivalent to Ethereum for high value settlement.
