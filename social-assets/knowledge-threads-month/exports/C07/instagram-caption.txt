A valid signature can be reused. A token can transfer less than requested.

Five smart-contract security interview questions on tools, accounting, replay, fuzzing and read-call limits.

1. Q: What is Slither, and what can it find?

A: A Solidity/Vyper static analyzer. It flags reentrancy, tx.origin authorization and unchecked calls. Review each finding; a clean run does not prove safety.

2. Q: What token behavior matters in a staking audit?

A: Transfer fees and rebases. For supported fee-on-transfer tokens, credit the received balance delta. Rebases need separate accounting or explicit rejection.

3. Q: What is signature replay, and how do you stop it?

A: Reusing a valid signed action. Sign and consume a nonce, bind the domain to chainId and verifyingContract, and enforce a deadline. EIP-712 alone does not prevent reuse.

4. Q: What is fuzzing?

A: Generated inputs test properties. Echidna fuzzes call sequences; Foundry supports fuzz and invariant tests. Example for a simple custody vault: assets must cover recorded liabilities.

5. Q: Can an unbounded view loop cause denial of service?

A: Yes. eth_call executes on an RPC node and can hit gas caps or timeouts. A getAllOwners() that grows forever can fail. Return bounded pages instead.
