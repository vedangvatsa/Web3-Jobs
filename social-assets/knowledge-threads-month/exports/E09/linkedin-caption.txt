For an Indian passport, Nepal is visa-free, Indonesia sells a 30-day visa on arrival, and Vietnam offers an advance eVisa. Here are five named examples with the documents, fees or conditions that distinguish them. Tourist travel, checked October 20.

1. Nepal: Indian nationals do not need a visa. Carry a valid Indian passport or Election Commission voter card. Nepal Tourism Board explicitly says Aadhaar is not an accepted travel document; a domestic ID is not automatically border ID.

2. Bhutan: Indian visitors need an entry permit, not a visa. For a 5-night inland trip, adult SDF is INR 6,000 (INR 1,200/night), before accommodation and the mandatory guide. Carry an Indian passport or voter ID.

3. Maldives: tourist visa issued on arrival, rather than visa-free entry. Bring an MRZ passport valid at least 1 month, prepaid registered accommodation and return ticket. Submit the free IMUGA traveller declaration within 96 hours before arrival.

4. Indonesia: Indian passports qualify for B1 visa on arrival, IDR 500,000 for up to 30 days. One extension can bring the total to 60 days. Carry a passport valid at least 6 months and onward/return ticket. Online e-VOA is also available.

5. Vietnam: Indian tourists can apply in advance for an eVisa of up to 90 days. USD 25 single-entry or USD 50 multiple-entry; no refund if refused. Follow the granted dates and use designated eVisa border gates.
