# "Running bitcoin": The Story of the First-Ever Bitcoin Transaction

On January 12, 2009, just days after the Bitcoin network went live, a simple transaction took place that would go down in history. Satoshi Nakamoto, the anonymous creator of Bitcoin, sent 10 BTC to Hal Finney, a renowned cryptographer and one of the earliest contributors to the project. This was the first-ever peer-to-peer transaction of bitcoin between two individuals, a pivotal moment that proved the network was not just a theoretical concept, but a functioning reality.

## The Recipient: Hal Finney

Hal Finney was no ordinary recipient. He was a legendary figure in the world of cryptography long before Bitcoin existed. As a lead developer for PGP Corporation, he was one of the earliest employees of the company that created Pretty Good Privacy, a widely used email encryption program.

Finney was also a dedicated "cypherpunk," part of a movement that advocated for the use of strong cryptography to bring about social and political change. He had been involved in previous digital cash experiments and immediately grasped the significance of Satoshi's invention.

When Satoshi released the first version of the Bitcoin software, Finney was one of the very first people to download and run it. On January 10, 2009, he famously tweeted:

> "Running bitcoin"

## The Transaction: Block 170

The historic transaction occurred at block 170 of the Bitcoin blockchain. Satoshi Nakamoto sent 10 bitcoins to Hal Finney's address. The purpose was simple: to test if the nascent network actually worked as designed.

It did.

For Finney and the small group of cryptographers watching, this was a magical moment. It was proof that a decentralized, peer-to-peer electronic cash system, an idea they had been chasing for decades, was finally viable.

## Finney's Vision for Bitcoin

Hal Finney's contributions went far beyond simply being the recipient of the first transaction. He was the first person other than Satoshi to run the Bitcoin software, and he provided valuable bug reports and suggestions in the early days.

Tragically, Finney was diagnosed with ALS in 2009. Despite his deteriorating physical condition, he continued to contribute to the Bitcoin community until his passing in 2014. In his final post on the Bitcointalk forum, he reflected on his early involvement and his vision for Bitcoin's future:

> "I thought I was dealing with a young man of Japanese ancestry who was very smart and sincere. I have had the good fortune to correspond with many brilliant people over the course of my life, so I recognize the signs... When Satoshi announced the first release of the software, I grabbed it right away. I think I was the first person besides Satoshi to run bitcoin."

He went on to speculate about Bitcoin's potential value. He imagined a future where Bitcoin becomes the dominant global payment system. In a famous calculation, he estimated that if Bitcoin were to capture the value of all household wealth in the world, each coin would be worth about $10 million. This was not a price prediction, but a thought experiment to illustrate the sheer scale of what Bitcoin could become.

## A Legacy Preserved

The transaction on January 12, 2009, was a small technical test, but it represented a giant leap for decentralized technology. It was the moment the theoretical became practical. Hal Finney's immediate recognition of Bitcoin's potential and his early involvement were critical in giving the project credibility in the eyes of the cryptography community.

The story of the first transaction is a tribute to the collaborative, open-source spirit that defined Bitcoin's early days. It was a simple exchange between two brilliant minds that laid the groundwork for a new financial paradigm.
