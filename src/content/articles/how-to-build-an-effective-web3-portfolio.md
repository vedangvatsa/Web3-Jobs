In the Web3 job market, your resume is secondary. Your LinkedIn profile is an afterthought. The single most important asset in your job search is your **portfolio**—a collection of your public, verifiable "proof of work." Hiring managers in this space are looking for tangible evidence of your skills, passion, and understanding of the crypto ecosystem.

A well-crafted portfolio is the ultimate signal that you have what it takes to succeed. This guide provides a practical roadmap for building a Web3 portfolio that will get you noticed and land you interviews, with tailored advice for both technical and non-technical roles. You can find more specific project ideas in our [developer portfolio guide](/building-a-web3-developer-portfolio).

### Foundational Principles for Your Portfolio

- **Quality over Quantity:** Three high-quality, well-documented projects or contributions are infinitely better than ten half-finished ones.
- **Show, Don't Tell:** Every item in your portfolio should be a link to a public, verifiable piece of work.
- **Tell a Story:** Your portfolio should tell the story of your journey into Web3. What are you passionate about? What have you learned? What value have you created?

### The Portfolio Project Roadmap for Developers

Your goal is to demonstrate technical competence, a security-first mindset, and an understanding of core Web3 concepts. Your GitHub is your temple.

#### Project 1: Foundational Smart Contracts
- **What to build:** An ERC-20 token and an ERC-721 [NFT collection](/what-are-nfts).
- **Why it matters:** This is the "Hello, World!" of Web3 development. It demonstrates that you understand token standards, can work with established libraries like OpenZeppelin, and can write basic [Solidity](/solidity-for-beginners).
- **Go the extra mile:** Deploy them to a public testnet and build a simple React frontend to interact with them.

#### Project 2: A Full-Stack dApp
- **What to build:** A simple DeFi or DAO primitive. A staking dApp is a classic choice.
- **Why it matters:** This shows you can connect a frontend to a smart contract, manage application state, and think about user flows. It's your first true full-stack project.
- **Go the extra mile:** Write a comprehensive test suite using Foundry or Hardhat. A developer who writes tests is seen as a true professional.

#### Project 3: The Open-Source Contribution
- **What to do:** Make a meaningful contribution to an existing, reputable open-source Web3 project.
- **Why it matters:** This is one of the most powerful signals you can send. It proves you can read and understand a large, professional codebase, collaborate with a team, and navigate a formal contribution process.
- **Go the extra mile:** Start small. Your first contribution could be fixing a typo in the documentation or adding a missing test case.

### The Portfolio "Project" Roadmap for Non-Developers

For roles in marketing, community, product, or ops, your "proof of work" is a portfolio of content and contributions that showcase your expertise.

#### Project 1: The Deep-Dive Analysis
- **What to do:** Pick a protocol or sector you're interested in and write a detailed analysis.
- **Example for a Marketer:** A critique of a project's go-to-market strategy.
- **Example for a PM:** A UX audit of a popular dApp, with mockups for proposed improvements.
- **Why it matters:** This demonstrates your strategic thinking and communication skills. Publish it on a blog like Mirror or as a Twitter thread.

#### Project 2: The Data-Driven Insight
- **What to do:** Learn basic SQL and create a dashboard on Dune Analytics.
- **Example:** Build a dashboard tracking the user growth, transaction volume, and retention for a protocol you admire.
- **Why it matters:** The ability to work with on-chain data is a massive advantage for any non-technical role. It shows you are data-driven and can generate your own insights.

#### Project 3: The DAO Contribution
- **What to do:** Become an active, value-add member of a [DAO](/how-to-contribute-to-daos-and-get-paid).
- **Example:** Be the person who reliably summarizes the weekly community calls. Or, write a well-researched governance proposal to improve a specific part of the DAO's operations.
- **Why it matters:** This is your verifiable, on-chain work experience. It proves you can collaborate in a decentralized environment and take initiative.

Your portfolio is a living document that tells your professional story. By consistently building, writing, and contributing in public, you create the undeniable evidence that will unlock your first (or next) job in the exciting and meritocratic Web3 industry.
