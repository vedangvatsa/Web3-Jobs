In Web3, your resume is more than just a list of past jobs. It's a curated summary of your skills, passion, and, most importantly, your "proof of work." A great Web3 resume connects your traditional experience with your on-chain activities to tell a compelling story. Here's how to build one.

### The Key Difference: On-Chain and Off-Chain

A traditional resume focuses solely on your off-chain, corporate experience. A Web3 resume combines both.

*   **Off-Chain:** Your traditional education, work history, and skills.
*   **On-Chain:** Your public contributions to the Web3 ecosystem. This is your proof of work.

### Key Sections of a Web3 Resume

**1. Header: Your Web3 Identity**

In addition to your name and email, include links to your Web3 presence.

*   **Essential:** GitHub (for developers), Twitter/Farcaster.
*   **Good to have:** Personal website/portfolio, blog, ENS address (e.g., `yourname.eth`).

**2. Summary: Your Web3 Narrative**

This is your elevator pitch. In 2-3 sentences, explain your passion for Web3 and what you bring to the table.

*   **Example:** "A full-stack developer with 5 years of experience in enterprise SaaS, now passionate about building more open and equitable financial systems on Ethereum. Seeking to apply my expertise in building scalable backends to the challenges of decentralized finance."

**3. Projects & Contributions: Your Proof of Work**

This is the most important section. Place it *before* your traditional work experience. This is where you showcase your initiative.

*   **DAO Contributions:** List the DAOs you're active in. Describe your role and accomplishments (e.g., "Authored three successful governance proposals," "Managed a $10,000 grant for a community event").
*   **Hackathon Projects:** List any hackathons you've participated in and what you built.
*   **Personal Projects:** Describe any dApps, tools, or content you've created. Link to the live project and the code on GitHub.
*   **Open Source Contributions:** Link to any pull requests you've had merged into open-source Web3 projects.

**4. Professional Experience**

List your traditional work history, but frame it through a Web3 lens.

*   **Focus on transferable skills:** Highlight experience in areas like remote work, agile development, financial modeling, or community management.
*   **Quantify your impact:** Use numbers to show the impact you had (e.g., "Managed a product that grew to 1 million MAUs," "Reduced infrastructure costs by 20%").

**5. Skills**

Create two subsections:

*   **Web3 Skills:** Solidity, Foundry, Ethers.js, The Graph, Tokenomics, DAO Governance, etc.
*   **General Skills:** Python, React, AWS, Project Management, etc.

By structuring your resume this way, you're not just telling a potential employer what you've done in the past; you're showing them what you are actively doing *right now* in the Web3 space. This proactive demonstration of skill and passion is what will make you stand out from the crowd.
